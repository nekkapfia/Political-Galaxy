# Core 1A: France – Parti Socialiste
## Personal Autonomy Analysis
**Endos Score: 47**  
**Xenos Score: N/A**

### Overview

The Parti Socialiste produces a mid-range Core 1A profile built from retention of the 1881 Press Law hate-speech framework, Gayssot and related memory offences, platform regulation under DSA-era rules, and equality frameworks, combined with a stronger residual attachment to classical laïcité and republican speech culture than LFI or EELV. Governing and programme pragmatism keeps identity-orthodoxy intensity thinner. No Endos/Xenos split is applied.

### Core 1A Relevant Policies / Elements

* Retention of French hate-speech legislation (1881 Press Law Art. 24 and related provisions on incitement to hatred or discrimination) as part of the republican legal order; no comprehensive narrowing agenda.
* Retention of Gayssot Law and related memory offences criminalising Holocaust denial / contestation of designated crimes against humanity.
* Support for platform content duties under DSA implementation and residual national obligations against illegal and harmful material.
* Equality and anti-discrimination frameworks retained as baseline social-democratic public policy; secondary institutional pressure on expression remains available.
* Stronger residual attachment to classical laïcité and republican speech culture than LFI or EELV; less programme elevation of anti-Islamophobia as a principle that treats critical discussion of Islam as presumptively illegitimate.
* Supports hate-speech law and platform regulation without the same intensity of identity-orthodoxy drive that characterises LFI.
* Gender and equality agendas present but less central as speech-restructuring projects than under LFI/EELV.
* Retention of ordinary direct-harm criminal prohibitions.

### Expanded Analysis

PS programmes conserve the dense French baseline. The 1881 hate-speech framework, Gayssot, and DSA-era platform duties remain settled. Equality frameworks continue to generate secondary institutional pressure. What distinguishes PS from LFI is the republican residual: classical laïcité and a weaker elevation of anti-Islamophobia as a speech-regulating principle that frames critical discussion of Islam as illegitimate.

Critical speech controls remain real. Institutional equality rules continue to operate. What is absent relative to LFI/EELV is the fuller identity-protective densification, especially on the Islam axis. Those intensifying layers do not characterise PS programme posture in the same way; their relative absence lightens the final set.

No stable, discretely targetable out-group differential is formalised by PS policy at the level required for an Endos/Xenos split. Differential patterns in the wider system are not uniquely amplified by the party into a formal dual structure.

The architecture sits above LFI/EELV densification and below any liberalising position. Cumulative weight is mid-range — baseline retention with thinner orthodoxy. Closest analogues are German SPD and UK Labour (Starmer-weighted).

### Why this produces Endos 47 / Xenos N/A

1881 hate-speech framework retained, Gayssot retained, platform regulation retained, equality frameworks retained, but without LFI-level anti-Islamophobia speech intensity or dense identity-orthodoxy drive, place Endos at 47. No split warranted.
