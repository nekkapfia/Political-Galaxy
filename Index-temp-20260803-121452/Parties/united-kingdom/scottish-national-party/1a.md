# Core 1A: United Kingdom – Scottish National Party
## Personal Autonomy Analysis
**Endos Score: 26**  
**Xenos Score: 22**

### Overview

The SNP’s governing programme produces a restrictive Core 1A profile built from the Hate Crime and Public Order (Scotland) Act 2021, the retained UK Online Safety Act, institutional measures treating critical speech as potentially stirring up hatred, gender self-identification rules that generate secondary speech and association constraints, and robust enforcement of identity-hostile expression offences. The denser Scottish hate-crime framework is the decisive instrument. Cumulative weight sits inside the ideological-enforcement range. Heightened scrutiny of speech critical of protected identity categories justifies the Endos/Xenos split.

### Core 1A Relevant Policies / Elements

* Hate Crime and Public Order (Scotland) Act 2021: stirring up hatred on the basis of race, religion, sexual orientation, transgender identity, age, disability and other characteristics. Expands protected characteristics beyond the English framework and, in important formulations, includes a “likely to” limb that lowers the threshold relative to intent-based English offences; in some applications reaches private settings.
* UK Online Safety Act architecture retained in full as it applies in Scotland; no opt-out or narrowing agenda.
* Institutional and educational measures — public-sector equality duties, school and university guidance, workplace policies — treating critical speech (especially on sex, gender and related topics) as potentially stirring up hatred or creating a hostile environment.
* Gender self-identification support and associated institutional rules: once affirmed gender is treated as operative, speech asserting biological sex (misgendering) and association decisions around single-sex spaces become subject to hostile-environment or potential hate-incident framing.
* Robust enforcement of public-order and communications offences against identity-hostile expression.
* Full retention of the Scottish hate-crime framework and Online Safety Act; no substantial narrowing proposed.
* Section 5-style and communications offences retained as they operate in Scotland.

### Expanded Analysis

The Hate Crime and Public Order (Scotland) Act 2021 is the central densifying instrument. It expands protected characteristics (explicitly including transgender identity and age) and lowers the threshold for criminal liability relative to English stirring-up offences. Enforcement practice under Police Scotland guidance has produced documented chilling effects on ordinary conversation, particularly around sex and gender. Together with the fully retained Online Safety Act this produces a dense content-based speech control architecture.

Gender self-identification policy adds a further layer. Institutional rules treating affirmed gender as authoritative convert misgendering and single-sex space decisions into hostile-environment or potential hate-incident matters — direct secondary constraints on expression and association.

Institutional and educational measures extend the same logic into schools, universities and public-sector workplaces through equality duties and guidance that frame critical speech on protected characteristics as risk factors.

Speech critical of certain identity categories (race, religion, sexual orientation, transgender identity) attracts heightened scrutiny. This is a stable, discretely targetable intensity differential.

Mandatory attitude-correction training is not evidenced as a central party-defining feature in the Green sense, which prevents a still lower placement while leaving the architecture inside the ideological-enforcement band.

### Why this produces Endos 26 / Xenos 22

Expanded Scottish hate-crime offences with lowered threshold, Online Safety Act retention, institutional orthodoxy measures, self-identification speech/association constraints, and residual public-order offences place Endos at 26. Heightened identity-targeted intensity produces Xenos 22.
