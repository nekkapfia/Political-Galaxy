# Core 1A: United Kingdom – 2016–2019
## Pre-COVID Enforcement Plateau
**Base Score: 60**  
**Xenos Score: N/A** (no split)

### Overview

The years immediately preceding the COVID pandemic were marked less by major new primary legislation than by sustained high enforcement intensity of the existing speech and public-order toolkit, together with the continued growth of non-crime hate-incident recording practices. The statutory architecture inherited from 1986 (Public Order Act), 2003 (Communications Act) and the intervening terrorism-expression measures remained fully in force. What changed was the practical density of application. Police and prosecutors made regular use of Section 5, communications offences and hate-speech provisions against a wide range of public and online expression. The cumulative effect was a modest but measurable further downward adjustment from the 2001–2015 baseline of 62.

### Core 1A Relevant Restrictions / Elements

* Sustained and in some domains intensified application of Public Order Act 1986 Section 5 (“threatening, abusive or insulting words or behaviour \ldots likely to cause harassment, alarm or distress”).
* Continued enforcement of Part III racial-hatred / stirring-up offences and related identity-based speech provisions.
* Full operation of Communications Act 2003 Section 127 and Malicious Communications Act offences against online and electronic speech.
* Retention of terrorism-related expression and glorification controls.
* Growth of non-crime hate-incident recording by police forces. While administrative rather than criminal, these practices formed part of the broader restrictive climate surrounding expression; the underlying speech offences remain the scored restrictions.
* Absence of the later Online Safety Act 2023 platform duties.
* No major liberalising repeals of any existing speech offence.

### Expanded Analysis

The restrictive profile of 2016–2019 is defined by a plateau of elevated enforcement rather than by a new legislative rupture of Online Safety Act scale. The legal instruments themselves were already in place; what intensified was their routine application to political speech, social-media posts, public demonstrations and everyday interactions. Non-crime hate-incident recording expanded the administrative shadow cast over expression even where no criminal threshold was met. Under Direct Restriction Only, the scored weight remains the criminal prohibitions (Section 5, communications offences, stirring-up provisions). Negative-Only Weighting records the continued presence and active use of these layers; the absence of still-further statutory expansion does not generate upward credit. The net result is a small further reduction from the preceding era’s score of 62.

No system-wide Endos/Xenos split is applied. Differential intensity of enforcement against particular political or identity-critical speech occurred, yet it did not crystallise into a formal, stable dual legal structure of the kind required by the optional-split rule.

**Key Shift:** Intensification of enforcement and administrative practice around the existing speech-restriction toolkit, without major new primary legislation of Online Safety Act scale, producing a modest further downward movement.

### Why this produces a Base Score of 60

Modest further downward adjustment from the 2001–2015 level of 62, driven by sustained high enforcement intensity of the retained Public Order Act, Communications Act and related speech offences. Negative-Only Weighting continues to apply; no upward credit is available for the absence of still-further expansions. The score remains above the later COVID collapse and only marginally above the post-Online Safety Act recovery level, reflecting a dense but still pre-platform-duty environment for voluntary expression.
