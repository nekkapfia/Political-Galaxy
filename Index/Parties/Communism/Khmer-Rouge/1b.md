## Regulation

### Overview
The Khmer Rouge system (Democratic Kampuchea 1975–1979) abolished money, markets and private property. The entire population was organised into forced-labour work units and cooperatives under military-style discipline. The economy was organised solely around state production and export quotas, primarily rice.

### Relevant Policies
- Complete confiscation of private property and elimination of currency and free markets
- Forced evacuation of cities and organisation of the population into collective production units
- Extreme forced labour (10–12+ hour days) under production quotas
- Deliberately subsistence or below-subsistence food rations
- State monopoly on rice production, distribution and export
- Total administrative direction of labour, mobility and consumption

### Expanded Analysis
Under the Khmer Rouge the distinction between state and economy collapsed completely. There was no residual private economic decision-making of any meaningful kind. Labour, production, distribution and consumption were directed by the party-state through forced work units. Extraction intensity produced mass starvation and excess mortality as a direct consequence of the system’s mechanical design. 

### Final Score
The decisive mechanical effects are total abolition of private property and markets, universal forced labour, and complete administrative control of production and consumption. This produces a Regulation Score of 18.

## Taxation

These proposals are scored via the separate Taxation Score method (not included in the Regulation Score above).

The Khmer Rouge system is an extreme command economy of the most radical type. Money itself was abolished; extraction occurred through direct appropriation of labour and output. The standard tax-to-GDP formula is meaningless.

**Alternative Method applied**  
Effective tithe rate: **90 %**  
Taxation Score = 100 − 90 = **10**

Justification rests on the abolition of money and private claims, universal forced labour, subsistence rationing, documented rice exports amid famine, and excess mortality as an indicator of extraction beyond sustainable levels.

**Taxation Score: 10**
