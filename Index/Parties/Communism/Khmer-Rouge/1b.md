## Regulation

### Overview
The Khmer Rouge organised Cambodia around abolition of money and markets for a period, forced collective labour, evacuation of cities, total state claim on output, and extinction of residual private economic activity in the formal sector.

### Relevant Policies / Elements
* Complete confiscation of private property
* Abolition of currency and free markets
* Forced evacuation of cities and organisation of the population into collective production units
* Extreme forced labour under production quotas under military discipline
* Deliberately subsistence or below-subsistence food rations
* State monopoly on rice production, distribution and export
* Total administrative direction of labour, mobility and consumption
* No residual private economic decision-making of any meaningful kind
* Extraction intensity producing mass starvation as a direct mechanical outcome of the system’s design

### Expanded Analysis
The Khmer Rouge organised Cambodia around abolition of money and markets for a period, forced collective labour, evacuation of cities, total state claim on output, and extinction of residual private economic activity in the formal sector. The institutional pattern is extensive state control of ownership, investment, labour allocation and surplus claim. Residual private activity, where it exists at all, is confined to the margins stated in the policy set. Ownership, forced labour and confiscatory mechanisms are Regulation; they are not mixed into the Taxation algebra.
