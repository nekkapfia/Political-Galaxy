## Regulation

### Overview
The Khmer Rouge system (Democratic Kampuchea 1975–1979) abolished money, markets and private property. The population was organised into forced-labour work units under military-style discipline. Production, distribution and consumption were directed solely by the party-state. Residual private economic decision-making of any meaningful kind was extinguished by design.

### Relevant Policies
- Complete confiscation of private property
- Abolition of currency and free markets
- Forced evacuation of cities and organisation of the population into collective production units
- Extreme forced labour under production quotas under military discipline
- Deliberately subsistence or below-subsistence food rations
- State monopoly on rice production, distribution and export
- Total administrative direction of labour, mobility and consumption
- No residual private economic decision-making of any meaningful kind
- Extraction intensity producing mass starvation as a direct mechanical outcome of the system’s design

### Expanded Analysis
The distinction between state and economy collapsed completely. There was no residual private economic decision-making of any meaningful kind. Labour, production, distribution and consumption were directed through forced work units. Relative to the floor bands of the slider: the system matches near-absolute to absolute state economic control — no private economic action of meaningful kind, resources and labour fully directed, distinction between state and economy collapsed. Money and markets were abolished, not merely restricted. Placement at the lower edge of extensive control is insufficient; the mechanical package reaches into the 6–16 / near-floor range.
