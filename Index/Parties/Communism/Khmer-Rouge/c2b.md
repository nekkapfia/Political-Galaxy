# Cultural 2B: Communism – Khmer Rouge
## National Pride Analysis
**Score: 40**

### Overview
The Khmer Rouge combined radical anti-urban, anti-traditional social engineering with a selective and extreme form of Khmer nationalist rhetoric. National pride was present but subordinated to a destructive revolutionary project that attacked much of existing Cambodian cultural continuity. Residual baseline of predominantly critical national framing: traditional pride is limited or heavily qualified; critical and atonement narratives carry substantial institutional weight.

### Relevant Policies / Elements
* Selective nationalist rhetoric against Vietnamese and other external enemies.
* Simultaneous destruction of traditional religious, urban and cultural institutions.
* Year Zero framing that rejected large parts of the existing national past.
* Symbols and rituals served the revolutionary organisation more than longer national continuity.
* Residual baseline of predominantly critical national framing: traditional pride is limited or heavily qualified; critical and atonement narratives carry substantial institutional weight.

### Expanded Analysis
The Khmer Rouge produces a Negative-band score. While anti-Vietnamese nationalism was intense, the regime’s Year Zero logic attacked the continuity of Cambodian society itself. National pride was therefore partial, instrumental and compatible with the erasure of large parts of the nation’s own cultural inheritance.

### Why this produces a Final Score of 40
Selective nationalist rhetoric coexisting with radical attack on traditional cultural continuity places the Khmer Rouge in the Negative band.
