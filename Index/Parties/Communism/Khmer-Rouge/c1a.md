# Cultural 1A: Communism – Khmer Rouge
## Foundation Analysis
**Score: 92**

### Overview
The Khmer Rouge (Democratic Kampuchea) grounded political legitimacy in Year Zero revolutionary ideology, the absolute authority of Angkar, and the total transformation of society according to a closed historical-moral vision. Ordinary demos preference, evidence-based policy and personal interest were extinguished as legitimate sources. The profile is extreme transcendent lean.

### Moral Justification – Relevant Elements
* Year Zero and continuous revolution as binding higher-order obligations requiring total social destruction and reconstruction.
* Angkar as the infallible organisational embodiment of correct historical and moral direction.
* Class and urban-rural categories treated as moral-political essences subject to elimination or forced transformation.
* Ordinary popular preference, market exchange and traditional authority rejected as illegitimate residues of the old society.
* Terror and forced labour justified as necessary instruments of historical purification.
* No residual liberal or demos-based constraint on the revolutionary project.

### Expanded Analysis
Khmer Rouge justification was totalising ideological transcendence. The living population had no standing to set aside Angkar’s line through preference or evidence; deviation was moral-political crime. Historical necessity and organisational authority extinguished mundane sources. The pattern is extreme transcendent lean, among the highest in the set.

### Why this produces a Final Score of 92
Year Zero ideology and Angkar authority as binding higher-order obligations that extinguish ordinary demos and evidence-based justification produce an extreme transcendent lean. The score is 92.
