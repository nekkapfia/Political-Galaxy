# Cultural 3: Communism – Khmer Rouge
## Ethnic and Cultural Exclusivity Analysis
**Score: 3A 0 | 3B 88**

### Overview
The Khmer Rouge defined membership through extreme political-cultural reset (Year Zero). Prior culture, religion, family forms, and urban identities were to be abolished. Targeting of certain ethnic and religious groups occurred, but the operating principle was political-class and cultural transformation rather than a formal ancestral blood code. The Exclusion Test is not met for 3A; 3B approaches maximum. Immigration and deportation remain out of scope.

### Relevant Policies / Elements
* Year Zero policy: abolition of prior culture, religion, markets, and family structures as the condition of the new society.
* Extreme demands for political-cultural transformation and loyalty to Angkar.
* Targeting of urban, educated, and some ethnic/religious categories occurred within a political-class framework, not a Nuremberg-style blood statute.
* No formal permanent ethnic bar to membership on pure ancestral grounds.
* Immigration and deportation out of scope.

### Expanded Analysis
The Exclusion Test is not met. Despite lethal targeting of groups, the system did not codify permanent ethnic exclusion by blood as the membership rule. 3A is 0. 3B is near maximum because full membership in the new society required total cultural and social transformation. This aligns with 80–89 to low 90s High / Maximum Cultural Demand.
