# Cultural 5A: Communism – Khmer Rouge
## Tradition vs Progress Analysis
**Score: 4**

### Overview
The Khmer Rouge extinguished inherited family, religious and national-continuity transmission through forced collective living, abolition of residual private family forms, and total redesign of education and social organisation.

### Cultural 5A Relevant Policies / Elements
* Forced evacuation of cities and abolition of residual private household and family living arrangements as the basic unit of social organisation.
* Destruction or suppression of Buddhist institutional authority and religious practice as competing sources of norm transmission.
* Abolition or radical redesign of prior education systems; literacy and socialisation subordinated to regime mobilisation and collective labour.
* Elimination of money, markets and prior professional and cultural elites as carriers of pre-revolutionary social continuity.
* Year Zero framing treating pre-revolutionary Cambodian society as a baseline to be extinguished rather than conserved.
* Residual private family, religious and customary transmission prohibited as preferred public or institutional order under the regime.
* Domain hierarchy: family organisation and education/socialisation redesigned at the root; material heritage irrelevant to placement.

### Expanded Analysis
The Khmer Rouge pattern on Cultural 5A is near-total institutional extinction of inherited traditional transmission. Primary domains—family organisation and education/socialisation—were forcibly redesigned: private household residual was attacked through collective living; Buddhist institutional authority was suppressed; prior schooling was abolished or subordinated to regime mobilisation. Year Zero treated pre-revolutionary society as material to be erased, not as a baseline to protect. The residual pre-revolutionary order was not conserved in any durable institutional form; elevating it as preferable was incompatible with the regime. Domain hierarchy places family and education transmission first: both were reconstructed against inherited continuity. This is the floor pattern for the axis—tradition incompatible with the system's design across primary domains.

### Why this produces a Final Score of 4
Forced collective living, abolition of residual private family forms, suppression of Buddhist institutional authority, and Year Zero extinction of pre-revolutionary social continuity place the Khmer Rouge at the absolute floor (0–10). No residual traditional transmission remained as a protected institutional baseline in primary domains. Higher placement would require evidence of durable conservation of inherited family, religious or educational continuity that the locked analysis does not show.
