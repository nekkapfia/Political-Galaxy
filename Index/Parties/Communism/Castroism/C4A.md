# Cultural 4A: Communism – Castroism
## Social Determinism Analysis
**Score: 52**

### Overview
Castroist Cuba conditioned opportunity more through political reliability and revolutionary credentials than through pure hereditary caste. Formal education and occupational pathways remained relatively open, but advancement into higher and more sensitive positions was filtered by loyalty and background assessments. The system produced noticeable residual constraint without reaching the heavier origin-locked regimes of Stalinism, Maoism, or Juche.

### Relevant Policies / Elements
* Political reliability and revolutionary credentials as gates on higher education, professional posts, and Party advancement.
* Formal educational expansion that increased absolute access while retaining loyalty filters for elite pathways.
* Residual background and family-political assessments that created headwinds for those with “wrong” associations.
* Significant formal mobility for individuals who demonstrated competence and political conformity.
* Absence of a comprehensive multi-generational hereditary classification system of the songbun type.

### Expanded Analysis
Cultural 4A measures birth and inherited position as constraints on life outcomes. Castroism retained substantial formal mobility through expanded schooling and occupational structures. The decisive filters were political reliability and revolutionary credentials rather than locked hereditary occupational assignment. Family background and political associations still created differential ease of advancement, placing the system above pure liberal-democratic residual gradients but below the heavier origin-gated Communist regimes. The pattern fits the 51–59 band: both inter-class and intra-class mobility were realistic and used, yet birth-linked and political-background advantages remained visible and consequential across multiple domains.

Cultural 4B equity diagnostics are out of scope.

### Why this produces a Final Score of 52
Political-reliability and background gates on higher pathways, combined with relatively open formal education and residual mobility, align with the 51–59 band. Score 52.
