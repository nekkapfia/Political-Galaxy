# Core 1A: Communism – Castroism
## Personal Autonomy Analysis
**Endos Score: 16**  
**Xenos Score: 11**

### Overview

Castroism as a governing political force produces a severely restrictive Core 1A profile defined by one-party monopoly of speech and association, Committees for the Defense of the Revolution (CDR) neighbourhood surveillance, exit controls, mandatory political education, suppression of independent media and organisation, and designated counter-revolutionary categories subject to imprisonment, labour camps and professional exclusion. Cumulative weight sits in the extreme-restriction / upper ideological-enforcement border. A clear Endos/Xenos split applies.

### Core 1A Relevant Policies / Elements

* One-party monopoly under the Communist Party of Cuba; no legal competitive politics or independent political organisation of any kind.
* Comprehensive state control of press, broadcasting and publishing; independent media prohibited as a matter of system design.
* CDR neighbourhood watch and informant structures making local expression, association and household political reliability continuously observable and reportable.
* Exit permits and travel controls; for long periods unauthorised departure was criminalised and legal exit was a political privilege.
* Mandatory political education and mass-organisation participation in workplaces, schools and communities — institutional routines enforcing ideological alignment.
* UMAP (early) and later prison and labour-camp systems for designated undesirables, including political opponents, religious nonconformists and others labelled antisocial or counter-revolutionary.
* Professional, educational and rationing consequences for political nonconformity, extending restriction beyond formal criminal penalties.
* Designated counter-revolutionaries and dissidents subject to imprisonment, systematic harassment and loss of livelihood.
* Ordinary violent-crime prohibitions exist but do not define placement.

### Expanded Analysis

Castroism established a durable single-party speech and association monopoly with dense local surveillance through the CDRs. Independent press and political organisation were eliminated. Exit controls turned international movement into a politically allocated privilege. Mandatory political education and mass organisations extended orthodoxy into daily institutional life.

For Endos — citizens not actively labelled counter-revolutionary — public political speech outside the Party line was closed; CDR and workplace consequences constrained private dissent; travel abroad was restricted. Placement at 16 reflects severe, systematic multi-domain restriction short of Stalinist mass-terror intensity or Juche hereditary totalism.

For Xenos — active dissidents, designated counter-revolutionaries and groups targeted in particular campaigns — imprisonment, camps, professional destruction and harassment were routine instruments. That differential pulls Xenos lower while remaining above the absolute floor.

Framing as anti-imperialism or defence of the revolution is discarded. Only the observable restriction architecture is scored.

### Why this produces Endos 16 / Xenos 11

One-party speech and association monopoly, CDR surveillance, exit controls, mandatory political education and suppression of independent media place Endos at 16. Designated counter-revolutionaries under prison, camp and exclusion policies produce Xenos 11.
