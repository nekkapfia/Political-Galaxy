# Core 2B: Communism – Castroism
## International Sovereignty Analysis
**Score: 36**

### Overview

Castroism’s foreign-policy practice includes sustained, ideologically driven interference in other states’ internal affairs: large-scale military intervention, training and support for revolutionary movements, and soft-power programmes with political alignment functions. The pattern is clearly interventionist while falling short of permanent multi-country occupation as the normal mode.

### Relevant Policies / Elements

* Operation Carlota (Angola), November 1975–May 1991: combat intervention in support of the MPLA government against South African and rival forces; peak troop levels commonly cited ~36,000 by 1976 and higher (order of 50,000–60,000) in the late 1980s; cumulative military personnel often estimated in the 300,000–380,000 range over the mission; civilian collaborators in the tens of thousands; Cuban deaths commonly cited in the ~2,000–2,700 range; Battle of Cuito Cuanavale (1987–88) as a major late engagement.
* Invitation by the MPLA provides partial mitigation relative to uninvited regime-change invasion; the action remains interference in Angolan (and regional) internal conflict.
* Earlier and parallel advisory, training and support roles in other African and Middle Eastern conflicts aligned with Soviet-bloc strategy.
* Training, arming and political support for Latin American guerrilla and revolutionary movements (continental foco-era networks and later solidarity campaigns).
* Intelligence and organisational assistance aimed at weakening or replacing governments hostile to the Cuban revolution.
* Soft power: education of foreign cadres, medical and civilian internationalist missions that also serve political alignment goals.
* Doctrine of proletarian internationalism and anti-imperialist solidarity treated as legitimate grounds for cross-border revolutionary assistance.
* Cuban territorial defence and actions entirely inside Cuban sovereign territory are out of scope for Core 2B.

### Expanded Analysis

Core 2B scores interference in other nations’ internal arrangements. Angola alone is a multi-year, large-scale military intervention inside another state’s conflict. Latin American revolutionary support programmes, even when limited or deniable, are classic soft-to-grey-zone interference. Soft-power medical and educational missions gain weight when systematically paired with political alignment goals.

Self-defence and invitation mitigations apply where relevant (MPLA request; Cuban territorial defence excluded). They do not convert the overall programme into non-intervention. The cumulative pattern of expeditionary war, revolutionary export and ideologically conditioned soft power places the system well below restrained non-intervention bands and above permanent multi-state occupation floors.

### Why this produces a Final Score of 36

Sustained Angola combat intervention at scale, systematic support for foreign revolutionary movements, and ideologically driven soft-power projection place Castroism in the mid-30s on international sovereignty (higher interference = lower score).
