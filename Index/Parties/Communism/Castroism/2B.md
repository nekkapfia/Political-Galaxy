# Core 2B: Communism – Castroism
## International Sovereignty Analysis
**Score: 34**

### Overview
Castroism’s foreign-policy practice includes sustained, ideologically driven interference in other states’ internal affairs: large-scale military intervention in Angola, training and support for revolutionary movements, and soft-power programmes with political alignment functions. The pattern is clearly interventionist while falling short of permanent multi-country occupation as the normal mode. Residual baseline of revolutionary internationalism remains active rather than restrained.

### Relevant Policies / Elements
* Operation Carlota (Angola), 1975–1991: combat intervention in support of the MPLA; peak troop levels commonly cited in the tens of thousands; cumulative deployments far larger; major late engagement at Cuito Cuanavale.
* Invitation by the MPLA provides partial mitigation relative to uninvited regime-change invasion; the action remains interference in Angolan and regional internal conflict.
* Advisory, training and support roles in other African and Middle Eastern conflicts aligned with Soviet-bloc strategy.
* Training, arming and political support for Latin American guerrilla and revolutionary movements.
* Soft power: education of foreign cadres and medical internationalist missions that also serve political alignment goals.
* Doctrine of proletarian internationalism treated as legitimate grounds for cross-border revolutionary assistance.
* Residual baseline of active revolutionary internationalism: external combat and political support remain standing instruments rather than exceptions.
* Cuban territorial defence is out of scope for Core 2B.

### Expanded Analysis
Core 2B scores interference in other nations’ internal arrangements. Angola alone is a multi-year, large-scale military intervention inside another state’s conflict. Latin American revolutionary support programmes are classic soft-to-grey-zone interference. Soft-power missions gain weight when systematically paired with political alignment. Invitation mitigation raises the score relative to pure uninvited occupation, but the overall pattern remains interventionist.

### Why this produces a Final Score of 34
Sustained overseas combat intervention plus revolutionary support networks, only partially mitigated by invitation, place Castroism in the low-to-mid 30s.
