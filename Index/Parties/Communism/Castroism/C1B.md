# Cultural 1B: Communism – Castroism
## Dogmatism Analysis
**Score: 92**

**Orthodoxy: 93, Tribalism: 91**

### Overview
Castroism combines extreme internal closure with comprehensive external rejection of competing moral frameworks. Inside the Party and regime, deviation from the revolutionary line is institutionally punished; outside, liberalism, capitalism and counter-revolutionary currents are treated as illegitimate and hostile rather than as debatable alternatives. Both Orthodoxy and Tribalism sit at the extreme end of the scale, producing a Strong band score anchored by concrete Party-state instruments.

### Relevant Policies / Elements
* Monopoly of the Communist Party of Cuba; no legal factional competition or organised internal opposition currents.
* Democratic centralism and Party discipline making public dissent from the line career- and liberty-threatening.
* CDR network (founded 1960; multi-million membership in later decades) extending surveillance and reporting into neighbourhood and workplace life.
* Criminal and administrative categories of counter-revolutionary activity and “ideological diversionism” covering non-violent political expression and organisation.
* State monopoly of media and education ensuring competing frameworks lack institutional platforms.
* Residual baseline of revolutionary orthodoxy remains binding even during periods of limited managerial flexibility (Special Period, post-2010 economic debates).

### Expanded Analysis
Orthodoxy is institutionalised through Party monopoly, security organs and mass organisations; revision of foundational political principles is not a recognised internal process comparable to open factional contestation. The CDR system and ideological-diversionism categories extend enforcement beyond formal Party structures into everyday social life. Tribalism is equally strong: alternative moral-political systems are framed as existential threats or moral failures rather than legitimate alternatives open to empirical contest. State media and education monopolies ensure that liberal, capitalist and counter-revolutionary frameworks lack any institutional platform from which to challenge the line. Intensity is not uniform across every decade—Special Period and post-2010 economic debates opened limited managerial space—but the bounding rule remains Party-line closure and external delegitimisation of competing frameworks. The pattern matches the Strong (86–95) calibration cluster occupied by other Party-state systems short of pure eliminationist intensity.

### Why this produces a Final Score of 92
Party monopoly, democratic-centralist discipline, CDR surveillance, criminalisation of ideological diversionism and media/education monopoly together produce near-total internal closure and comprehensive external rejection. Both aspects sit at the extreme end; the formula therefore yields a Strong-band score of 92, consistent with the calibrated Castroism position below the absolute ceiling of Juche or Khmer Rouge.
