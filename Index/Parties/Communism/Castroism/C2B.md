# Cultural 2B: Communism – Castroism
## National Pride Analysis
**Score: 58**

### Overview
Castroism combined strong affirmation of Cuban national independence and anti-imperial achievement with Marxist-Leninist ideology. National pride in the revolution and sovereignty was genuine and institutionally promoted. Residual baseline of national affirmation remains positive even where ideology reframes the content of that pride.

### Relevant Policies / Elements
* Education and media celebrate Cuban independence, the revolution and resistance to external domination.
* Symbols and rituals affirm the Cuban nation and its revolutionary path.
* Critical frames applied to the pre-revolutionary order and to external enemies, not to the Cuban nation as such.
* National pride functions as a real emotional and political resource.
* Residual baseline of institutional national affirmation; the regime does not organise national shame or pathologise Cuban identity itself.

### Expanded Analysis
Castroism produces a Positive-band score. Cuban national pride is institutionally real and oriented toward independence and revolutionary achievement. It is not pure traditional historical pride, nor is it organising national shame. Critical energy is directed outward and toward the pre-revolutionary order rather than against the nation as a continuous subject. The residual pattern is affirmative rather than guilt- or shame-oriented.

### Why this produces a Final Score of 58
Genuine institutional affirmation of Cuban national independence and revolutionary continuity, with residual positive national framing, places Castroism in the Positive band.
