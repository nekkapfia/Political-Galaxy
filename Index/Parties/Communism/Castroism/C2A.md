# Cultural 2A: Communism – Castroism
## Individuality Analysis
**Score: 26**

### Overview
Castroism subordinates individual merit, contract and status to collective revolutionary and class categories. Rights, opportunities and political standing are routinely allocated according to group membership.

### Allocation Mechanisms
* Class origin and revolutionary reliability as recurring criteria in education access, professional advancement and political trust.
* Mass organisations mediating access to goods, housing queues and social recognition.
* Political loyalty and participation treated as group-performance criteria for full standing.
* Suppression of independent civil association; individuals cannot form status-bearing groups outside Party-approved structures.
* Residual private economic activity (when permitted) framed as concession, not recognition of individual economic sovereignty.

### Expanded Analysis
The dominant allocation principle is collective-political rather than individual-meritocratic. Even where formal education expansion reduced some birth-class barriers, the political category of unreliable or counter-revolutionary systematically structured status and opportunity. Self-ID style identity allocation is not the mechanism; class-and-loyalty allocation is. The result is very strong group primacy without requiring liberal identity-politics forms.

### Why this produces a Final Score of 26
Routine subordination of individual standing to revolutionary-class and loyalty categories places Castroism in the very strong group-subordination band. Score 26.
