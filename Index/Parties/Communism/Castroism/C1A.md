# Cultural 1A: Castroism
## Foundation Analysis
Final Score: 0 (50M)

### Overview
Castroism organises moral legitimacy around revolutionary socialism and anti-imperialism as historical project, social provision as achievement, performance and survival of the state, and the concrete interests of the Cuban people.

### Moral Justification – Relevant Elements

#### Mundane Justifications
* Revolutionary socialism and anti-imperialism are presented as the historical project of the Cuban people and the Party.
* Social provision, education and healthcare are cited as achievements of the revolutionary process.
* Performance and survival of the revolutionary state are invoked as evidence of legitimacy.
* The concrete interests of the Cuban people and working population are presented as the constituency the project serves.
* National strength and independence appear as goods for a living political community under external pressure.
* Continuity of the revolutionary project is treated as a political requirement of the same secular frame.

### Expanded Analysis
Revolutionary socialism as historical project, social provision, performance, constituency interest, and national strength organise the moral conversation. These are secular this-worldly claims. No divine command, Cosmic law, worship-level deification, or sacralised Ideal organises legitimacy. Transcendent sources are entirely absent from the moral justifications. The foundation is Mundane.
