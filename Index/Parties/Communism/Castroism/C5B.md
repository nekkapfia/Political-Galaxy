# Cultural 5B: Communism – Castroism
## Radicalism (Pace of Change) Analysis
**Score: 80**

### Overview

Castroism, especially in its foundational decades, pursued compressed, high-volume institutional transformation: rapid nationalisations, the literacy campaign, mass organisations and restructuring of property, education and political life with limited tolerance for extended deliberation or gradual sequencing. Later periods show more administrative gradualism, but the governing ideology and founding practice remain accelerationist.

### Observable Pace and Intensity

* 1959–60 nationalisation sequence: agrarian reform (May 1959), US property waves, Laws 890/891 (October 1960) — compressed transfer of the commanding heights.
* Revolutionary Offensive (March 1968): completion of private retail nationalisation in a single campaign year.
* National Literacy Campaign (calendar year 1961): ~707,000 alphabetised with a mass instructor mobilisation on the order of ~271,000 — short, intense delivery rather than multi-decade sequencing.
* Creation of CDR (from September 1960), new state structures and Party monopoly without prolonged pluralist negotiation.
* Operation Carlota (Angola): rapid air and sea deployment from November 1975, reaching tens of thousands of troops within months — decisive commitment tempo.
* Legal and administrative mechanisms that prioritised revolutionary urgency over extended consultation or pilot-only reform.
* Post-Soviet Special Period and later reforms show capacity for slower adaptation under constraint, but the ideological preference remains for decisive transformative action when the regime prioritises a goal.
* Opposition to liberal deliberative or market-gradualist sequencing when framed as delay of socialist construction.

### Expanded Analysis

Crisis and founding-revolution acceleration are not dismissed, but Castroism converted accelerated transformation into the normal mode for core institutional change across a sustained founding period (especially 1959–68). That matches high-radicalism exemplars more than consensus gradualism. Later caution under economic stress moderates but does not erase the programmatic preference for rapid, totalising change when the Party chooses to move. Score reflects the system’s characteristic tempo, not only a single crisis year.

Angola appears here only as evidence of commitment tempo (compressed deployment), not as foreign-policy content (which belongs on Core 2B).

### Why this produces a Final Score of 80

Sustained compressed transformation of property, education, political organisation and selected international commitments, with low institutional tolerance for deliberative delay on priority goals, places Castroism at 80 on pace and intensity of change.
