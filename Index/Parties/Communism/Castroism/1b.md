## Regulation

### Overview
Castroism organised the Cuban economy around dominant state ownership of production, central planning of investment and output, administrative labour allocation, long-duration rationing as a standing allocation system, and restricted residual private and cooperative activity under size caps and political oversight.

### Relevant Policies
- Dominant state ownership of industry, land (after agrarian reform) and services
- Central planning of investment, output and material allocation
- Administrative labour allocation and restrictions on independent employment
- Long-duration rationing of key consumer goods as a standing distribution system
- Restricted residual private and cooperative activity (size caps, negative lists, oversight; periodic tightening)
- Priority of political and social objectives in resource allocation
- Limited openings that did not restore independent decision-making in the commanding heights

### Expanded Analysis
Private and cooperative activity remained quantitatively and legally subordinate. Planning directed investment and production. Labour allocation operated under administrative constraints. Rationing shaped consumption for extended periods as a normal institutional feature, not only a short emergency. Residual market elements after limited openings did not alter state dominance of the productive sector. Relative to floor bands: money and residual private/cooperative activity persisted; the system did not abolish all private economic forms. The package remains inside extensive state control of production, investment and labour.
