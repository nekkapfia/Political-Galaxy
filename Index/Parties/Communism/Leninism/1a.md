# Core 1A: Communism – Leninism
## Personal Autonomy Analysis
**Endos Score: 18**
**Xenos Score: 10**

### Overview

Leninism as a governing political force produces a severely restrictive Core 1A profile defined by one-party monopoly, suppression of independent political expression and press, Cheka-led political terror, early concentration camps and forced labour for opponents, and class-enemy designation with differential coercion. Cumulative weight sits at the border of extreme restrictions and upper ideological-enforcement bands. A clear Endos/Xenos split applies.

### Core 1A Relevant Policies / Elements

* Suppression of the Constituent Assembly and elimination of competitive multi-party politics.
* Banning of opposition parties and independent socialist, anarchist and liberal organisations; no legal channel for organised political dissent.
* Creation of the Cheka and systematic use of revolutionary terror, hostage-taking and summary execution against class and political enemies.
* Comprehensive press controls and closure of non-Bolshevik newspapers; political publication outside Party control eliminated.
* War Communism measures including forced labour compulsion and coercive grain requisition enforced by political violence (economic ownership structure itself is out of scope for Core 1A).
* Early concentration camps and forced labour for political and class opponents as standing instruments of policy.
* Red Terror as explicit, announced policy against designated internal enemies rather than episodic emergency measure.
* Ban on factions within the Party itself (from 1921), further closing internal political speech.
* Residual private life outside the political sphere remained larger than under later Stalinism, but political expression and association were already tightly restricted.

### Expanded Analysis

Leninism replaced residual 1917 pluralism with single-party monopoly backed by political police and revolutionary violence. Independent political organisation, free press and organised opposition were eliminated as a matter of programme, not temporary wartime excess. Class and political enemies faced terror, camps and summary violence as routine tools of rule.

For Endos — workers and accepted Soviet citizens outside enemy categories — public political speech and association outside the Party were closed. Private non-political life retained limited residual space that later Stalinism would shrink further. Placement at 18 reflects severe, systematic restriction of the political domain and forced labour compulsion without the full bureaucratic totalisation, internal passport regime and mass operations of the 1930s.

For Xenos — bourgeoisie, opposition socialists, anarchists, kulaks under War Communism, and other designated enemies — coercion was lethal and systematic: Red Terror, camps, hostage-taking, expropriation. That differential pulls the out-group score into the upper floor / extreme-restriction band.

Framing as revolutionary necessity or civil-war emergency is discarded. The restrictive architecture persisted as constitutive of the regime after major combat ended.

### Why this produces Endos 18 / Xenos 10

One-party monopoly, press elimination, Cheka terror, early camps, labour compulsion and faction ban place Endos at 18. Designated class and political enemies under Red Terror and camp systems produce Xenos 10.
