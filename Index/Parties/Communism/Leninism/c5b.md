# Cultural 5B: Communism – Leninism
## Radicalism (Pace of Change) Analysis
**Score: 86**

### Overview

Leninism is characterised by rapid revolutionary seizure and immediate institutional replacement. War Communism, the creation of new state and Party structures, and the suppression of intervening organisations were carried through on compressed timelines with low tolerance for gradualist or pluralist sequencing in the foundational period.

### Observable Pace and Intensity

* October 1917 seizure followed by rapid construction of new state organs and dismantling of prior institutional layers.
* War Communism (1918–21): compressed economic and administrative controls, forced requisitioning and centralisation under extreme time pressure.
* Creation of Party monopoly and suppression of independent political and civil organisations on short timelines.
* Legal and administrative practice that prioritised revolutionary urgency over extended consultation or pilot reform.
* Later New Economic Policy introduced partial stabilisation, but the founding model remained accelerationist.
* Observable pattern in the core revolutionary years shows high-volume institutional transformation with minimal deliberative intervals.

### Expanded Analysis

Leninism established accelerated institutional replacement as the founding method of the Soviet system. Subsequent stabilisation phases moderate the long-term average, yet the characteristic tempo of the revolutionary and War Communism periods remains high-radicalism. The score of 86 reflects that foundational design against delay.

### Why this produces a Final Score of 86

Rapid revolutionary institutional replacement, War Communism’s compressed controls, and low tolerance for deliberative sequencing in the founding period place Leninism at 86.
