# Cultural 2B: Communism – Leninism
## National Pride Analysis
**Score: 48**

### Overview
Leninism subordinated national identity to class and internationalist criteria while still mobilising Russian and Soviet symbols for state purposes. National pride was instrumental and secondary. Residual baseline of predominantly critical national framing: traditional pride is limited or heavily qualified; critical and atonement narratives carry substantial institutional weight.

### Relevant Policies / Elements
* Official ideology prioritised class and internationalism over national continuity.
* Russian and Soviet symbols used instrumentally for state legitimacy.
* Pre-revolutionary national history largely criticised or reframed.
* Cultural policy oscillated between utilisation and suspicion of national forms.
* Residual baseline of predominantly critical national framing: traditional pride is limited or heavily qualified; critical and atonement narratives carry substantial institutional weight.

### Expanded Analysis
Leninism produces a score near the Balanced/Negative boundary. National identity was never the primary organising principle; it was mobilised when useful and criticised when it conflicted with class ideology. The result is limited and instrumental rather than affirmative national pride.

### Why this produces a Final Score of 48
Instrumental and secondary treatment of national identity, with official priority given to class and internationalist frames, places Leninism at the lower edge of the Balanced band.
