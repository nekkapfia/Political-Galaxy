## Regulation

### Overview
Leninism in its War Communism phase organised the economy around requisitioning, nationalisation of large industry, labour direction and suppression of private trade; NEP later restored limited market mechanisms under continued party control of the commanding heights.

### Relevant Policies / Elements
* Nationalisation of banks, large industry and the commanding heights
* Forced requisitioning of agricultural surplus (prodrazvyorstka) enforced by state detachments
* Suppression of private trade and “speculation” during War Communism
* State direction of labour and resources
* NEP residual markets (1921–28) as temporary retreat, not the defining mechanical core of the model being scored
* Trajectory toward centralised planning and reduced independent private decision-making
* Use of political police and requisitioning apparatus as enforcement of economic extraction

### Expanded Analysis
Leninism in its War Communism phase organised the economy around requisitioning, nationalisation of large industry, labour direction and suppression of private trade; NEP later restored limited market mechanisms under continued party control of the commanding heights. The institutional pattern is extensive state control of ownership, investment, labour allocation and surplus claim. Residual private activity, where it exists at all, is confined to the margins stated in the policy set. Ownership, forced labour and confiscatory mechanisms are Regulation; they are not mixed into the Taxation algebra.
