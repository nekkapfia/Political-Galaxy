## Regulation

### Overview
Leninism, in its peak War Communism expression (1918–1921), combined extensive nationalisation with forced grain requisitioning and severe restriction of private trade. The economy was subordinated to the military and political survival of the new regime through administrative command.

### Relevant Policies
- Rapid nationalisation of large industry, banking, transport and foreign trade
- Extension of state control into smaller enterprises and trade
- Forced grain requisitioning (prodrazvyorstka) at fixed low prices or by force
- Severe restriction or suppression of private trade
- Administrative allocation of resources to the Red Army and urban centres
- Suppression of independent peasant marketing

### Expanded Analysis
Under peak War Communism the state claimed the decisive share of available output through nationalisation and requisitioning. Private economic decision-making was driven underground or eliminated in the formal sector. Consumption was subordinated to military and urban priorities, producing famine conditions in several regions. The subsequent NEP represented a tactical retreat from this intensity; the pure Leninist extraction moment is the War Communism phase. The package exhibits extensive nationalisation, forced requisitioning and suppression of private trade.

### Final Score
The decisive mechanical effects are extensive nationalisation, forced agricultural requisitioning, and suppression of private trade under administrative-military command. This produces a Regulation Score of 22.

## Taxation

These proposals are scored via the separate Taxation Score method (not included in the Regulation Score above).

Peak War Communism is an extreme command economy operating under conditions of institutional collapse and hyperinflation. Formal tax statistics are incomplete and distorted. Extraction occurred primarily through requisitioning and nationalisation. The standard tax-to-GDP formula is inappropriate.

**Alternative Method applied**

Taxation Score = 100 − 75 = **25**

Justification rests on the intensity of prodrazvyorstka, nationalisation decrees, collapse of residual private claims, and the recognition in the subsequent NEP that the extraction level was unsustainable.

**Taxation Score: 25**
