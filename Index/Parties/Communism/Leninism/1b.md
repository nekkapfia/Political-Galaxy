## Regulation

### Overview
Leninism in power combined nationalisation of the commanding heights, forced requisitioning of agricultural surplus (prodrazvyorstka), suppression of private trade during War Communism, and state direction of labour and resources. The NEP restored limited markets as a temporary retreat; the defining command core is War Communism and the trajectory toward centralised control, not the NEP interlude alone.

### Relevant Policies
- Nationalisation of banks, large industry and the commanding heights
- Forced requisitioning of agricultural surplus (prodrazvyorstka) enforced by state detachments
- Suppression of private trade and “speculation” during War Communism
- State direction of labour and resources
- NEP residual markets (1921–28) as temporary retreat, not the defining mechanical core of the model being scored
- Trajectory toward centralised planning and reduced independent private decision-making
- Use of political police and requisitioning apparatus as enforcement of economic extraction

### Expanded Analysis
War Communism extinguished ordinary private commercial decision-making through requisitioning, nationalisation and suppression of trade. Enforcement was coercive. The NEP restored limited market mechanisms while retaining state control of the commanding heights; scoring the Leninist package by averaging NEP softens the command core. Relative to floor bands: even War Communism did not permanently abolish money in the absolute sense later seen under the Khmer Rouge, but private trade and independent agricultural disposal were criminalised and suppressed. The defining mechanical package matches extensive state control organised around regime survival and political goals, with the War Communism core sitting in the lower portion of that range.
