# Cultural 4A: Communism – Leninism
## Social Determinism Analysis
**Score: 50**

### Overview
Early Soviet Leninism treated class origin and political reliability as important filters on advancement while retaining comparatively more formal channels for upward mobility through Party, education, and industrial promotion than the later Stalinist hardening. Individual effort and political conformity could produce trajectory change, but origin markers still created routine differential ease. The system sits at the boundary of the noticeable-shift band.

### Relevant Policies / Elements
* Class-origin considerations in Party recruitment, education, and promotion, especially in the early post-revolutionary years.
* Formal mobility pathways through technical education, Red Army advancement, and industrial hierarchies that remained relatively open compared with later periods.
* Political reliability as an additional gate that interacted with, but was not identical to, hereditary class background.
* Residual capacity for individuals from non-elite origins to rise rapidly through competence and revolutionary service.
* Absence of a fully stabilised multi-generational hereditary status system.

### Expanded Analysis
Cultural 4A scores the degree to which birth circumstances constrain outcomes. Leninism retained a strong class analysis and used origin as a practical filter, yet the early Soviet system still offered substantial formal mobility channels. The pattern is lighter than Stalinist or Maoist origin-gated regimes and heavier than pure liberal residual systems. It fits the upper end of the 41–50 / lower end of the 51–59 range: access to better pathways was possible through effort and political service, but origin and reliability still shaped ease and options. Soft statistical correlation without institutional filters would score lower; the early Soviet origin and loyalty practices supply the additional weight.

Class-origin and reliability filters combined with comparatively open formal mobility channels place Leninism at the boundary of the 41–50 and 51–59 bands. Score 50.
