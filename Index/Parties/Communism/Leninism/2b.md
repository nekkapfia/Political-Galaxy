# Core 2B: Communism – Leninism
## International Sovereignty Analysis
**Score: 26**

### Overview
Leninism institutionalises revolutionary internationalism through the Comintern and early Soviet interventions on the periphery. The pattern includes cross-border political control efforts, Red Army operations beyond core Russian territory in the civil-war period, and organised support for foreign revolutionary seizure of power. Residual baseline of non-interventionist restraint is absent; the doctrine treats external revolutionary assistance as a standing obligation.

### Relevant Policies / Elements
* Communist International (Comintern, founded 1919) as a permanent structure for directing, funding and assisting foreign communist parties toward revolutionary takeover of their own states.
* Red Army interventions and political restructuring in borderlands and neighbouring territories during and after the civil war (Ukraine, Caucasus, Central Asia, Polish campaign 1920).
* Doctrine of the Soviet state as the base of world revolution, generating corresponding external obligations to support foreign seizures of power.
* Organised training, cadre development and material support for foreign revolutionary movements as legitimate foreign-policy activity.
* Early attempts at political control over neighbouring socialist or revolutionary formations.
* Residual baseline: no institutional preference for non-interference; external revolutionary assistance is the default posture rather than an exception.
* Self-defence of Soviet territory is out of scope for Core 2B.

### Expanded Analysis
Core 2B measures interference in other states’ internal affairs. Leninism converts revolutionary internationalism into organised state and party instruments that aim to reshape foreign political orders. The Comintern is a standing transnational command-and-support structure, not episodic diplomacy. Red Army operations beyond the core Russian lands during the civil-war and immediate post-war period established the precedent of using military force to influence neighbouring internal arrangements. Later Stalinist satellite systems intensified the same logic; the Leninist foundation already sits well below non-interventionist baselines because the residual posture is active external revolutionary assistance rather than restraint.

### Why this produces a Final Score of 26
Standing Comintern direction of foreign parties, early cross-border military-political control, and the doctrinal obligation to support revolutionary seizures of power produce a clear low-20s pattern of systematic interference, only partially mitigated by the still-formative character of the early Soviet state.
