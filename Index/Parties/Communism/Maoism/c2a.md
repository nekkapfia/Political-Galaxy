# Cultural 2A: Communism – Maoism
## Individuality Analysis
**Score: 16**

### Overview
Maoism subordinated individual status to class origin, political reliability and revolutionary category, with continuous mass-campaign reinforcement of collective over person-unit allocation. Residual baseline of group unit of measure: significant residual institutional practice organises rights, opportunities or moral standing around group membership rather than the individual alone.

### Allocation Mechanisms
* Class-background and political-reliability criteria structuring education, advancement and political trust.
* Mass campaigns and work-unit systems mediating status and opportunity.
* Collective and party-defined categories organising recognition and access.
* Individual performance channels existing only inside revolutionary-category constraints.
* Independent individual status outside approved structures systematically suppressed.
* Residual baseline of group unit of measure: significant residual institutional practice organises rights, opportunities or moral standing around group membership rather than the individual alone.

### Expanded Analysis
The dominant allocation principle is collective-political rather than individual-meritocratic. Even where technical or examination channels existed, political category systematically structured high-status outcomes. The person as sovereign agent was secondary to revolutionary-class location. The pattern is severe group collectivism.

### Why this produces a Final Score of 16
Revolutionary-class and political categories as primary units of status and opportunity place Maoism in the severe group-collectivism band. Score 16.
