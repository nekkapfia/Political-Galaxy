# Cultural 5B: Communism – Maoism
## Radicalism (Pace of Change) Analysis
**Score: 92**

### Overview

Maoism is defined by continuous, high-intensity campaigns of institutional and social transformation. The Great Leap Forward, the Cultural Revolution and the doctrine of permanent or continuous revolution institutionalised rapid, disruptive change as the normal mode of governance for extended periods, with limited tolerance for gradualist sequencing or stabilising pauses.

### Observable Pace and Intensity

* Great Leap Forward (1958–62): compressed attempt at rapid industrialisation and collectivisation on a national scale with extreme mobilisation intensity.
* Cultural Revolution (1966–76): multi-year campaign of institutional rupture, mass mobilisation against established Party and cultural structures, and repeated waves of disruptive reset.
* Doctrine of continuous revolution treating ongoing transformative campaigns as necessary rather than exceptional.
* Use of mass mobilisation, campaign methods and political directives to override normal administrative sequencing and expert gradualism.
* Education, media and organisational life structured around urgency of transformation and struggle against stabilising or “revisionist” delay.
* Observable pattern across multiple decades shows high volume and short intervals between major disruptive initiatives.

### Expanded Analysis

Maoism converted accelerated, campaign-style transformation into a sustained governing method. While not every year reached the absolute intensity of 1958–61 or 1966–69, the overall system design rejected gradualist stabilisation as a legitimate long-term mode. This places Maoism firmly in the extreme high band, just below the absolute floor-adjacent cases such as the Khmer Rouge.

### Why this produces a Final Score of 92

Sustained reliance on high-intensity mass campaigns and continuous revolution as the normal mode of institutional change, with low tolerance for deliberative or gradualist sequencing, places Maoism at 92.
