# Cultural 2B: Communism – Maoism
## National Pride Analysis
**Score: 52**

### Overview
Maoism combined strong affirmation of Chinese national revival and revolutionary achievement with periodic critical campaigns against “old” culture and internal enemies. National pride was real but subordinate to ideological mobilisation. Residual baseline of mixed or conditional national framing: pride and critical perspectives coexist without a single dominant residual direction.

### Relevant Policies / Elements
* Affirmative narrative of Chinese standing up and revolutionary transformation.
* Cultural Revolution and related campaigns attacked traditional cultural forms while still claiming national purpose.
* Symbols and rituals celebrated the party, the leader and national recovery.
* Pre-revolutionary history selectively criticised or utilised according to political need.
* Residual baseline of mixed or conditional national framing: pride and critical perspectives coexist without a single dominant residual direction.

### Expanded Analysis
Maoism sits in the Balanced band. National pride in China’s recovery and revolutionary path was genuine and institutionally promoted, yet it was repeatedly disrupted by ideological campaigns that attacked traditional continuity. The result is contested rather than pure pride orthodoxy.

### Why this produces a Final Score of 52
Genuine national-affirmative content coexisting with repeated critical campaigns against traditional culture places Maoism in the Balanced band.
