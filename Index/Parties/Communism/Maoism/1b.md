## Regulation

### Overview
Maoism organised the Chinese economy through collectivisation and communes, high forced extraction from agriculture, administrative mass labour mobilisation, hukou mobility controls, detailed planning targets, and periodic political campaigns that disrupted ordinary enterprise decision-making. Peak periods (notably the Great Leap Forward) approached near-command intensity.

### Relevant Policies
- Collectivisation of agriculture and formation of people’s communes
- High forced extraction from agriculture (procurement quotas; price scissors)
- Administrative mass labour mobilisation (Great Leap campaigns, backyard steel, water conservancy)
- Hukou system restricting labour mobility and rural-urban movement
- Priority of accumulation and political goals over household consumption
- Detailed planning targets for industry and agriculture
- Residual household plots periodically restricted or suppressed
- Cultural Revolution disruption of ordinary managerial decision-making (political struggle replacing enterprise autonomy)
- State ownership of industry and monopoly of key distribution channels
- Famine (GLF) as mechanical outcome of extraction and mobilisation intensity

### Expanded Analysis
Communes and procurement claimed the agricultural surplus under administrative direction. Mass campaigns mobilised labour outside household or market choice. Hukou constrained mobility. Planning and political campaigns directed industry. Residual plots were politically contingent. Peak GLF conditions involved extreme administrative direction of labour and materials with catastrophic consumption outcomes — evidence of how complete the claim on labour and output could become. Relative to floor bands: money, residual plots and some household activity persisted outside peak campaign years; the system did not permanently abolish all private economic activity. Averaging the whole Mao period softens peak severity; the mechanical core remains extensive state control of production, investment and labour organised around political and accumulation goals, with peak years sitting at or below the lower edge of that range.
