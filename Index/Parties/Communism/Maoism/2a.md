# Core 2A: Communism – Maoism
## National Sovereignty Analysis
**Score: 81**

### Overview
Maoism combined exclusive Party-state control of core sovereign functions with deliberate rejection of Soviet institutional leadership and a policy of national self-reliance. External institutional authority over Chinese internal affairs was rejected.

### National Sovereignty Relevant Policies / Elements
* Absolute Party-state control of territory, borders, law and armed forces.
* Break with the Soviet Union and rejection of Soviet institutional leadership.
* Policy of self-reliance in critical economic and military domains.
* Selective external engagement (including the later US opening) subordinated to Chinese strategic control.
* Rejection of external judicial or political authority over Chinese internal affairs.

### Expanded Analysis
Maoism maintained absolute Party-state control of territory, borders, law and armed forces, broke with Soviet institutional leadership, and pursued self-reliance in critical economic and military domains. External engagement, including the later opening to the United States, remained subordinated to Chinese strategic control. No external institutional authority over Chinese internal affairs was accepted. The institutional pattern on Core 2A is exclusive national control combined with deliberate rejection of external institutional constraint. International revolutionary or socialist organisation was treated as an instrument of Chinese state interest rather than a constraint upon it. Primary drag from external pooling of authority is near-absent.

### Why this produces a Final Score of 81
Exclusive Party-state control, break with Soviet institutional leadership and self-reliance in critical domains place the final set inside the 80–89 High – Strict closure with minimal controlled connection points band. The score of 81 follows from exclusive national control without external pooling of core authority.
