# Core 2B: Communism – Maoism
## International Sovereignty Analysis
**Score: 30**

### Overview
Maoism combines support for people’s-war insurgencies abroad, border conflicts with neighbouring states, and ideological claims to leadership of international revolutionary movements. External interference is real and sustained, though less systematically institutionalised as a multi-country occupation system than Stalinism. Residual baseline of revolutionary internationalism remains active rather than restrained.

### Relevant Policies / Elements
* Export of people’s-war doctrine and material or political support for foreign insurgencies (e.g. Southeast Asia, Africa, Latin America in varying intensity).
* Border conflicts with India, the Soviet Union and Vietnam that involved territorial and political pressure beyond pure defensive posture.
* Ideological competition for leadership of international communist and anti-imperialist movements, generating organised external political influence efforts.
* No permanent Eastern-Europe-style satellite empire, but clear pattern of cross-border revolutionary assistance and territorial pressure.
* Residual baseline of revolutionary internationalism: external support for people’s war and ideological leadership claims remain standing orientations rather than exceptions.
* Self-defence of Chinese territory is out of scope for Core 2B.

### Expanded Analysis
Core 2B scores interference in other nations’ internal arrangements. Maoist support for foreign revolutionary violence and repeated border conflicts constitute observable external interference. The absence of a formal multi-state occupation system keeps the score above the lowest Stalinist or Nazi placements, yet the overall pattern remains clearly interventionist because the residual doctrine treats external revolutionary assistance as legitimate and desirable.

### Why this produces a Final Score of 30
Revolutionary export, repeated border conflicts, and a residual baseline of active internationalist assistance produce a low score without reaching the absolute floor of permanent multi-country occupation.
