# Core 2B: Communism – Hoxhaism
## International Sovereignty Analysis
**Score: 74**

### Overview
Hoxhaist Albania, after successive breaks with Yugoslavia, the Soviet Union and China, adopted an extreme fortress isolationism. External military projection was minimal; the regime concentrated on domestic control and autarky. High placement reflects sustained non-interference in other states’ internal affairs rather than expansion or proxy warfare. Residual baseline is deliberate withdrawal from external revolutionary or occupation commitments.

### Relevant Policies / Elements
* Progressive rupture with all major communist patrons; refusal of further foreign military basing or integration.
* No sustained overseas combat deployments or occupation regimes.
* Minimal material support for foreign revolutionary movements compared with Soviet or Chinese practice.
* Fortress defence posture oriented to territorial survival, not external transformation of other governments.
* Residual baseline of isolationist non-interference: the mature Hoxhaist system institutionalised withdrawal rather than external political control.
* Self-defence of Albanian territory is out of scope for Core 2B.

### Expanded Analysis
Core 2B measures interference in other nations’ internal arrangements. Hoxhaism’s defining external pattern was withdrawal and isolation. After the Sino-Albanian split the regime had neither the capacity nor the doctrine for large-scale cross-border political or military control. Internal totalitarianism is scored on other axes; it does not automatically lower Core 2B. The residual posture is non-interference by design, not by incapacity alone.

### Why this produces a Final Score of 74
Extreme isolationism, absence of sustained external intervention or occupation, and a residual baseline of deliberate non-interference place Hoxhaism in the high non-intervention band.
