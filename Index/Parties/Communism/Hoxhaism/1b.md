## Regulation

### Overview
Hoxhaism organised the Albanian economy around near-total state ownership, extreme central planning, administrative labour direction, and constitutional isolation from foreign capital and residual market forms. Private counterweight was minimal.

### Relevant Policies
- Near-total state ownership of productive capital
- Extreme central planning of production, investment and allocation
- Administrative labour direction
- Constitutional and practical isolation from foreign capital and residual market forms
- Minimal private plots and private activity under tight constraint
- Priority of political and autarkic objectives over household consumption
- No significant independent enterprise sector at any stage

### Expanded Analysis
The state owned essentially the entire formal productive sector. Planning was comprehensive. Labour was administratively directed. Foreign capital and significant residual market organisation were excluded by design. Private activity survived only at the margins. Relative to floor bands: residual household activity and money persisted; the system did not reach absolute extinction of all private economic forms. The near-absence of private counterweight places the package in the lower half of extensive state control.
