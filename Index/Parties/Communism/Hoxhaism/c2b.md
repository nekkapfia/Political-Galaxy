# Cultural 2B: Communism – Hoxhaism
## National Pride Analysis
**Score: 54**

### Overview
Hoxhaism combined intense Albanian national isolationism and affirmation with rigid Stalinist ideology. National pride in Albania’s unique path and self-reliance was real and institutionally promoted, yet remained heavily subordinated to party orthodoxy and ideological purity. Residual baseline of mixed or conditional national framing: pride and critical perspectives coexist without a single dominant residual direction.

### Relevant Policies / Elements
* Strong affirmative narrative of Albanian independence, self-reliance and defiance of both East and West.
* Extreme isolationism framed as national defence and civilisational uniqueness.
* Cultural policy oscillated between national affirmation and ideological purification campaigns.
* Pre-communist history selectively utilised when it served the regime and criticised when it conflicted with orthodoxy.
* Symbols and rituals affirmed the Albanian nation under party leadership rather than traditional pre-communist continuity.
* Residual baseline of mixed or conditional national framing: pride and critical perspectives coexist without a single dominant residual direction.

### Expanded Analysis
Hoxhaism produces a Positive-band score. Albanian national pride was genuine and formed a core part of the regime’s isolationist identity. At the same time it remained conditional on absolute ideological conformity; traditional cultural forms could be attacked when they conflicted with the party line. The result is real but constrained national affirmation rather than open historical pride or organising national shame.

### Why this produces a Final Score of 54
Institutional affirmation of Albanian national uniqueness and self-reliance, qualified by rigid ideological control and selective treatment of the pre-communist past, places Hoxhaism in the Positive band.
