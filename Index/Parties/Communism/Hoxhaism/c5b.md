# Cultural 5B: Communism – Hoxhaism
## Radicalism (Pace of Change) Analysis
**Score: 74**

### Overview

Hoxhaism combined extreme isolationist control with periods of rapid institutional and social transformation, particularly in the early decades of the Albanian regime. Collectivisation, atheism campaigns, and repeated political resets were conducted with high intensity and limited tolerance for gradualist sequencing, even as later years showed greater administrative rigidity.

### Observable Pace and Intensity

* Rapid post-war institutional capture and alignment with Soviet then Chinese models, followed by abrupt breaks.
* Forced collectivisation and rural transformation campaigns executed on compressed timelines.
* Aggressive atheism and cultural campaigns that dismantled independent religious and traditional structures with limited sequencing.
* Political and organisational purges conducted as high-intensity resets rather than gradual personnel change.
* Extreme isolation reinforced central capacity to impose tempo without external deliberative constraints.
* Later periods of stagnation and defensive rigidity moderate the long-term average but do not erase the early high-intensity design.

### Expanded Analysis

Hoxhaism’s early decades display clear high-radicalism features: compressed campaigns, low tolerance for intervening process, and repeated institutional rupture. Sustained isolation later produced rigidity that can appear gradualist, yet the characteristic governing method remained capable of and oriented toward rapid, totalising directives. The score of 74 sits in the mid-high to high band, below the most extreme continuous-revolution cases.

### Why this produces a Final Score of 74

Early high-intensity transformation campaigns and sustained capacity for rapid institutional directives, tempered by later rigidity, place Hoxhaism at 74.
