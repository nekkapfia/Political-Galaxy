# Cultural 5A: Communism – Hoxhaism
## Tradition vs Progress Analysis
**Score: 10**

### Overview
Hoxhaism subordinated inherited family, religious and national-continuity transmission to extreme party control, including a total anti-religious campaign and rejection of later instrumental nationalism of the Soviet type.

### Cultural 5A Relevant Policies / Elements
* Total institutional ban on religious practice; mass closure of churches, mosques and religious institutions; religion treated as incompatible with the regime.
* Education and public culture monopolised by anti-revisionist Marxism-Leninism and Party ideology; no confessional or classical traditional curriculum as public baseline.
* Family and social organisation subordinated to Party and security-police surveillance; independent customary authority eliminated as rival transmission.
* Extreme external isolation after breaks with Yugoslavia, the Soviet Union and China, closing residual external traditional or religious influence.
* No later instrumental rehabilitation of national-religious tradition of the wartime Soviet Orthodox utility type.
* Residual pre-communist religious and customary order was extinguished as public institutional baseline; elevating it remained prohibited.
* Domain hierarchy: religious authority and education content redesigned at the root against inherited continuity.

### Expanded Analysis
Hoxhaism combines total anti-religious institutional policy with Party monopoly of education and cultural transmission. Primary domains leave no durable public space for inherited confessional or customary baselines: religion was banned as institutional practice; education transmitted Party ideology only; family and social life were politicised under surveillance. Unlike Stalinism's later instrumental use of Russian national history and wartime Orthodox utility, Hoxhaism did not develop a comparable rehabilitative nationalism that softens the transformative pattern. The residual pre-communist religious and customary order was extinguished as public baseline. Domain hierarchy places religious authority and education first—both reconstructed against inherited continuity.

### Why this produces a Final Score of 10
Total religious ban, Party monopoly of education and cultural transmission, and absence of later instrumental national-religious rehabilitation place Hoxhaism at the floor band (0–10). Higher placement would require residual institutional protection of traditional transmission that the locked analysis does not show.
