## Regulation

### Overview
Juche organises the North Korean economy around near-total state and military ownership and control, Songun (military-first) allocation priority, extreme labour extraction and mass mobilisation, administrative control of mobility and political classification affecting economic opportunity, and subordination of residual informal markets.

### Relevant Policies
- Near-total state and military ownership of productive capital
- Songun / military-first allocation as a standing priority rule over civilian consumption
- Extreme labour extraction and mass mobilisation (speed battles, campaign labour)
- Administrative control of labour allocation and mobility
- Political classification systems affecting access to economic opportunity and rations
- Residual informal markets under surveillance and periodic crackdown
- Severe restrictions on independent private enterprise of any scale
- Famine (Arduous March) as mechanical outcome of extraction and priority rules

### Expanded Analysis
The formal productive sector is state- or military-controlled. Songun prioritises military and regime needs over civilian consumption as a standing rule. Labour is subject to intensive mobilisation. Mobility and political classification constrain economic opportunity. Residual informal markets exist under political risk and do not restore independent decision-making in the commanding heights. Relative to floor bands: informal markets and residual household activity persist; money has not been abolished. The combination of ownership, military-first priority, mass mobilisation and mobility/political controls places the package in the lower half of extensive state control; peak famine years sit lower still.
