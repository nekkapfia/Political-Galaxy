# Cultural 1A: Communism – Stalinism
## Foundation Analysis
**Score: 89**

### Overview
Stalinism grounds political legitimacy in the Party-state as embodiment of historical necessity, rapid socialist construction, and the elimination of class enemies as moral-historical requirements. Ordinary demos preference, liberal legality and individual interest are extinguished as independent sources. The profile is extreme transcendent lean.

### Moral Justification – Relevant Elements
* Party and leader as authoritative interpreters of Marxist-Leninist historical truth.
* Forced industrialisation and collectivisation as necessary stages of historical progress, not optional policy.
* Class enemy categories subject to terror, deportation and elimination as historical-moral necessity.
* Ordinary legality and preference subordinated to revolutionary necessity.
* Performance and power of the socialist state as proof of correct line inside the ideological frame.
* No residual liberal or demos-based constraint on the project.

### Expanded Analysis
Stalinism organises justification through historical-ideological transcendence enforced by the Party-state. The demos does not choose the line; the line defines legitimate politics. Terror, collectivisation and mass repression are justified as requirements of historical progress rather than as contingent policy failures. Mundane performance language exists only as validation of the authorised path. The pattern is extreme transcendent lean.

### Why this produces a Final Score of 89
Historical necessity and Party authority as binding higher-order sources that extinguish ordinary demos justification produce extreme transcendent lean. The score is 89.
