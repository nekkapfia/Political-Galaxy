# Cultural 3: Communism – Stalinism
## Ethnic and Cultural Exclusivity Analysis
**Score: 3A 0 | 3B 76**

### Overview
Stalinism defined membership of the core political community by class position, political reliability, and ideological loyalty rather than by blood. Nationalities policy produced severe repression of specific groups, but the Exclusion Test is not met: no ethnicity was permanently barred from membership solely on ethnic grounds under a formal ancestral code. Trusted membership required dense political-cultural transformation and severance from “bourgeois”, religious, and particularist identities. Immigration and deportation remain out of scope.

### Relevant Policies / Elements
* Membership criteria centred on class origin, political loyalty, and acceptance of the Party line, not ancestral blood.
* Mass deportations and special settlement regimes targeted national groups, but operated as political and security measures rather than permanent ethnic bars to citizenship or Party membership.
* Extreme demands for ideological conformity, cultural homogenisation, and destruction of independent religious and national cultural institutions.
* “Enemy of the people” categories were political, social, and sometimes national, yet the core gatekeeper remained political reliability.
* Immigration and deportation out of scope.

### Expanded Analysis
The Exclusion Test is not met. Despite severe nationalities repression, Stalinism did not operate a racial blood code that permanently excluded ethnicities from membership of the Soviet political community on ancestral grounds alone. 3A is therefore 0.

3B is very high. Full trusted membership required intensive political-cultural transformation, loyalty demonstration, and abandonment of competing identities. This aligns with Predominantly Demanding to High Cultural Demand (70–79).

### Why this produces 3A 0 / 3B 76
3A aligns with 0 (no permanent ethnic bar under Exclusion Test). 3B aligns with 70–79 (dense political-cultural transformation and loyalty demands).
