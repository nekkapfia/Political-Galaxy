# Core 2A: Communism – Stalinism
## National Sovereignty Analysis
**Score: 82**

### Overview
Stalinism constructed a fully sovereign centralised state under the doctrine of socialism in one country, subordinating international communist organisation to Soviet state interests and rejecting external institutional authority over Soviet internal affairs.

### National Sovereignty Relevant Policies / Elements
* “Socialism in one country” as the explicit prioritisation of Soviet state strength over immediate international revolution.
* Exclusive Soviet control of territory, borders, law, military command and economic planning.
* Comintern and later international structures operated as instruments of Soviet foreign policy rather than external authority over the Soviet state.
* Rejection of any external institutional authority over Soviet internal affairs.
* Rapid industrial and military mobilisation under complete national direction.
* External projection into neighbouring states and later Warsaw Pact residual as extension of Soviet state interest rather than pooling of Soviet authority outward.

### Expanded Analysis
Stalinism constructed a fully sovereign, centralised Soviet state under the doctrine of socialism in one country. Territory, borders, law, military command and economic planning remained under exclusive Soviet control. The Comintern and later international structures operated as instruments of Soviet foreign policy rather than as constraints upon the Soviet state. No external institutional authority over Soviet internal affairs was accepted. External projection into neighbouring states and later the Warsaw Pact residual served Soviet state interest rather than pooling of Soviet authority outward. The institutional pattern on Core 2A is exclusive national control with international revolutionary organisation subordinated to state interest. Primary drag from external pooling of authority is near-absent.
