# Core 2B: Communism – Stalinism
## International Sovereignty Analysis
**Score: 22**

### Overview
Stalinism establishes and maintains a system of satellite states in Eastern Europe, conducts direct military interventions to preserve political control (Hungary 1956, Czechoslovakia 1968), and treats neighbouring governments as subordinate elements of a Soviet sphere. The pattern is sustained, systematic interference in other states’ internal political orders. Residual baseline of respect for satellite sovereignty is effectively absent.

### Relevant Policies / Elements
* Installation and enforcement of aligned regimes across Eastern Europe after 1945 (Poland, Hungary, Czechoslovakia, East Germany, Bulgaria, Romania, etc.).
* Military interventions to prevent deviation and restore orthodoxy (Hungary 1956, Czechoslovakia 1968 / Prague Spring).
* Political, security and economic structures (Comecon, Warsaw Pact, bilateral treaties, security-service liaison) that subordinate satellite governments to Moscow.
* Sphere-of-influence doctrine treating neighbouring internal arrangements as legitimate objects of Soviet control rather than sovereign choices.
* Residual baseline of permanent political and military oversight; deviation is not treated as a domestic matter of the satellite state.
* Self-defence of Soviet territory is out of scope for Core 2B.

### Expanded Analysis
Core 2B measures interference in other nations’ internal affairs. Stalinism’s Eastern European system is a textbook case of sustained external political and military control. The installation of aligned regimes, the permanent security and economic architecture that keeps them aligned, and the willingness to use force to reverse deviation (1956, 1968) confirm that satellite internal orders were not treated as sovereign. Occupation, proxy control and forced alignment were normal operating modes, not exceptions. Residual non-interference language is absent; the system is designed around ongoing external direction.

### Why this produces a Final Score of 22
Systematic satellite installation and maintenance, repeated military enforcement of political conformity, and the institutional architecture of subordination place Stalinism near the bottom of the real-world range.
