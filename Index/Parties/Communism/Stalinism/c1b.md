# Cultural 1B: Communism – Stalinism
## Dogmatism Analysis
**Score: 95**

**Orthodoxy: 96, Tribalism: 94**

### Overview
Stalinism combines extreme Party monopoly and terror-backed orthodoxy with comprehensive rejection of external and internal rival frameworks. The line is closed; alternatives are enemies. Both aspects reach near-ceiling intensity, placing Stalinism at the upper end of the Strong band alongside Hoxhaism.

### Relevant Policies / Elements
* Complete Party monopoly; destruction of internal factional opposition.
* Great Terror, show trials and Gulag as mechanisms against deviation and invented enemies.
* State monopoly of media, education and association.
* Ideological categories of wrecking, deviation and counter-revolution covering dissent.
* Liberal, bourgeois, Trotskyist and other socialist currents treated as mortal enemies.
* Residual baseline of total organisational and security capture leaving no recognised internal process for overthrowing foundational principles.

### Expanded Analysis
Orthodoxy is extreme: complete Party monopoly, destruction of internal faction, Great Terror and Gulag mechanisms, and state monopoly of media and education closed legitimate internal challenge to the line. Ideological categories of wrecking, deviation and counter-revolution covered dissent. Tribalism is extreme: liberal, bourgeois, Trotskyist and other socialist currents were mortal enemies rather than legitimate rivals. There was no recognised internal process for overthrowing foundational principles comparable to open factional contestation in plural systems. Mechanisms combined legal, security and organisational totalism. The institutional pattern is near-ceiling dogmatism calibrated at the top of the Strong (86–95) band.

### Why this produces a Final Score of 95
Complete Party monopoly, Great Terror and Gulag enforcement, media/education monopoly, and systematic enemy designation of all rival currents produce extreme Orthodoxy and Tribalism. The formula yields 95, matching the calibrated Stalinism position.
