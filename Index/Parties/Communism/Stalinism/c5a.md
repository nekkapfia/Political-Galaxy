# Cultural 5A: Communism – Stalinism
## Tradition vs Progress Analysis
**Score: 11**

### Overview
Stalinism combined early revolutionary destruction of traditional landed, religious and familial authorities with later instrumental use of Russian national history, military tradition and family policy under strict Party control. Pre-revolutionary traditional continuity was never restored as an autonomous baseline.

### Cultural 5A Relevant Policies / Elements
* Early destruction of traditional landed, religious and familial authorities through collectivisation, dekulakisation and anti-religious campaigns.
* Independent Orthodox and customary authority eliminated as rival sources of social norm transmission; religious institutions permitted only under state control when useful.
* Later partial rehabilitation of Russian national history, military tradition and family policy strictly as instruments of state power, not as restoration of the pre-revolutionary baseline.
* Cultural transmission monopolised by the Party; unmodified Tsarist, autonomous religious or independent peasant-traditional order prohibited as a preferred public model.
* Industrial and social modernisation treated as overriding goals; inherited traditional structures retained no autonomous institutional role in education or family law.
* Residual private custom and instrumental public “tradition” existed only under Party sovereignty—not as a protected continuity default.
* Domain hierarchy: education and family transmission under Party direction; early destruction defines the long-run baseline.

### Expanded Analysis
Primary domains under Stalinism were reorganised against inherited traditional transmission. Collectivisation and anti-religious campaigns broke landed society and the Church as independent carriers of social norms. Education and cultural content were Party-directed; family policy, even when later more conservative in form, remained under state determination rather than autonomous confessional or customary authority. The residual baseline after the early period was not pre-revolutionary continuity restored: it was socialist modernisation with selective instrumental retrieval of national history and family rhetoric. Elevating Tsarist, autonomous Orthodox or independent peasant-traditional order as preferable remained politically illegitimate. Domain hierarchy stays transformative: law and institutional design destroyed independent traditional authorities; education transmitted Party-authorised content. Instrumental nationalism does not convert the pattern into mid-range preservation.

### Why this produces a Final Score of 11
Foundational destruction of traditional landed, religious and familial authorities, Party monopoly of cultural transmission, and prohibition of elevating the pre-revolutionary order as preferred place Stalinism at the edge of the floor / low 11–20 band. Later instrumental recovery of national history and family policy does not restore autonomous traditional baselines.
