# Core 1B: Communism – Stalinism
## Economic Autonomy Analysis
**Final Score: 29**
**Regulation Score: 16**
**Taxation Score: 65**

## Regulation

### Overview
Stalinism organised the Soviet economy around forced industrialisation, near-total state ownership of industry, collectivisation with compulsory procurement, central material-balance planning, administrative labour direction, internal mobility controls, and the use of camp and special-settlement labour as a structural component of the labour-allocation system.

### Relevant Policies / Elements
* Near-total state and cooperative-collective ownership of productive capital by the mid-1930s.
* Collectivisation of agriculture and compulsory grain deliveries under administrative quotas.
* Central planning of production, investment and material balances (Gosplan; five-year plans).
* Administrative direction of labour; criminalisation of unauthorised job-changing and absenteeism (e.g. 1940 decrees).
* Internal passport / propiska system restricting labour mobility and residence.
* Gulag and special-settlement labour as a standing component of labour allocation and resource extraction.
* High forced accumulation rates with priority of heavy industry and military capacity over consumption.
* Residual private activity limited to personal plots and the narrowest legal margins.

### Expanded Analysis
The state claimed the decisive share of national product through ownership and procurement. Collectivisation enabled extraction under compulsory quotas. Planning directed investment and materials. Labour was subject to administrative direction, mobility controls and, at scale, camp and special-settlement labour as an economic input. Criminalisation of ordinary job exit turned labour allocation into a police-enforced system. Residual private activity survived only at the margins. Money and formal residual plots persisted; the system did not abolish all private economic activity in the absolute sense of the lowest bands. The combination of ownership, planning, forced labour as allocation, mobility controls and extraction intensity places the package in the lower half of extensive state control organised around political-industrial objectives.

### Regulation Score
Near-total state ownership, compulsory procurement, central planning, administrative labour direction, internal mobility controls and structural forced labour as allocation, with residual private activity confined to the narrowest margins, produce a Regulation Score of 16.

## Final Score

A = (16 + 65) / 2 = 40.5
L = min(16, 65) = 16
d = |16 − 65| / 100 = 0.49
F = (1 − 0.49) × 40.5 + 0.49 × 16 = 28.495 → **29**

**Final Score: 29**

### Why this produces a Final Score of 29
Regulation at 16 reflects near-total ownership, compulsory procurement, central planning, administrative labour direction, mobility controls and structural forced labour. Taxation at 65 is the pure algebraic inverse of the stated 35% effective fiscal claim under the Alternative Method. The A/L/d combination with strict ceiling produces Final Score 29.
