## Regulation

### Overview
Stalinism organised the Soviet economy around forced industrialisation, near-total state ownership of industry, collectivisation with compulsory procurement, central material-balance planning, administrative labour direction, internal mobility controls, and the use of camp and special-settlement labour as a structural component of the labour-allocation system.

### Relevant Policies
- Near-total state and cooperative-collective ownership of productive capital by the mid-1930s
- Collectivisation of agriculture and compulsory grain deliveries
- Central planning of production, investment and material balances (Gosplan; five-year plans)
- Administrative direction of labour; criminalisation of unauthorised job-changing and absenteeism (e.g. 1940 decrees)
- Internal passport / propiska system restricting labour mobility and residence
- Gulag and special-settlement labour as a standing component of labour allocation and resource extraction (not only political repression)
- High forced accumulation rates (investment share of GNP roughly doubled 1928–1937)
- Priority of heavy industry and military capacity over consumption
- Residual private activity limited to personal plots and the narrowest legal margins
- Famine linked to procurement intensity as a mechanical outcome of extraction (not scored as morality; evidence of claim on output)

### Expanded Analysis
The state claimed the decisive share of national product through ownership and procurement. Collectivisation enabled extraction under compulsory quotas. Planning directed investment and materials. Labour was subject to administrative direction, mobility controls and, at scale, camp/special-settlement labour as an economic input. Criminalisation of ordinary job exit turned labour allocation into a police-enforced system. Residual private activity survived only at the margins. Relative to floor bands: money and formal residual plots persisted; the system did not abolish all private economic activity in the absolute sense of band 0–2. The combination of ownership, planning, forced labour as allocation, mobility controls and extraction intensity places the package in the lower half of extensive state control organised around political-industrial objectives.
