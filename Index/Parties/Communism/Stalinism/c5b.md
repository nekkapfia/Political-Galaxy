# Cultural 5B: Communism – Stalinism
## Radicalism (Pace of Change) Analysis
**Score: 88**

### Overview

Stalinism institutionalised compressed, high-intensity transformation through forced collectivisation, rapid industrialisation under the Five-Year Plans, and repeated political campaigns that overrode gradualist or specialist sequencing. The system treated accelerated structural change as the normal and necessary mode for extended periods.

### Observable Pace and Intensity

* Forced collectivisation (late 1920s–early 1930s) executed as a rapid, nationwide campaign with extreme mobilisation intensity and minimal tolerance for gradual peasant transition.
* First Five-Year Plan and subsequent plans prioritising compressed industrialisation timelines over sequenced, expert-led development.
* Political and institutional campaigns (purges, organisational resets) conducted as high-volume, short-interval operations.
* Administrative and legal mechanisms designed to override local, specialist or deliberative resistance to central tempo.
* Education and propaganda framing rapid transformation and struggle against “delay” or “sabotage” as core virtues.
* Observable pattern across the core Stalinist decades shows sustained preference for accelerated, disruptive implementation.

### Expanded Analysis

Stalinism converted campaign-style acceleration into a prolonged governing method. While intensity varied by year, the institutional design consistently subordinated gradualist and deliberative processes to rapid structural targets. This places Stalinism in the high-radicalism band, immediately below the most extreme total-reset cases.

### Why this produces a Final Score of 88

Sustained use of forced collectivisation, compressed industrial plans and political campaigns that systematically overrode deliberative sequencing places Stalinism at 88.
