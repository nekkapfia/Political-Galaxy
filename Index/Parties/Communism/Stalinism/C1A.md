# Cultural 1A: Stalinism
## Foundation Analysis
Final Score: 0 (50M)

### Overview
Stalinism organises moral legitimacy around historical necessity of socialist construction, the concrete interests of the working class, and performance and power of the socialist state. These are secular this-worldly claims.

### Moral Justification – Relevant Elements

#### Mundane Justifications
* Historical necessity of rapid socialist construction and the elimination of class enemies is framed as the scientific and political requirement of the revolutionary project.
* The concrete interests of the working class and the construction of socialism for the people are invoked as grounds of the project.
* Performance and power of the socialist state are cited as proof of the correct line.
* Material output and state strength appear as evidence of success and legitimacy.
* Forced industrialisation and collectivisation are justified as necessary stages of historical progress toward the classless society.

### Expanded Analysis
Historical necessity, class interest, performance, and material construction organise the moral conversation. These are secular this-worldly claims: historical analysis, constituency interest, and empirical outcomes. No divine command, Cosmic or Natural law, worship-level deification, or sacralisation of a people as ontological Ideal appears as an organising source. Extreme intensity and closure of the project belong on Dogmatism, Radicalism, and related axes — not on Foundation as Transcendent. Transcendent sources are entirely absent from the moral justifications. The foundation is Mundane.
