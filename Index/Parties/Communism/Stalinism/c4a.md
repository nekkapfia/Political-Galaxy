# Cultural 4A: Communism – Stalinism
## Social Determinism Analysis
**Score: 68**

### Overview
Stalinism combined formal mobility channels (education, Party advancement, industrial promotion) with significant gates based on class origin, political reliability, and registration status. Individual effort could produce trajectory change, but only inside constraints set by origin markers and loyalty assessments. The system was neither pure hereditary caste nor pure open mobility; it produced gated, origin-conditioned advancement characteristic of the moderate-to-predominantly deterministic range.

### Relevant Policies / Elements
* Class-origin questionnaires and soft hereditary markers that conditioned access to higher education, Party membership, and responsible posts.
* Political reliability and registration (propiska and related controls) as additional gates on residence, employment, and advancement.
* Formal mobility pathways through technical education and industrial promotion that remained open in principle but were filtered by origin and loyalty.
* Purges and origin-based suspicion that periodically closed pathways for those with “bad” class backgrounds or family associations.
* Residual capacity for individuals from favoured or neutral origins to rise through competence and political conformity.

### Expanded Analysis
Cultural 4A tracks birth constraint versus mobility. Under Stalinism, birth and family background mattered substantially: class origin affected educational and occupational chances, and political reliability of relatives could close doors. At the same time, the system retained real formal channels—technical institutes, Party schools, industrial hierarchies—through which individuals could improve status. The pattern matches the 60–69 band: inter-class movement was possible but hard and headwinds were strong; once inside a stratum, early advantages and origin continued to shape further rise. It does not reach the 70–79 or higher bands because legitimate (if constrained) pathways out of lower origins existed and were used. Soft statistical correlation alone is insufficient for high scores; the institutional origin and reliability gates supply the additional weight that places Stalinism in the upper-moderate range.

### Why this produces a Final Score of 68
Origin-conditioned gates on education and advancement, combined with real but constrained formal mobility channels, align with the 60–69 band (inter-class possible but hard; intra-class still limited by early advantages). Score 68.
