# Cultural 2B: Communism – Stalinism
## National Pride Analysis
**Score: 50**

### Overview
Stalinism combined intense affirmation of the Soviet state and its achievements with selective critical frames toward the pre-revolutionary past and toward “enemy” nations. National (Soviet) pride was real but subordinated to party and ideological criteria. Residual baseline of mixed or conditional national framing: pride and critical perspectives coexist without a single dominant residual direction.

### Relevant Policies / Elements
* Strong affirmative narrative of Soviet achievement, industrialisation and wartime victory.
* Pre-revolutionary Russian history selectively nationalised or criticised according to ideological need.
* Symbols and rituals celebrated the Soviet state and its leadership.
* Critical frames applied to class enemies and to the Tsarist past, not to the Soviet nation as such.
* Residual baseline of mixed or conditional national framing: pride and critical perspectives coexist without a single dominant residual direction.

### Expanded Analysis
Stalinism produces a Balanced score. Soviet national pride was institutionally powerful, yet it was always conditional on ideological conformity and could be redirected against internal “enemies.” The result is neither pure pride orthodoxy nor organising national shame.

### Why this produces a Final Score of 50
Intense but ideologically conditional affirmation of the Soviet nation, combined with selective critical frames, places Stalinism in the Balanced band.
