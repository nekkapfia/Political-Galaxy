# Cultural 5A: Communism – Marxism
## Tradition vs Progress Analysis
**Score: 16**

### Overview
Classical Marxism is a theory of historical transformation that treats existing traditional and bourgeois social forms as historically transient and destined for revolutionary replacement. It does not prescribe preservation of traditional norms as an institutional baseline.

### Cultural 5A Relevant Policies / Elements
* Historical-materialist framing of traditional social forms as stages to be superseded through revolutionary change, not conserved as end-states.
* Critique of religion, family forms and customary authority as expressions of underlying class structures requiring transformation.
* Orientation toward revolutionary modernisation of social relations rather than preservation of inherited baselines in education, family or culture.
* No programme for institutional preservation of pre-socialist traditional norms in family law, schooling or public ritual.
* Cultural and social continuity treated as subordinate to transformative progress in theory and in derivative political practice.
* Residual traditional practice, where acknowledged, appears as ideology or lag to be overcome, not as a protected institutional default.
* Domain hierarchy in theoretical application: family, religion and custom as superstructure destined for transformation.

### Expanded Analysis
Classical Marxism supplies no institutional machinery of its own, but its Cultural 5A content is unambiguous across the domains that matter. Traditional family forms, religious authority and customary legitimacy are theorised as class expressions destined for supersession. There is no theoretical warrant for treating pre-socialist education content, family transmission or confessional authority as baselines the political order exists to protect. The residual baseline implied by the theory is continuous historical transformation: what exists is transitional. Domain hierarchy therefore has no preservationist layer in primary domains—only the expectation that law, education and family relations will be reorganised as class structure is transformed. Because the document is pure theory without a specific enforcement apparatus, the pattern is transformative orientation rather than absolute institutional prohibition, which places it in the upper part of the near-absolute progressive band rather than at the floor of implemented Year Zero regimes.

### Why this produces a Final Score of 16
Transformative orientation across religion, family and custom with no warrant for institutional protection of pre-socialist norms places Marxism in the 11–20 band. Residual traditional forms are lag, not baseline. Score 16 sits above implemented campaign regimes because pure theory lacks enforcement machinery, while remaining clearly transformative on the axis.
