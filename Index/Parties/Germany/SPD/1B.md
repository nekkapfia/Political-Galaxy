# Core 1B: Germany – SPD
## Economic Autonomy Analysis
**Regulation Score: 36**
**Taxation Score: 56**
**Final Score: 40**

### Overview

SPD combines social-democratic tax progressivity (relief for most households, higher taxes on the very rich), minimum-wage and Bürgergeld densification, and retained green/social regulation. Taxation Score sits below pure baseline cut packages; Regulation is denser than Union/FDP under the 100-year test.

### Relevant Policies / Elements

* Income tax relief for most households; higher taxes on the very rich.
* Secure pension level at least 48% long-term; reject pension cuts and raising retirement age from 67.
* Raise national minimum wage toward €15/hour (programme discussion).
* Bürgergeld as path out of unemployment; more Jobcenter counselling funds; collective agreements to reduce pay gaps.
* Free lunch for daycare and elementary children in some formulations.
* Germany OECD tax-to-GDP baseline ~37–38%; GDP ~€4.3–4.5tn.
* Green transition and social-market labour rules retained as Regulation density.

### Expanded Analysis

**Taxation:** Formula `(1 − tax/GDP) × 100`. Progressive redistribution and limited broad-based cuts yield **56**, below AfD/FDP relief packages. **Regulation:** Minimum wage, Bürgergeld machinery, and residual green industrial rules densify non-tax constraints → **36**. Final **40**.

### Why this produces a Final Score of 40

Taxation **56** and Regulation **36** combine to Final **40**.
