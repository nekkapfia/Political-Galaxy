# Core 1A: Germany – SPD
## Personal Autonomy Analysis
**Score: 48**

### Overview

The SPD’s programme produces a mid-range Core 1A profile built from retention of *Volksverhetzung* and related hate-speech law, platform regulation in the NetzDG–DSA lineage, and equality frameworks, without the densest identity-orthodoxy or attitude-correction cultures associated with Die Grünen. Governing pragmatism keeps the party closer to baseline retention plus selective enforcement than to green-left densification. No Endos/Xenos split is applied.

### Core 1A Relevant Policies / Elements

* Retention and defence of criminal hate-speech law (*Volksverhetzung* §130 StGB and related provisions) as part of the democratic legal order; no comprehensive narrowing agenda.
* Support for platform content duties against illegal and clearly harmful material under NetzDG logic and DSA enforcement, including removal obligations and regulatory oversight.
* Equality and anti-discrimination frameworks (AGG and related public-sector duties) retained as baseline public policy; hostile-environment logic remains available in employment and institutional settings.
* Less programme emphasis than Die Grünen on gender self-identification as a speech-and-institutions restructuring project; more cautious institutional posture on secondary speech and single-sex space effects.
* Weaker association with mandatory or quasi-mandatory diversity and attitude-correction cultures than Green-influenced institutions; training and equality frameworks remain present but lighter in intensity and reach.
* Social-cohesion and “respect” framing that supports existing speech limits without maximal identity-orthodoxy language.
* No systematic programme defence of speech critical of migration, Islam or gender-identity orthodoxy against institutional pressure.
* Retention of ordinary direct-harm criminal prohibitions.

### Expanded Analysis

SPD programmes conserve the dense German baseline. *Volksverhetzung*, platform removal duties and AGG-style equality rules remain settled architecture. The party does not drive the extra orthodoxy layer as hard as the Greens: gender self-identification is not elevated as a comprehensive speech-and-spaces restructuring project, and diversity/attitude-training cultures are thinner under SPD institutional influence than under Green participation in government.

Critical speech controls remain real. Institutional equality rules continue to generate secondary pressure in public-sector and employment settings. What is absent relative to the Greens is the fuller package of identity-protective intensification. Those layers simply do not characterise SPD governance in the same way; their absence lightens the final set without any removal-credit logic.

No stable, discretely targetable out-group differential is formalised by SPD policy at the level required for an Endos/Xenos split. Differential enforcement patterns in the wider system are not uniquely amplified by the party into a formal dual structure.

The architecture sits above Die Grünen densification and below any liberalising or repeal-oriented position. Cumulative weight is mid-range — baseline retention with thinner orthodoxy. Closest UK analogue is Labour under Starmer-weighted scoring rather than the Green densification pole.

### Why this produces Endos 48 / Xenos N/A

*Volksverhetzung* retained, platform regulation retained, equality frameworks retained, but without Green-level identity-orthodoxy intensity or strong attitude-correction cultures, place Endos at 48. No split warranted.
