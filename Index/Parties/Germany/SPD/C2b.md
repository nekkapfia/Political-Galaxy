# Cultural 2B: Germany – SPD
## National Pride Analysis
**Score: 27**

### Overview
The SPD occupies a middle-to-low position that accepts the post-1945 guilt framework while occasionally attempting limited expressions of national confidence. Traditional German national identity remains heavily subordinated to historical responsibility. Residual baseline of shame- or guilt-oriented framing: national pride is treated as problematic or secondary; critical and pathologising frames predominate in education and cultural institutions.

### Relevant Policies / Elements
* Strong commitment to Holocaust remembrance and historical atonement.
* Pride almost exclusively limited to the post-1945 democratic and social achievements of the Federal Republic.
* Cultural figures and longer German history treated with caution and rarely claimed as sources of national pride.
* Rhetoric frequently balances any national assertion with references to historical guilt and European responsibility.
* Acceptance and continuation of the post-1945 pattern of near-absence of new traditional national statues.
* Residual baseline of shame- or guilt-oriented framing: national pride is treated as problematic or secondary; critical and pathologising frames predominate in education and cultural institutions.

### Expanded Analysis
The SPD reinforces the dominant culture of self-limitation and the de-emphasis of traditional national identity. When it speaks of pride it almost always means pride in the democratic Federal Republic or in social achievements, not in the longer historical nation. The party has not sought to reverse the de-nationalisation of cultural figures or the commemorative architecture of atonement. Its score therefore sits in the upper Shame / lower Guilt range.

### Why this produces a Final Score of 27
Acceptance of the guilt framework as baseline, confinement of pride to the post-1945 construct, and continued de-emphasis of traditional national identity place the SPD in the Shame band.
