# Cultural 5A: Germany – BSW
## Tradition vs Progress Analysis
**Score: 47**

### Overview
BSW critiques gender ideology and identity-political frameworks in schools and public institutions while supporting conventional family structures without a full Leitkultur restoration programme.

### Cultural 5A Relevant Policies / Elements
* Critique of gender ideology in schools and of identity-political frameworks in public institutions.
* Opposition to open progressive cultural transformation while retaining left-economic priorities.
* Migration and integration framed through social cohesion and interests of the established population.
* Support for conventional family structures in platform language without a full Leitkultur or positive-history curriculum restoration programme.
* Residual progressive settlement on many education and status frameworks is partially contested but not systematically dismantled.
* Continuity pressure is selective (gender/identity in schools, family language) rather than comprehensive redesign of national-narrative curriculum and institutional transmission.

### Expanded Analysis
BSW occupies contested low-mid ground. It pushes back on gender ideology and identity frameworks in education and public institutions and supports conventional family language, but it does not advance a full AfD-style Leitkultur and positive-history baseline for curriculum and citizenship. The residual progressive settlement remains largely in place on national-narrative framing and broader institutional culture; BSW contests specific progressive orthodoxy without reconstructing traditional transmission as the comprehensive public default. Domain hierarchy shows selective pressure in education content (gender/identity) and family rhetoric, without systematic restoration of national-continuity curriculum as the organising frame.

### Why this produces a Final Score of 47
Critique of gender ideology and identity frameworks plus conventional family support, without full Leitkultur restoration or systematic curriculum reversal, place BSW in the contested low-mid band (41–50). Higher mid-high placement would require traditional national and familial transmission to be institutionalised as the baseline across primary domains; the locked analysis shows selective contestation inside a largely progressive residual settlement.
