## Regulation

### Overview
BSW combines left-economic expansion of restrictions (higher minimum wage, stronger collective bargaining, state intervention against market-dominating firms) with selective rollback of specific green-mandate instruments (opposition to the combustion-engine ban and heating law as over-reach), while retaining the broader residual labour and most residual green-industrial architecture.

### Relevant Policies / Elements
* Higher minimum wage (programme target around €15) and stronger Tarifbindung.
* State intervention against market-dominating companies; anti-monopoly and break-up language for large digital and corporate groups.
* Bürgerversicherung direction and expanded social-insurance claim.
* Selective rollback: opposition to Verbrenner ban and Heizungsgesetz; lower energy prices via policy change.
* Residual Kündigungsschutz, co-determination and standard competition baseline retained and in places expanded.
* Residual green architecture partly challenged on instrument design but not fully repealed as a system.

### Expanded Analysis
BSW expands ordinary labour and social-insurance layers and expands state intervention against concentrated private power. Selective opposition to specific technology bans and heating mandates lightens parts of the residual green layer without removing the broader Energiewende and industrial-steering residual. Residual labour protections continue and are reinforced. The institutional pattern is redistributive and interventionist expansion of restrictions on labour and corporate structure combined with partial instrument-level resistance to green mandates, leaving most residual green and labour baselines in place.

### Regulation Score
Labour and intervention expansion of restrictions with only selective green-mandate rollback produces a Regulation Score of 38.

## Taxation

Primary method applies. Net package effect is higher aggregate claim through wealth, high-income and capital-income expansion of restrictions, only partly offset by low-earner relief and selective energy-tax measures.

* Higher burden on high incomes, large wealth and capital income at ordinary rates; wealth-tax reactivation; financial-transaction tax.
* Relief for low and normal earners; tax-free pensions up to stated thresholds.
* Equalisation of international group taxation versus Mittelstand.
* Order-of-magnitude net increase in effective claim relative to baseline estimated at several points of GDP.
* Baseline tax-to-GDP ~38%; post-package effective rate estimated ~52%.

**Taxation Score** = (1 − 0.52) × 100 = 48.0 → **48**

**Taxation Score: 48**
