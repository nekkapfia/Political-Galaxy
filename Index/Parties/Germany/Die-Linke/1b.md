Germany - Die Linke - Core 1B
Final Score: 37

## Regulation

### Overview
Die Linke combines accelerated green industrial transformation with extensive state direction, large-scale public investment, and a clear preference for public ownership of key infrastructure and strategic sectors. Markets continue to operate in many areas, but the state takes a leading role in deciding investment priorities, ownership structures and labour conditions.

### Relevant Policies
- Large public investment programmes (scale of €120–200 billion per year range) directed at climate-neutral industrial conversion, infrastructure and social services
- Public ownership or rekommunalisierung of energy networks, key utilities and strategic industrial assets; support for companies in exchange for society shares
- Acceleration of renewable expansion, coal phase-out and climate-neutrality timelines
- Strengthening of labour protections, co-determination and collective bargaining coverage
- Social energy pricing (sockel tariffs), bans on electricity and gas cut-offs, and related price-steering measures
- Maintain and expand climate-mandate and industrial-steering frameworks
- Maintain ordinary Kündigungsschutz while seeking to expand its scope and co-determination rights

### Expanded Analysis
Under a Die Linke platform the regulatory environment would feature dense green industrial mandates together with a substantial increase in direct state ownership and directed investment. Firms in strategic sectors would face both climate-related compliance obligations and a higher probability of public equity stakes or public-control conditions attached to support. Energy networks and core utilities would move toward public ownership. Labour-market rules would be tightened. The combination produces a high degree of state direction over production, investment and labour allocation while market mechanisms continue to operate in non-strategic areas.

The package retains ordinary labour protections and residual planning rules, but these are secondary to the dominant mechanical features of public ownership, large-scale directed investment and intensified climate-industrial steering.

### Final Score
The decisive mechanical effects are the combination of public ownership of key infrastructure, large directed investment programmes, intensified climate-industrial mandates and stronger labour regulation. This produces a Regulation Score of 28.

## Taxation

These proposals are scored via the separate Taxation Score method (not included in the Regulation Score above).

- Reintroduction of a wealth tax on high private assets: ~€15–30 billion gain
- Higher corporate taxes and excess-profit / windfall taxes on energy and large corporations: ~€10–20 billion gain
- Higher top income-tax rates and reform of inheritance taxation on large estates: ~€8–15 billion gain
- Income-tax and contribution relief for lower and middle earners: ~€8–15 billion cost
- Social energy-price measures and related fiscal transfers

**Overall estimated net fiscal effect of the tax package**: ~€20–40 billion net gain per year  
**Current GDP reference**: ≈ €4.6 trillion  
**Taxation Score: 52**
