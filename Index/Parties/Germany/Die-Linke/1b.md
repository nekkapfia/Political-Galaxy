## Regulation

### Overview
Die Linke produces a lower Core 1B Regulation profile defined by strong residual and expanded labour protections, residual green architecture retained and intensified, preference for public ownership and industrial planning in strategic sectors, and expanded public investment direction.

### Relevant Policies / Elements
* Strong residual and expanded labour protections (Kündigungsschutz, co-determination, collective bargaining) with preference for further expansion of collective rights.
* Retention and intensification of residual climate targets and residual green industrial policy.
* Preference for public ownership and industrial planning in strategic sectors and utilities.
* Expanded public investment and planning tools directing capital and production.
* Residual competition-law baseline continues but is subordinated to public-ownership and industrial-policy priorities.
* Residual secondary regulatory baseline continues and is deepened; no high-priority residual green-architecture repeal or residual labour-market deregulation is locked.

### Expanded Analysis
The Die Linke final set retains and expands residual Kündigungsschutz, residual co-determination and residual collective-bargaining institutions. Residual climate and green-industrial architecture is intensified. Public ownership and industrial-planning preferences in strategic sectors reduce the scope of private voluntary decision-making. Expanded public investment tools direct capital and production. Residual competition rules remain but are secondary to ownership and planning priorities. The institutional pattern is residual continuity of labour and green restrictions combined with substantial additional public-ownership and industrial-planning direction.

### Regulation Score
Strong residual and expanded labour protections, intensification of residual climate and green industrial policy, preference for public ownership and industrial planning, and expanded public investment direction produce a Regulation Score of 28.
