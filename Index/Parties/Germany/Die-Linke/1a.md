# Core 1A: Germany – Die Linke
## Personal Autonomy Analysis
**Score: 32**

### Overview

Die Linke’s programme produces a restrictive Core 1A profile built from strong anti-racism and anti-discrimination commitments, defence of hate-speech enforcement against right-wing and racist expression, support for platform regulation, full alignment with LGBTIQA* and self-identification agendas, and identity-protective framing of migration-related speech. Limited governing reach constrains nationwide administrative embedding, but the normative direction is clearly densifying. Heightened risk on identity-critical speech is content-triggered, not a stable group dual structure; no Endos/Xenos split applies.

### Core 1A Relevant Policies / Elements

* Strong programmatic defence of criminal hate-speech law and its use against racist, right-wing extremist and related group-directed expression; no narrowing agenda.
* Support for platform regulation against illegal and hateful content in the NetzDG–DSA framework.
* Explicit opposition to racism, antimuslim racism, antisemitism, homophobia and related forms of group-directed hostility as core programme commitments.
* Full support for LGBTIQA* rights including self-identification positions; opposition to “rolling back” queer rights (explicit contrast with BSW).
* Migration-related speech heavily framed through anti-racism and equal rights independent of origin; critical speech on migration/Islam faces elevated normative and mobilisation pressure.
* Anti-discrimination and minority-protection frameworks treated as organising principles; institutional pressure where the party has local or cultural influence.
* Limited state capacity for nationwide mandatory attitude-correction systems, but clear normative support for diversity and anti-discrimination institutional cultures.
* Retention of ordinary direct-harm criminal prohibitions.

### Expanded Analysis

Die Linke is identity-protective and anti-discrimination maximalist on paper. Programmes elevate anti-racism (including antimuslim racism), queer rights and minority protection as non-negotiable. Hate-speech enforcement and platform duties against right-wing and racist expression are affirmed. Gender self-identification and related institutional agendas are supported; the party positions itself against any rollback associated with BSW.

Limited governing reach means fewer embedded nationwide training empires than under parties with sustained federal executive control. Densification therefore runs more through norms, mobilisation and local institutional practice than through administrative systems. That still leaves a dense final set: criminal/regulatory baseline plus strong identity-protective speech politics.

Speech critical of specific protected identity categories attracts heightened political and mobilisation-backed scrutiny. That differential is content-triggered: anyone who utters the covered criticism faces elevated risk. There is no stable, closed out-group under a separate legal dual structure. Under stability and non-overlap requirements the split is invalid.

### Why this produces a Score of 32

Hate-speech defence, platform regulation, strong anti-racism and queer/self-ID commitments, and identity-protective framing of migration speech place the regime inside **28–36** at **32**. No Endos/Xenos split. Framing is discarded. Only observable restrictions are scored against band text.
