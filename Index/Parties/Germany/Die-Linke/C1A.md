# Cultural 1A: Die-Linke
## Foundation Analysis
Final Score: 0 (50M)

### Overview
Die Linke organises moral legitimacy around working-class interest, social protection, systemic critique of economic arrangements, and electoral competition. These are secular this-worldly claims.

### Moral Justification – Relevant Elements

#### Mundane Justifications
* Working-class and social-protection claims are framed as primary moral requirements.
* Systemic injustice and structural illegitimacy of current economic arrangements are presented as requiring transformative change within a secular frame.
* Electoral competition and popular mobilisation are treated as legitimate means.
* Public ownership and redistribution are presented as necessary corrections.
* Concrete harms to living standards and social security are cited as evidence.
* Anti-militarism and critique of capitalist globalisation are advanced as political and moral claims.
* Socialist and justice language is used to critique the existing order and to identify the party’s political lineage.

### Expanded Analysis
Working-class interest, social protection, systemic critique, electoral means, and concrete harms organise the moral conversation. These are secular this-worldly claims. No divine command, Cosmic or Natural law, worship-level deification, or sacralised Ideal organises legitimacy. Transcendent sources are entirely absent from the moral justifications. The foundation is Mundane.
