Germany - CDU/CSU - Core 1B
Final Score: 53
Regulation score: 47, Taxation score: 62

## Regulation

### Overview
CDU/CSU pursues moderate deregulation focused on bureaucracy reduction, abolition of the national Supply Chain Act, and a more technology-open, pragmatic approach to energy and climate policy. It retains the overall climate framework and emissions trading while removing the most rigid technology mandates and easing compliance burdens for business.

### Relevant Policies
- Abolition of the national Lieferkettensorgfaltspflichtengesetz
- Significant bureaucracy reduction (target 25 % cut in bureaucratic costs for business, sunset/review of reporting obligations)
- Withdrawal or major softening of the Gebäudeenergiegesetz (heating law) in favour of technology-open solutions
- Technology-open energy policy: continued renewables expansion alongside openness to nuclear research, gas capacity and pragmatic industrial energy pricing
- Maintain ordinary Kündigungsschutz and co-determination
- Maintain core emissions-trading framework and basic non-Net-Zero environmental standards
- Maintain standard competition rules and residual planning constraints

### Expanded Analysis
Under a CDU/CSU platform the densest layers of national supply-chain and heating mandates would be removed and general bureaucratic reporting obligations reduced, lowering compliance costs especially for mid-sized firms. Energy policy would shift toward technology openness and affordability while remaining inside the broader climate and emissions-trading architecture. The party does not propose the comprehensive dismantling of green industrial policy seen in AfD platforms, nor the stronger labour-market tightening associated with BSW or the left.

The package leaves in place ordinary labour-market protections (Kündigungsschutz and co-determination), standard competition rules, residual planning constraints, and the core emissions-trading system. These residual elements, together with continued climate-framework commitments, remain in force.

### Final Score
The decisive mechanical effects are the abolition of the national Supply Chain Act, bureaucracy reduction, and the softening of rigid technology mandates in heating and energy. These produce a clear but limited reduction in directed regulation relative to the current baseline. Retention of the emissions-trading framework, ordinary labour protections and residual planning rules limits how far the overall environment moves. This produces a Regulation Score of 47.

## Taxation

These proposals are scored via the separate Taxation Score method (not included in the Regulation Score above).

- Corporate tax reductions toward a maximum effective rate around 25 % and improved depreciation/loss offsetting: ~€12–20 billion cost
- Full or phased abolition of the Solidaritätszuschlag: ~€10–13 billion cost
- Income-tax relief (higher basic allowance, flattening of progression, family-related reliefs): ~€8–12 billion cost
- Energy and electricity tax / levy reductions (Stromsteuer to EU minimum, grid-fee relief, industrial electricity price): ~€8–12 billion cost
- Gastronomy VAT reduction and other targeted reliefs: ~€2–4 billion cost

**Overall estimated net fiscal cost of the tax package**: ~€35–55 billion per year  
**Current GDP reference**: ≈ €4.6 trillion  
**Taxation Score: 62**
