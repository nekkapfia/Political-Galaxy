## Regulation

### Overview
CDU/CSU produces a mid-range Core 1B Regulation profile defined by moderate deregulation focused on bureaucracy reduction, abolition of the national Supply Chain Act, and a more technology-open approach to energy and climate policy, while retaining residual Kündigungsschutz, residual co-determination, residual climate framework and residual emissions-trading architecture.

### Relevant Policies / Elements
* Abolition of the national Lieferkettensorgfaltspflichtengesetz (Supply Chain Act).
* Significant bureaucracy reduction (target cut in bureaucratic costs for business, sunset/review of reporting obligations).
* Withdrawal or major softening of the Gebäudeenergiegesetz (heating law) in favour of technology-open solutions.
* Technology-open energy policy: continued renewables expansion alongside openness to nuclear research, gas capacity and pragmatic industrial energy pricing.
* Retention of residual ordinary Kündigungsschutz and residual co-determination; no high-priority full labour-market deregulation is locked.
* Retention of residual core emissions-trading framework and residual climate commitments; no high-priority full Energiewende dismantling is locked.
* Residual competition-law baseline and residual planning constraints continue.

### Expanded Analysis
The CDU/CSU final set removes the residual national Supply Chain Act and reduces residual bureaucratic reporting obligations. Rigid technology mandates in heating are softened in favour of technology-open solutions. Energy policy shifts toward technology openness and affordability while remaining inside the residual climate and emissions-trading architecture. Residual Kündigungsschutz and residual co-determination continue. Residual competition rules and residual planning constraints remain. The institutional pattern is moderate reduction of residual mandate and compliance layers combined with continuity of residual labour protections and residual climate-framework commitments.

### Regulation Score
Abolition of the residual Supply Chain Act, bureaucracy reduction, and softening of rigid technology mandates, while retaining residual Kündigungsschutz, residual co-determination, residual emissions-trading framework and residual climate commitments, produce a Regulation Score of 47.
