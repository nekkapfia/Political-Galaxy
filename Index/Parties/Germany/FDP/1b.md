## Regulation

### Overview
FDP prioritises radical bureaucracy reduction, technology openness and a shift from detailed mandates toward market-based instruments (primarily emissions trading). It seeks to abolish the national Supply Chain Act, reverse rigid heating rules, and restore greater voluntary decision-making in energy and industrial investment while retaining a basic climate framework.

### Relevant Policies
- Immediate multi-year bureaucracy moratorium, “one-in-two-out” rule, and constitutional bureaucracy brake
- Full abolition of the national Lieferkettensorgfaltspflichtengesetz and opposition to equivalent EU burdens
- Withdrawal or major softening of the Gebäudeenergiegesetz in favour of technology-open solutions
- Technology-open energy policy: market integration of renewables, openness to nuclear research and new reactor generations, rejection of detailed technology bans
- Preference for emissions trading as the primary climate instrument over sectoral mandates and industrial steering
- Labour-market flexibilisation (working-time rules, reduction of selected employment reporting and Tariftreue requirements)
- Maintain ordinary Kündigungsschutz and co-determination in their core form
- Maintain standard competition rules and residual planning constraints

### Expanded Analysis
Under an FDP platform the densest layers of national supply-chain, heating and reporting mandates would be removed and a structural brake on new bureaucracy introduced. Energy and industrial policy would shift toward technology openness and price-based instruments rather than binding expansion paths or ownership conditions. Labour-market rules would be modestly liberalised. The overall regulatory environment would feature substantially lower compliance costs and greater voluntary choice in production and investment decisions while remaining inside a residual climate and labour framework.

The package leaves in place ordinary labour-market protections, standard competition rules and residual planning constraints. These residual elements, together with continued emissions-trading commitments, keep the package below a fully light-touch market order but produce a clear upward movement relative to the current baseline and to the more interventionist parties.

### Final Score
The decisive mechanical effects are structural bureaucracy reduction, abolition of the Supply Chain Act, technology-open reversal of heating mandates, and a shift toward emissions trading over detailed industrial steering. This produces a Regulation Score of 51.
