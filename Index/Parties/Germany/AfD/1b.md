## Economic Autonomy Analysis
**Final Score: 58**

## Regulation

### Overview
AfD proposes a comprehensive deregulatory package. It centres on ending the Energiewende architecture, abolishing green mandates and industrial steering, dismantling extensive reporting and supply-chain obligations, and restoring technology-open energy choices, while retaining ordinary labour protections, competition rules and basic non-Net-Zero environmental standards.

### Relevant Policies / Elements
* Full repeal of the EEG, Klimaschutzgesetz, Windenergieflächenbedarfsgesetz and related renewable priority and expansion mandates.
* Abolition of the national CO₂ pricing regime (BEHG) and associated behavioural mandates.
* Repeal of the Lieferkettensorgfaltspflichtengesetz and major reduction of EU-derived sustainability reporting (CSRD, taxonomy).
* Cancellation of the Gebäudeenergiegesetz (heating law) and the combustion-engine phase-out.
* Significant bureaucracy reduction (target cuts in bureaucratic costs, sunset clauses, one-in-two-out principle).
* Restart of nuclear power, extension of coal capacity, and removal of technology bans in energy and industry.
* Maintain ordinary Kündigungsschutz and co-determination rules.
* Maintain standard competition law and basic non-Net-Zero environmental standards.

### Expanded Analysis
Under an AfD platform the German regulatory environment would shift from a system heavily shaped by climate targets, technology mandates and extensive compliance layers toward a more market-centred, technology-neutral regime. Firms would face substantially fewer directed investment requirements, reporting obligations and phase-out deadlines. Energy pricing and generation choices would be returned largely to market and engineering criteria rather than statutory expansion paths. Bureaucracy reduction and abolition of the Supply Chain Act would lower ongoing compliance costs, particularly for mid-sized manufacturers. The package leaves in place ordinary labour-market protection, standard competition rules, and basic environmental standards not tied to Net Zero or industrial-steering mandates. These residual constraints remain, while the dominant mechanical change is removal of the dense green-industrial and mandate layer that characterises the German baseline.

### Regulation Score
Comprehensive removal of green mandates, industrial steering and associated compliance regimes, with retention of ordinary labour protections, competition rules and non-Net-Zero environmental standards, produces a Regulation Score of 53.

## Taxation

Primary method applies.

* Major reduction or abolition of green levies: ~€15–25 billion cost
* Corporate tax reductions: ~€12–20 billion cost
* Income-tax relief and Solidaritätszuschlag abolition: ~€20–30 billion cost
* Inheritance/wealth tax reductions: ~€2–5 billion cost
* Overall estimated net cost: ~€50–80 billion (midpoint €55 billion)
* Baseline tax revenue: €1,710 billion (38.0% × €4,500 billion)
* Tax revenue after package: €1,655 billion
* Current GDP: €4,500 billion

**Taxation Score** = (1 − 1655 / 4500) × 100 = 63.22 → **64**

**Taxation Score: 64**

## Final Score

A = (53 + 64) / 2 = 58.5
L = min(53, 64) = 53
d = |53 − 64| / 100 = 0.11
F = (1 − 0.11) × 58.5 + 0.11 × 53 = 52.065 + 5.83 = 57.895 → **58**

**Final Score: 58**

### Why this produces a Final Score of 58
Regulation at 53 reflects comprehensive removal of residual green-industrial and mandate layers with retention of ordinary labour and competition baselines. Taxation at 64 is the pure algebraic result of the stated package cost against baseline tax-to-GDP under the Primary Method with strict ceiling. The A/L/d combination produces Final Score 58.
