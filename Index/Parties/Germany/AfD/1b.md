## Regulation

### Overview
AfD proposes the strongest deregulatory package among major German parties. It centres on ending the Energiewende architecture, abolishing green mandates and industrial steering, dismantling extensive reporting and supply-chain obligations, and restoring technology-open energy choices (nuclear, coal, conventional gas).

### Relevant Policies
- Full repeal of the EEG, Klimaschutzgesetz, Windenergieflächenbedarfsgesetz and related renewable priority and expansion mandates
- Abolition of the national CO₂ pricing regime (BEHG) and associated behavioural mandates
- Repeal of the Lieferkettensorgfaltspflichtengesetz and major reduction of EU-derived sustainability reporting (CSRD, taxonomy)
- Cancellation of the Gebäudeenergiegesetz (heating law) and the combustion-engine phase-out
- Significant bureaucracy reduction (target 25 % cut in bureaucratic costs by 2030, sunset clauses on new laws, “one-in-two-out” principle)
- Restart of nuclear power, extension of coal capacity, and removal of technology bans in energy and industry
- Broad reduction of prior restraints on investment, production and land-use decisions that currently flow from green industrial policy
- Maintain ordinary Kündigungsschutz and co-determination rules
- Maintain standard competition law and basic non-Net-Zero environmental standards

### Expanded Analysis
Under an AfD platform the German regulatory environment would shift from a system heavily shaped by climate targets, technology mandates and extensive compliance layers toward a more market-centred, technology-neutral regime. Firms would face substantially fewer directed investment requirements, reporting obligations and phase-out deadlines. Energy pricing and generation choices would be returned largely to market and engineering criteria rather than statutory expansion paths. Bureaucracy reduction and the abolition of the Supply Chain Act would lower ongoing compliance costs, particularly for mid-sized manufacturers.

The package leaves in place the core elements of ordinary labour-market protection (Kündigungsschutz and co-determination), standard competition rules, and basic environmental standards that are not tied to Net Zero or industrial-steering mandates. These residual constraints remain, while the dominant mechanical change is the removal of the dense green-industrial and mandate layer that currently characterises the German baseline.

### Final Score
The decisive mechanical effects are the comprehensive removal of green mandates, industrial steering and associated compliance regimes. The breadth of deregulation in energy, industrial policy, reporting and technology bans produces a clear reduction in state direction relative to the current baseline. Retention of ordinary labour protections, competition rules and non-Net-Zero environmental standards limits how far the overall environment moves. This produces a Regulation Score of 53.

## Taxation

These proposals are scored via the separate Taxation Score method (not included in the Regulation Score above).

- Major reduction or abolition of green levies (EEG-related charges, national CO₂ tax and related environmental levies): ~€15–25 billion revenue loss (partially offset by ending subsidy outflows)
- Corporate tax reductions and simplification toward internationally competitive rates: ~€12–20 billion cost
- Income-tax relief (higher basic allowance ≈ €15 000, rate reductions, full abolition of the Solidaritätszuschlag): ~€20–30 billion cost
- Abolition or major reduction of inheritance tax and selected property-related taxes: ~€8–15 billion cost
- Energy and electricity taxes reduced to EU minimum levels: ~€5–10 billion cost
- Additional reliefs (gastronomy VAT, etc.): ~€3–6 billion cost

**Overall estimated net fiscal cost of the tax package**: ~€50–85 billion per year  
**Current GDP reference**: ≈ €4.6 trillion  
**Taxation Score: 68**
