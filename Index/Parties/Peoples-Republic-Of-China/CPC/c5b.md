# Cultural 5B: Peoples Republic of China – CPC
## Radicalism (Pace of Change) Analysis
**Score: 68**

### Overview

The Communist Party of China retains campaign-style mobilisation and compressed political-regulatory campaigns as a normal tool of governance under Xi Jinping. Anti-corruption drives, common-prosperity measures, zero-COVID style mobilisations and rapid regulatory interventions demonstrate continued capacity and preference for accelerated implementation when the centre prioritises a goal. The score sits in the mid-high band.

### Observable Pace and Intensity

* Recurring political and regulatory campaigns executed on compressed timelines with high mobilisation intensity (anti-corruption, tech-sector rectification, common prosperity, zero-COVID local implementation).
* Central directives that leave limited room for local deliberative delay once a campaign is launched.
* Education, propaganda and organisational systems that frame rapid compliance with central tempo as a core virtue.
* Simultaneous capacity for longer-term planning and stabilisation; the system is not in permanent Year-Zero mode.
* Observable pattern is therefore higher-tempo than consensus democracies while remaining below the continuous extreme radicalism of peak Maoism or the Khmer Rouge.

### Expanded Analysis

The CPC under Xi has re-emphasised campaign methods that compress implementation and override gradualist local or sectoral resistance. That keeps the score well above gradualist systems and in the mid-high range (68). It does not reach the extreme continuous-revolution scores of earlier Maoist peaks.

### Why this produces a Final Score of 68

Continued use of compressed political and regulatory campaigns as a normal governing tool, with low tolerance for deliberative delay once the centre moves, places the CPC at 68.
