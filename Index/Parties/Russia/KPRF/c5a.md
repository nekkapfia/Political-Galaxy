# Cultural 5A: Russia – KPRF
## Tradition vs Progress Analysis
**Score: 44**

### Overview
KPRF defends Soviet-era collective cultural forms and Russian historical continuity against Western progressive models without restoring pre-Soviet religious or monarchical baselines.

### Cultural 5A Relevant Policies / Elements
* Defence of Soviet-era collective cultural forms and Russian historical continuity against liberal-progressive Western models.
* More conservative positions on family and religion than Western left parties.
* No programme to restore pre-Soviet religious or monarchical baselines as autonomous institutional orders.
* Selective continuity with Soviet and national inheritance without full traditionalist restoration of what the revolution dismantled.
* Residual Soviet cultural and institutional memory is defended; Western progressive gender and cultural agendas are rejected; pre-revolutionary traditional autonomy is not reconstituted.
* Education and cultural positions combine Soviet-historical continuity with resistance to progressive Western frameworks.

### Expanded Analysis
KPRF’s pattern is selective continuity. It defends Soviet-era collective cultural forms and Russian historical continuity against Western progressive models and is more conservative on family and religion than Western left parties. It does not restore pre-Soviet religious or monarchical baselines as autonomous orders. Domain hierarchy shows continuity pressure against progressive Western frameworks in education and cultural narrative, with family positions more conventional than Western left analogues, but the residual baseline is Soviet-national rather than pre-revolutionary traditional. The gap between Soviet-era continuity and pre-Soviet traditional restoration keeps the profile contested rather than mid-high preservation of the long inherited baseline the revolution itself disrupted.

### Why this produces a Final Score of 44
Defence of Soviet-era cultural forms and Russian historical continuity against Western progressive models, with more conservative family/religion positions than Western left parties but without restoring pre-Soviet traditional baselines, places KPRF in the contested low-mid band (41–50). Higher placement would require reconstitution of pre-revolutionary traditional transmission as autonomous institutional baseline.
