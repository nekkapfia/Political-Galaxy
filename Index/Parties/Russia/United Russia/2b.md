# Core 2B: Russia – United Russia
## International Sovereignty Analysis
**Score: 18**

### Overview
United Russia operates as the dominant party of power inside a state whose foreign-policy practice includes sustained military intervention (Georgia 2008, Ukraine 2014–present, Syria), sphere-of-influence enforcement, and systematic pressure on neighbouring internal orders. The party’s residual baseline is alignment with, rather than restraint of, these instruments.

### Relevant Policies / Elements
* Consistent parliamentary and organisational support for major Russian military interventions and the political frameworks that justify them (Georgia, Crimea/Donbas, full-scale Ukraine war, Syria).
* Endorsement of sphere-of-influence doctrine treating post-Soviet and near-abroad internal arrangements as legitimate objects of Russian security management.
* Support for legal, economic and informational instruments that pressure neighbouring states’ internal political space.
* Residual baseline of great-power interventionist posture; the party does not function as an internal brake on external interference.
* Self-defence of Russian territory is out of scope for Core 2B; actions that restructure neighbouring internal orders are not.

### Expanded Analysis
Core 2B measures interference in other nations’ internal affairs. United Russia is the dominant vehicle of the political system that has conducted multi-year combat operations, territorial revision, and sustained political pressure beyond Russia’s borders. The party’s role is to legitimise and sustain these policies rather than to constrain them. Residual non-interference language is absent from the operative posture; the pattern is one of the lowest in the contemporary set.

### Why this produces a Final Score of 18
Sustained support for military interventions that restructure neighbouring internal orders, sphere-of-influence enforcement, and the absence of any residual restraint posture place United Russia near the floor of the real-world range.
