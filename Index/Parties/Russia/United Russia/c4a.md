# Cultural 4A: Russia – United Russia
## Social Determinism Analysis
**Score: 40**

### Overview
United Russia operates as the dominant party inside the post-Soviet Russian system, which retains residual origin, network, and state-mediated opportunity structures. Formal access to education is largely open; practical advancement is shaped by family resources, connections, and political-administrative filters more heavily than in Western liberal-democratic systems. The party’s governing role reinforces the existing filtered mobility architecture.

### Relevant Policies / Elements
* Governance of a system that retains residual origin and network advantages in education and occupation.
* State-mediated opportunity structures and administrative filters on higher advancement.
* Formal educational access that remains relatively open compared with high-determinism regimes.
* No comprehensive formal hereditary classification system of the songbun type.
* Residual political reliability and connection-based gates on elite pathways.

### Expanded Analysis
Cultural 4A scores birth-constraint versus mobility. Post-Soviet Russia under United Russia combines formal educational openness with stronger residual network, origin, and administrative filters than Western systems. Formal access remains usable; practical ease is more heavily patterned by family resources, connections, and political reliability. This places the score at the boundary of the 31–40 and 41–50 bands. Soft statistical correlation alone would score lower; residual institutional filters and the party’s governing reinforcement of those filters supply the additional weight. Cultural 4B equity diagnostics are out of scope.

### Why this produces a Final Score of 40
Formal educational openness plus residual origin/network and administrative filters reinforced by governing practice, without comprehensive hereditary assignment, aligns with the upper 31–40 / lower 41–50 boundary. Score 40.
