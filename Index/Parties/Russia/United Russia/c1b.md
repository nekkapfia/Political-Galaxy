# Cultural 1B: Russia – United Russia
## Dogmatism Analysis
**Score: 87**

**Orthodoxy: 88, Tribalism: 86**

### Overview
United Russia operates inside a high-orthodoxy state-managed political field with limited legitimate internal challenge to foundational state-patriotic positions, and rejects liberal-Western frameworks as hostile. Both aspects are very high, producing a Strong-band score.

### Relevant Policies / Elements
* Dominant-party discipline aligned with presidential vertical.
* State-patriotic and sovereignty positions as closed public orthodoxy.
* Legal and administrative mechanisms constraining organised liberal opposition.
* Foreign-agent and extremism categories limiting external-linked frameworks.
* Education and media alignment with official historical and patriotic narrative.
* Residual baseline of managed-system closure that leaves limited residual space for open challenge to foundational positions.

### Expanded Analysis
Orthodoxy is very high within the managed political system: dominant-party discipline aligned with the presidential vertical treats state-patriotic and sovereignty positions as closed public orthodoxy. Legal and administrative mechanisms constrain organised liberal opposition; education and media align with official historical and patriotic narrative. Tribalism is very high: Western liberal frameworks are treated as geopolitical and moral threats rather than legitimate alternative systems for Russia. Foreign-agent and related categories limit external-linked moral-political frameworks. Mechanisms operate at state scale without full one-party constitutional monopoly of the Soviet type, but effective closure remains very high. The institutional pattern is very high dogmatism, calibrated in the Strong (86–95) band.

### Why this produces a Final Score of 87
Dominant-party discipline, closed state-patriotic and sovereignty orthodoxy, legal/administrative constraints on liberal opposition, foreign-agent categories, and media/education alignment produce very high Orthodoxy and Tribalism. The formula yields 87.
