# Core 2A: Russia – LDPR
## National Sovereignty Analysis
**Score: 79**
**Status: FULLY LOCKED**

### Overview
The LDPR maximises Russian national independence, rejects Western institutional constraints and prioritises unilateral control over foreign policy, defence and strategic resources. Residual external institutional limitation is treated as illegitimate; residual constraints that continue are ordinary and non-transferring.

### National Sovereignty Relevant Policies / Elements
* Strong nationalist emphasis on Russian territorial integrity and historical claims.
* Rejection of Western institutional constraints and NATO expansion; residual Western institutional reach is to be eliminated where possible.
* Supports maximal national control over foreign policy, defence and strategic resources.
* Favours unilateral or bilateral action over multilateral constraint.
* Treats external criticism of domestic governance as illegitimate interference.

### Expanded Analysis
The LDPR’s foreign and domestic posture is built around the premise that residual external institutional constraint on Russian decision-making is illegitimate. Western-led organisations are treated as adversarial arenas rather than sources of residual binding authority; foreign policy, defence and control of strategic resources are reserved as unilateral residual national prerogatives; external criticism of internal governance is dismissed as interference. Residual regional multipolar projects are instruments of Russian influence, not residual constraints upon it. On Core 2A the pattern is near-maximal formal and practical independence from residual external institutional limitation. Residual constraints that continue are limited to ordinary non-transferring forms.

### Why this produces a Final Score of 79
Maximisation of residual independence from residual external institutional constraint and unilateral residual control of foreign policy, defence and strategic resources produce a score of 79 through near-complete retention of residual sovereign authority. Residual architecture that continues does not transfer core residual law-making or territorial control.
