# Core 2B: Russia – LDPR
## International Sovereignty Analysis
**Score: 24**

### Overview
LDPR’s platform and rhetoric emphasise Russian great-power status, sphere-of-influence claims, and readiness to use force or pressure to protect Russian interests and co-ethnics abroad. Within the managed Russian political field it functions as a nationalist amplifier of external-interference postures rather than a restraint force. Residual non-interventionist language is minimal.

### Relevant Policies / Elements
* Explicit support for Russian sphere-of-influence claims and protection of Russian-speaking populations beyond Russia’s borders.
* Rhetoric and platform positions favouring the use of military, economic and political pressure to reshape neighbouring internal arrangements when Russian interests are at stake.
* Alignment with, and amplification of, state postures that treat post-Soviet space as a legitimate zone of privileged interest.
* Limited independent control of the actual instruments of Russian foreign policy; score reflects platform aims and consistent rhetorical support for interventionist options.
* Residual baseline of great-power entitlement rather than restraint.
* Self-defence of Russian territory is out of scope for Core 2B.

### Expanded Analysis
Core 2B measures interference in other states’ internal affairs. LDPR does not design the full architecture of Russian external policy, but its platform and consistent public positions treat sphere-of-influence management, protection of co-ethnics, and the use of force or pressure as legitimate and desirable. Within the managed system it functions as a nationalist pressure for more rather than less external interference. The residual posture is therefore low-scoring.

### Why this produces a Final Score of 24
Platform endorsement of sphere-of-influence claims, readiness to use pressure or force to protect external Russian interests, and amplification of interventionist state postures place LDPR in the low-to-mid 20s.
