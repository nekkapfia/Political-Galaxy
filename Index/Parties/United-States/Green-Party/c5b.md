# Cultural 5B: United States – Green Party
## Radicalism (Pace of Change) Analysis
**Score: 42**

### Overview

The US Green Party pairs strong urgency language on climate, ecology, and systemic change with a clear preference for participatory, deliberative, and decentralised processes. Like its UK counterpart, it does not translate outcome urgency into institutional design that systematically overrides consultation, legal sequencing, or extended review. The observable pattern on pace remains low-to-mid.

### Observable Pace and Intensity

* Strong emergency and urgency framing on climate, Green New Deal-style programmes, and related systemic reforms.
* Consistent emphasis on grassroots participation, community decision-making, and deliberative processes as preferred routes to change.
* Platform and practice that treat extended public engagement and caution regarding top-down rapid implementation as virtues rather than obstacles.
* Limited national governing experience; observable pattern is drawn from national platform language and local/activist practice.
* Structural US constraints (separation of powers, federalism) reinforce rather than counteract the party’s deliberative tilt.
* Comparison with higher-scoring accelerationist parties: urgency of ends does not produce design against delay.

### Expanded Analysis

The scoring guide separates rhetoric about the need for rapid outcomes from institutional mechanisms that actually compress implementation. The US Green Party scores on the latter. Its participatory and deliberative commitments keep it in the low-to-mid band, consistent with the UK Green Party calibration and well below parties that explicitly reject or seek to override process constraints.

### Why this produces a Final Score of 42

Urgency rhetoric on climate and systemic change is combined with a sustained preference for participatory and deliberative processes, producing a score of 42.
