# Core 1A: United States – Green Party
## Personal Autonomy Analysis
**Score: 54**

### Overview
The US Green Party produces a mid-range Core 1A profile built from retention of the residual First Amendment speech baseline, explicit opposition to national-security expansions that erode civil liberties, and a locked programme of drug-policy liberalisation that treats personal possession and use as a health rather than criminal matter. The platform affirms free expression and assembly and does not lock new identity-based speech offences or federal platform content duties. No Endos/Xenos split applies.

### Core 1A Relevant Policies / Elements
* Affirmation of free expression and assembly; call for strict enforcement of First Amendment rights of speech, association and petition.
* Opposition to Patriot Act and related national-security expansions that undermine Bill of Rights protections; support for restoration of habeas corpus and an end to torture.
* Opposition to intimidation of lawful protesters and support for students’ constitutional speech rights.
* Explicit end to the war on drugs: legalisation of cannabis, treatment of drug abuse as a health issue, reduced penalties for simple possession and use of other drugs, and expanded treatment and education resources.
* Prison as last resort for violent crime; alternative community-based programmes for non-violent offences; opposition to the death penalty.
* Opposition to national ID systems and surveillance expansions justified by immigration enforcement.
* Retention of ordinary direct-harm criminal prohibitions (force, fraud, true threats, incitement).
* No locked federal platform-duty or Online-Safety-Act-style architecture; no locked mandatory attitude-correction layers as central programme instruments.

### Expanded Analysis
Under the US residual baseline the First Amendment remains comparatively strong and there is no federal general platform-duty regime on legal speech. The Green programme does not add content-based speech offences or identity-orthodoxy enforcement instruments. It emphasises restoration of protest and speech rights against national-security overreach and locks substantial liberalisation of residual federal and state drug criminalisation for personal possession and use. Ordinary direct-harm criminal law continues. Social-justice and anti-discrimination framing appear in the wider platform but do not translate into a locked federal speech-code architecture that would justify a discrete Endos/Xenos intensity split on identity-critical speech. The institutional pattern is residual speech baseline retained and defended, combined with concrete removal of victimless drug-possession criminalisation, without new layers of content-based restriction.

### Why this produces a Final Score of 54
Retention of the residual First Amendment baseline, opposition to national-security speech and detention expansions, and locked drug-policy liberalisation without new identity-based speech offences or platform-duty architecture place the score in the mid band at 54. No Endos/Xenos split is applied.

Framing as public health or social justice is discarded. Removals of drug-war restrictions grant no upward credit beyond the fact that those items are absent from the final set; the score is set by what remains and by the liberalising programme’s actual residual floor under US baseline speech protection.
