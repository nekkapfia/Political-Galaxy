# Core 1B: United States – Green Party
## Economic Autonomy Analysis
**Final Score: 43**
**Regulation Score: 34**
**Taxation Score: 56**

## Regulation

### Overview
The US Green Party produces a restrictive Core 1B Regulation profile defined by aggressive curbing of corporate power, strong labour and living-wage instruments, comprehensive green and ecological mandates including upstream carbon taxes and rights-of-nature framing, and expansion of public ownership or control in infrastructure, energy, water, health care and related sectors, while residual ordinary competition rules continue under intensified antitrust direction.

### Relevant Policies / Elements
* Reduction of economic and political power of large corporations; end corporate personhood; redesign of corporations to serve society, democracy and environment.
* Strong and effectively enforced antitrust laws against concentration of economic and political power.
* Living-wage / livable-income framing and reinforcement of labour protections and workplace rights.
* Comprehensive ecological sustainability programme: upstream carbon taxes on fossil fuels, clean-air and greenhouse measures, zero-waste and resource constraints, agriculture and biological-diversity rules, rights-of-nature instruments.
* Public ownership or control framed as appropriate for transportation, communication, water, power and related infrastructure, health care, education and most social services.
* Residual FLSA, NLRA and OSHA baselines retained and in places expanded; residual IRA/EPA-type green-industrial architecture expanded into explicit carbon-tax and ecological-mandate layers.
* Local economic development and small-business support inside the broader corporate-power-reduction and green-mandate architecture.

### Expanded Analysis
The Green programme organises economic decision-making around ecological and social-justice priorities that subordinate short-term private preference to climate, intergenerational and equity duties. Corporate form and concentrated private power are treated as problems requiring structural reduction and redesign. Labour and living-wage instruments raise the residual labour floor. Green mandates—carbon taxation, resource and emissions constraints, rights of nature—extend residual federal environmental architecture into a denser behavioural and industrial steering layer. Public-sector expansion in infrastructure and social services further reduces the domain of unconstrained private allocation. Residual competition rules continue but are directed toward breaking concentration rather than neutral market-process protection alone. The institutional pattern is redistributive and ecological expansion of residual labour, corporate and green baselines with expanded public ownership instruments.

### Regulation Score
Locked corporate-power reduction, labour expansion, comprehensive green mandates and public-ownership expansion produce a Regulation Score of 34.

## Taxation

Primary method applies.

* Strongly progressive federal and state taxation, wealth-tax instruments, carbon and eco-taxes, closure of corporate loopholes and higher claim on high incomes and capital, with low-earner relief, produce a higher residual tax-to-GDP ratio than lower-rate packages.
* Estimated tax revenue after package: $11,000 billion
* Current GDP: $25,000 billion

**Taxation Score** = (1 − 11000 / 25000) × 100 = 56.00 → **56**

Ownership, public-sector expansion and corporate redesign remain Regulation only.

**Taxation Score: 56**

## Final Score

A = (34 + 56) / 2 = 45.0
L = min(34, 56) = 34
d = |34 − 56| / 100 = 0.22
F = (1 − 0.22) × 45.0 + 0.22 × 34 = 35.10 + 7.48 = 42.58 → **43**

**Final Score: 43**

### Why this produces a Final Score of 43
Regulation at 34 reflects locked corporate-power reduction, labour expansion, green mandates and public-ownership expansion against residual US baselines. Taxation at 56 is the pure algebraic inverse of the stated higher post-package tax claim under the Primary Method. The A/L/d combination with strict ceiling produces Final Score 43.
