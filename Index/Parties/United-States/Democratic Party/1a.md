# Core 1A: United States – Democratic Party
## Personal Autonomy Analysis
**Score: 30**

### Overview

The Democratic Party, scored as a whole on the restrictions that would exist under its institutional governing programme, produces a restrictive Core 1A profile built from defence of federal and platform-adjacent content-moderation regimes, expansion of identity-based discrimination and hostile-environment frameworks, institutional DEI and attitude-related training cultures across government and aligned institutions, and heightened regulatory and professional risk for speech critical of protected identity categories. ### Core 1A Relevant Policies / Elements

* Defence and expansion of civil-rights and anti-discrimination frameworks (Title VI/VII logic, hostile-environment doctrine) that treat certain critical speech on race, gender, and related identity categories as potentially discriminatory or harassing in employment, education and federally influenced settings.
* Support for platform and intermediary pressure regimes that encourage removal of “hate,” “misinformation,” and identity-hostile content; governing practice has included coordination and regulatory threat surfaces even where formal statutes remain contested.
* Institutional DEI, diversity, and anti-bias training cultures across federal agencies, contractors, universities and aligned private institutions; programmes frequently target attitudes and beliefs rather than solely external conduct.
* Gender-identity institutional rules (recognition, facilities, sports, speech norms including preferred pronouns) generating secondary constraints on expression and association.
* Affirmative defence of existing hate-crime and bias-motivation frameworks; resistance to narrowing identity-protective speech and conduct rules.
* Professional and institutional sanctions risk for dissent from prevailing identity orthodoxy in academia, medicine, media and corporate environments aligned with party priorities.
* No comprehensive programme to restore viewpoint-neutral speech rules in institutional settings or to dismantle hostile-environment expansions that reach non-violent expression.
* Retention of ordinary direct-harm criminal prohibitions.

### Expanded Analysis

The party-as-a-whole score reflects institutional Democratic governing practice and platform priorities, not only progressive-wing maximalism. Hostile-environment and anti-discrimination law has been interpreted and enforced in ways that reach speech. DEI and anti-bias training cultures have been embedded across federal and federally influenced institutions. Gender-identity rules have secondary speech and association effects. Platform-pressure politics have treated identity-hostile and “misinformation” categories as legitimate targets of regulatory and informal control.

Speech critical of specific protected identity categories (race, gender identity, and related topics) attracts heightened institutional, professional and platform risk relative to other political speech. This is a stable, discretely targetable intensity differential.

The architecture sits in the ideological-enforcement range: denser institutional speech control than classical liberal baseline retention, without systematic physical coercion. It sits near European progressive densification poles (UK Green/SNP cluster, German Greens, French LFI) on identity-speech orthodoxy, with a distinct American emphasis on institutional and platform mechanisms rather than expansive criminal hate-speech statutes of the European type.
