# Cultural 5A: United States – Libertarian Party
## Tradition vs Progress Analysis
**Score: 45**

### Overview
Libertarians reject state enforcement of both progressive identity mandates and traditional moral norms, leaving cultural transmission to private and voluntary institutions.

### Cultural 5A Relevant Policies / Elements
* Rejection of state enforcement of progressive identity mandates through curriculum or status law.
* Rejection of state enforcement of traditional moral norms through curriculum or status law.
* Cultural transmission left to private and voluntary institutions rather than government imposition of either progressive orthodoxy or traditionalist discipline.
* Opposition to progressive institutional transformation and to traditionalist state preservation alike.
* Residual progressive state mandates in education and status rules are opposed as state overreach; traditionalist legal baselines are also refused.
* Non-intervention in primary domains produces a distinctive contested pattern defined by neither progressive redesign nor traditionalist institutional protection.

### Expanded Analysis
The Libertarian pattern is unusual on Cultural 5A: primary domains are not captured for progressive redesign, but neither are they institutionalised for traditional transmission. The party opposes progressive identity mandates in curriculum and status law and equally refuses state enforcement of traditional moral norms. Cultural transmission is privatised. The residual progressive settlement in public institutions is contested as overreach; a traditionalist restoration programme is not substituted. Domain hierarchy shows non-intervention as the organising principle in education content and family-status design. That blocks progressive institutional transformation without establishing traditional continuity as the public default—hence contested mid placement defined by absence of state-driven movement in either direction.

### Why this produces a Final Score of 45
Rejection of both progressive identity mandates and traditionalist state discipline, with cultural transmission left to private institutions, places Libertarians in the contested low-mid band (41–50). Higher preservationist placement would require institutional protection of traditional transmission as public baseline; lower progressive placement would require affirmative progressive redesign of education and status rules. Neither is present.
