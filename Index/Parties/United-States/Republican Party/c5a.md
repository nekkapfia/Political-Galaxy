# Cultural 5A: United States – Republican Party
## Tradition vs Progress Analysis
**Score: 61**

### Overview
Republicans defend conventional family structures and national historical continuity in curriculum while resisting progressive gender and education mandates and seeking parental control over school content.

### Cultural 5A Relevant Policies / Elements
* Opposition to progressive gender and race framing in curriculum; parental notification and opt-out; curriculum transparency; anti-CRT and anti-gender-ideology statutes as models in some states.
* Parental rights in education and medical decisions for minors as central organising demand; conventional family form preference.
* Opposition to DEI mandates in federal contracting, agencies and education accreditation; reversal demands for progressive institutional capture.
* Emphasis on national historical continuity, patriotism and conventional cultural transmission in curriculum and public ritual.
* Residual progressive elements in many public systems continue unless reversed; continuity residual (parental rights, local control) also present and stronger in some states.
* Domain hierarchy: education content and family/parental-status primary; material heritage secondary.

### Expanded Analysis
Republicans defend conventional family structures, resist progressive gender and education mandates, and emphasise national historical continuity and parental control over school content. Progressive orthodoxy in media, regulators and public institutions is targeted for reversal. Domain hierarchy places education content and family/parental-status first. The residual progressive settlement in many public systems continues unless reversed by locked instruments; continuity residual (parental rights, local control, conventional family preference) is stronger where Republicans govern. The modern constitutional order is accepted; within it, traditional social norms are treated as requiring concrete legal and educational protection. That is mid-band preservation relative to the Democratic progressive baseline, short of European-style confessional restoration but systematic on curriculum and parental authority.

### Why this produces a Final Score of 61
Curriculum opposition to progressive gender and race framing, parental rights instruments, DEI reversal demands, and conventional family preference against residual progressive settlement in primary domains place Republicans in the mid band (60–69). Higher mid-high placement would require more comprehensive national-continuity baseline institutionalisation of the RN/AfD type; lower placement would ignore concrete reversal instruments in education and parental-status domains.
