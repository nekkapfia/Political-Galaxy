# Cultural 5A: France – Rassemblement National
## Tradition vs Progress Analysis
**Score: 66**

### Overview
RN centres French cultural identity, heritage teaching and national continuity while opposing progressive gender and identity policies in law and schools.

### Cultural 5A Relevant Policies / Elements
* Platform centres French cultural identity, heritage teaching and national continuity as organising goals for education and public policy.
* More traditional positions on family status, education content and social norms relative to the progressive left and centre, with critique of progressive gender and identity policies in law and schools.
* Explicit support for transmission of French language, history and cultural norms as the institutional baseline rather than optional content.
* Framing of progressive cultural change as disruptive to social cohesion, with platform demands for reversal in education and cultural policy.
* Residual progressive settlement on gender and identity frameworks is targeted for rollback in schools and law, not accepted as permanent default.
* Heritage and national continuity treated as primary transmission goods, not secondary decoration inside a progressive architecture.

### Expanded Analysis
RN’s programme treats national-historical continuity and conventional cultural transmission as the intended operating system of education and public culture. Primary domains are oriented toward preservation: curriculum and public policy are to transmit French language, history and cultural norms as baseline; progressive gender and identity frameworks in schools and law are opposed; family and social positions are more traditional than the left and centre. The residual progressive settlement is not accepted—it is framed as disruption requiring reversal. Domain hierarchy therefore prioritises education content and institutional transmission of national continuity, with family-status traditionalism as supporting structure. This is systematic orientation toward traditional transmission within the Fifth Republic framework, not selective signalling inside an untouched progressive residual order.

### Why this produces a Final Score of 66
Concrete demands for French history and cultural norms as institutional baseline, opposition to progressive gender/identity policies in law and schools, and framing of progressive cultural change as requiring reversal place RN in the mid-high band (66–79). The residual progressive settlement is challenged rather than conserved. Lower contested placement would understate the programme’s treatment of national continuity as the default in primary domains.
