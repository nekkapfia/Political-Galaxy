### Overview
Renaissance organises moral legitimacy around competence, delivery, institutional reform and electoral mandate. Transcendent higher-law sources do not organise its claims.

### Moral Justification – Relevant Elements

#### Mundane Justifications
* Competence, delivery and institutional reform are presented as the central sources of political legitimacy.
* Economic openness, European cooperation and pragmatic problem-solving are framed as requirements of effective government.
* Individual opportunity and social mobility are cited as goods of a modern society.
* Evidence-based policy and administrative capacity are treated as core tests of governing success.
* Popular support and electoral mandate are retained as legitimating procedures.
* National interest and practical problem-solving for citizens appear as supporting claims.

### Expanded Analysis
Competence, delivery, institutional reform, economic openness, pragmatic problem-solving, individual opportunity, evidence-based policy, electoral mandate and national interest organise the entire moral conversation. No divine command, Cosmic or Natural law, worship-level deification or sacred Ideal appears. The foundation is Mundane: performance and demos will.
