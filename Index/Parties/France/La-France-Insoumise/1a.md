# Core 1A: France – La France Insoumise
## Personal Autonomy Analysis
**Score: 24**

### Overview

La France Insoumise’s programme produces a restrictive Core 1A profile built from affirmative defence of the 1881 Press Law hate-speech framework and related offences, prioritisation of the fight against Islamophobia as a speech-regulating principle, support for platform regulation under residual national rules and the DSA, and institutional anti-discrimination frameworks. Cumulative weight sits inside the ideological-enforcement range. Heightened intensity on Islam-related and associated identity-critical speech The restriction set is scored as a single combined profile under Core 1A rules (no valid stable dual-structure out-group for an Endos/Xenos split).

### Core 1A Relevant Policies / Elements

* Affirmative defence of French hate-speech legislation (1881 Press Law Art. 24 and related provisions on incitement to hatred or discrimination on origin, race, religion, sex, sexual orientation, gender identity, disability) and its enforcement against racist and anti-Muslim expression.
* Explicit prioritisation of the fight against Islamophobia, including mobilisation participation and treatment of certain critical speech on Islam, Islamic doctrine or Islamist politics as illegitimate or hateful.
* Support for platform content regulation against harmful and identity-hostile material (DSA implementation and residual national obligations after Avia).
* Institutional anti-discrimination and equality frameworks generating secondary pressure on expression in public, educational and campaign settings.
* Within the Nouveau Front Populaire, LFI priorities set the denser identity-protective tone when the party is the dominant partner.
* Shift over the last decade from harder ultra-secularist positions toward identity-protective speech politics on Islam; laïcité residual is weak as a softener of speech intensity.
* No programme to narrow core hate-speech or platform-regulation architecture.
* Retention of ordinary direct-harm criminal prohibitions.

### Expanded Analysis

LFI is the densest progressive actor on the French left for Core 1A. The decisive feature is the elevation of anti-Islamophobia into a concrete speech-regulating principle. Critical speech on Islam or related identity questions faces heightened political, mobilisation and institutional scrutiny relative to parallel criticism of other religions or ideologies. This is a stable, discretely targetable intensity differential.

Hate-speech enforcement and platform duties remain fully supported. Institutional equality frameworks extend constraints into public-sector and campaign settings. Coalition practice inside NFP carries LFI’s restrictive tone rather than diluting it when LFI is weighted.

Unlike the Parti Socialiste, LFI does not offer a strong republican / laïcité residual that would lighten identity-speech intensity. The architecture is baseline criminal/regulatory speech law plus identity-protective densification focused especially on the Islam axis.

### Why this produces Endos 29 / Xenos 24

Hate-speech defence, platform regulation, institutional anti-discrimination pressure, and prioritisation of anti-Islamophobia as a speech-regulating principle place Endos at 29. Heightened intensity on Islam-related and associated identity-critical speech produces Xenos 24.
