# Core 2A: France – La France Insoumise
## National Sovereignty Analysis
**Score: 50**

### Overview
La France Insoumise seeks renegotiation of EU treaties, selective non-application of constraining rules and a more independent French defence posture while remaining inside European membership. External pooling of authority is contested through reform rather than exit.

### National Sovereignty Relevant Policies / Elements
* Supports renegotiation of EU treaties and selective non-application of rules judged incompatible with national social and economic priorities.
* Supports a more independent French foreign and defence policy with reduced NATO operational constraints.
* Supports national control over industrial policy, public services and key economic levers.
* Opposes certain free-trade and fiscal constraints associated with EU and international economic regimes.
* Prioritises renegotiation of EU treaties and national-priority clauses while remaining inside the European framework; euro framework continues.

### Expanded Analysis
La France Insoumise supports renegotiation of EU treaties and selective non-application of rules judged incompatible with national social and economic priorities, alongside a more independent French defence posture with reduced NATO operational constraints. Formal Frexit is not the immediate programme. The euro and ECB constraints continue. The institutional pattern on Core 2A is confrontational expansion of national policy space inside a still-European framework, short of institutional exit. EU membership architecture remains primary drag; intensity is moderated by selective non-application claims. National residual capacity is asserted within the residual external framework rather than through structural exit.

### Why this produces a Final Score of 50
Treaty renegotiation and selective non-application of EU rules inside continued membership expand national policy space without institutional exit. External pooling of authority continues and is explicit. The final set therefore sits inside the 41–50 Low–Mid to 51–59 Mid–Lower transition. The score of 50 reflects contested pooling of authority rather than structural exit from EU architecture.
