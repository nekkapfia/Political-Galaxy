# Cultural 1A: France – La France Insoumise
## Foundation Analysis
**Score: 66**

### Overview
La France Insoumise presents itself as the force of rupture with liberal economics, Atlanticist foreign policy and elite governance. Core aims are social justice, ecological planning, popular sovereignty via institutional change, and solidarity with the disadvantaged. Transcendent sources—systemic justice and ecological duty—are strong and organising alongside mundane popular-sovereignty language. Residual baseline of mixed transcendent and mundane justification: higher-order frames coexist with substantial residual demotic, procedural or welfare legitimacy claims.

### Moral Justification – Relevant Elements
* The existing economic order frequently criticised as systemically unjust, not merely suboptimal.
* Ecological emergency framed as a moral duty requiring planning and constraint on present consumption.
* Popular sovereignty asserted against elites, European constraints and established institutions, often via calls for a new republic and citizen initiative.
* Solidarity and anti-austerity claims carry independent moral weight.
* National language sometimes appropriated in social rather than ethnic terms.
* Electoral and movement mobilisation as means to higher justice ends.
* Residual baseline of mixed transcendent and mundane justification: higher-order frames coexist with substantial residual demotic, procedural or welfare legitimacy claims.

### Expanded Analysis
LFI mixes systemic justice, ecological duty and popular-sovereignty claims. The movement presents itself as the authentic voice of the people against a self-serving elite while grounding much of its programme in moralised critiques of capitalism and ecological destruction that outrank ordinary market and short-term majoritarian preference. Transcendent sources are organising; mundane popular-sovereignty language prevents a pure high-T score of the green-maximal or historical-communist type. The profile is clearly on the transcendent side of the French spectrum.

### Why this produces a Final Score of 66
Transcendent sources (systemic justice and ecological duty) are strong and organising, while popular-sovereignty language adds a mundane element. The overall profile remains clearly transcendent-leaning. The score is 66.
