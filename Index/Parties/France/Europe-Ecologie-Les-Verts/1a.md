# Core 1A: France – Europe Écologie Les Verts
## Personal Autonomy Analysis
**Score: 25**

### Overview

Europe Écologie Les Verts produces a restrictive Core 1A profile built from support for the 1881 Press Law hate-speech framework, platform regulation under DSA-era rules, strong identity and anti-discrimination frameworks, and close alignment with LFI-style prioritisation of protecting designated groups from critical speech. Ecological framing is discarded as justification. Cumulative weight sits beside LFI inside the ideological-enforcement range. Heightened intensity against identity-critical speech The restriction set is scored as a single combined profile under Core 1A rules (no valid stable dual-structure out-group for an Endos/Xenos split).

### Core 1A Relevant Policies / Elements

* Affirmative support for French hate-speech legislation (1881 Press Law framework on incitement to hatred or discrimination on origin, race, religion, sex, sexual orientation, gender identity, disability) and its enforcement against racist, anti-Muslim, sexist and related identity-hostile expression; no narrowing agenda.
* Support for platform content regulation (DSA implementation and residual national obligations after Avia) aimed at restricting harmful and identity-related material online.
* Strong institutional anti-discrimination and equality frameworks generating secondary pressure on expression in public-sector, educational, cultural and campaign settings; hostile-environment logic applies.
* Identity-protective norms treating critical speech on race, gender, migration, Islam and related topics as potentially harmful, illegitimate or as creating a hostile environment.
* Alignment with Nouveau Front Populaire and LFI-weighted priorities that reinforce rather than soften identity-protective speech norms when green-left and radical left act together.
* Gender and queer institutional agendas generally supported; secondary effects on speech (including misgendering frames) and association around sex-based categories accepted.
* Ecological and climate framing can extend institutional orthodoxy pressure into professional and public roles, but the operative Core 1A constraints remain the speech and equality layers.
* No programme to repeal or substantially narrow core hate-speech or platform-regulation architecture.
* Retention of ordinary direct-harm criminal prohibitions.

### Expanded Analysis

EELV sits on the denser progressive pole with LFI. It supports the same hate-speech and platform-regulation architecture and shares the identity-protective orientation that treats certain critical speech as harmful or illegitimate. Unlike the Parti Socialiste, it does not offer a meaningful laïcité residual that would lighten identity-speech intensity.

Institutional anti-discrimination rules extend constraints into public-sector, educational and cultural settings. Coalition practice with LFI inside the Nouveau Front Populaire carries the same restrictive tone rather than diluting it. Speech critical of specific protected identity categories (race, gender, Islam, migration and related topics) attracts heightened political and institutional scrutiny relative to other political speech — a stable, discretely targetable intensity differential.

Mandatory attitude-correction training is less formalised as a national EELV programme than under UK Green councils, which prevents a still lower placement, but the identity-speech and institutional orthodoxy architecture remains dense and cumulative.

### Why this produces Endos 30 / Xenos 25

Hate-speech defence, platform regulation, institutional anti-discrimination pressure, identity-protective norms, and LFI-aligned priorities place Endos at 30. Heightened intensity on identity-critical speech produces Xenos 25.
