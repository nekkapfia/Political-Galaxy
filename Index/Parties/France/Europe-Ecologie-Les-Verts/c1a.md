# Cultural 1A: France – Europe Écologie Les Verts
## Foundation Analysis
**Score: 74**

### Overview
EELV presents ecological breakdown and intergenerational injustice as defining moral crises. Core aims are systemic transformation to protect climate, ecosystems and future generations. Organising sources are strongly transcendent: duties to the living world and future persons that regularly override present majorities and short-term economic preference. Residual baseline of mixed transcendent and mundane justification: higher-order frames coexist with substantial residual demotic, procedural or welfare legitimacy claims.

### Moral Justification – Relevant Elements
* Ecological and climate duties treated as binding moral requirements constraining present consumption and growth.
* Intergenerational justice as independent ethical claim on behalf of future persons.
* Economic arrangements criticised in moral terms for destructive ecological impact.
* Scientific evidence as support for prior ethical imperative.
* Progressive social justice claims operate alongside the ecological core.
* Conventional majoritarian economic politics treated as morally inadequate where they conflict with planetary limits.
* Residual baseline of mixed transcendent and mundane justification: higher-order frames coexist with substantial residual demotic, procedural or welfare legitimacy claims.

### Expanded Analysis
EELV organises justification through duties that outrank present preference. Climate and ecological limits are moral constraints that legitimate transformation even when costly to living majorities. Intergenerational language treats future persons as independent moral patients. Science establishes the factual basis of an already-primary ethical duty. The institutional profile is strong transcendent lean, slightly below purest green maximalism only where coalition and republican constraints soften absolute override language.

### Why this produces a Final Score of 74
The transcendent source is primary and organising. Present democratic will and economic interests are regularly subordinated to ecological and intergenerational claims. The score is 74.
