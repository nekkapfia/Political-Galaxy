# Core 2A: United Kingdom – Green Party
## National Sovereignty Analysis
**Score: 33**

### Overview
The Green Party retains the ECHR and Windsor Framework, opposes deterrent migration schemes and expands legal routes, accepting lower national control in favour of rights-based international frameworks. External pooling of authority is preferred.

### National Sovereignty Relevant Policies / Elements
* Opposes deterrent schemes such as Rwanda and favours expansion of safe and legal routes as the primary migration mechanism.
* Supports continued UK membership of the ECHR and its domestic incorporation.
* Supports maintenance of the Windsor Framework and Northern Ireland’s residual regulatory alignment arrangements.
* Favours higher legal immigration and more open asylum pathways.
* Supports international climate and human-rights institutions that create binding external policy commitments; post-Brexit external pooling of authority continues.

### Expanded Analysis
The Green Party retains the ECHR and the Windsor Framework as standing external constraints, rejects deterrent schemes such as Rwanda, and expands legal and humanitarian routes. International climate and human-rights institutions are supported as sources of binding external commitment. Post-Brexit external pooling of authority is not dismantled. The institutional pattern on Core 2A is acceptance of lower unilateral national control in favour of rights-based international frameworks and higher managed migration intake. ECHR, Windsor Framework and international institutional pooling of authority constitute primary drag.

### Why this produces a Final Score of 33
Expanded legal and humanitarian routes together with retention of the ECHR and Windsor Framework emphasise international integration over unilateral sovereign decision-making. External legal pooling of authority is explicit and preferred, placing the final set inside the 31–40 Low band. Constraints that continue are locked. The score of 33 follows from prioritisation of rights-based external frameworks and higher inflows rather than recovery of unilateral control.
