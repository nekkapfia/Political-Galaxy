# Cultural 5A: United Kingdom – Green Party
## Tradition vs Progress Analysis
**Score: 18**

### Overview
The Greens subordinate inherited national and familial forms to climate, equity and identity-progressive goals, treating traditional continuity as subject to reform rather than protection.

### Cultural 5A Relevant Policies / Elements
* Platform support for curriculum decolonisation and for gender self-identification in law and institutional practice.
* Education and cultural policy emphasise climate, equity and critical reframing of national narratives over positive continuity teaching.
* Family and social policy support legal recognition of non-traditional family forms and gender self-identification frameworks.
* Traditional national and familial forms treated as subjects of reform rather than as baselines requiring protection.
* Heritage and national continuity subordinated to progressive social and ecological transformation goals in platform content.
* Residual progressive settlement is extended; pre-progressive family and continuity curriculum are not defended.

### Expanded Analysis
Primary domains under Green programme are transformative. Curriculum decolonisation and critical national narrative replace positive continuity as preferred education framing; gender self-identification and non-traditional family recognition redesign status rules; traditional forms are reform material rather than protected defaults. The residual progressive settlement is extended, not contested by any programme to restore conventional family law or continuity curriculum. Domain hierarchy places education framing and family-status design first; heritage is secondary and subordinated to climate and equity goals. Progressive primacy is consistent across high-weight domains with minimal institutional space for unmodified traditional baselines.


### Why this produces a Final Score of 18
Curriculum decolonisation, gender self-identification, non-traditional family recognition, and treatment of traditional continuity as reform material place the UK Greens in the very low 11–20 band. Residual progressive settlement is extended. No programme protects traditional transmission as the public default in primary domains, so placement above the absolute floor reflects only the absence of total enforcement machinery rather than any continuity project.
