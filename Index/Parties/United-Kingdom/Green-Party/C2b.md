# Cultural 2B: United Kingdom – Green Party
## National Pride Analysis
**Score: 26**

### Overview
The Green Party treats traditional British national identity and much of the nation’s longer history as problematic. National pride is secondary or suspect; education and cultural policy prioritise critical, post-colonial and atonement-oriented frames. Residual baseline of shame- or guilt-oriented framing: national pride is treated as problematic or secondary; critical and pathologising frames predominate in education and cultural institutions.

### Relevant Policies / Elements
* Critical national history and systemic-injustice framing in education and cultural policy positions.
* Emphasis on repair, diversity of narrative and challenging traditional national self-understanding.
* Positive founding myths treated as incomplete without critical overlay.
* Pride language minimised relative to corrective moral education.
* Local actions (where Greens hold influence) consistent with critical reinterpretation of symbols and curriculum.
* Residual baseline of shame- or guilt-oriented framing: national pride is treated as problematic or secondary; critical and pathologising frames predominate in education and cultural institutions.

### Expanded Analysis
If the Greens gained national power they would deepen critical and atonement-oriented framing of British history rather than restore affirmative national narratives. Traditional national pride is not treated as a legitimate organising principle. The score sits in the Remorse band, reflecting an active preference for shame-oriented and post-national cultural education over continuity or celebration of the longer nation.

### Why this produces a Final Score of 26
Organising cultural and educational priorities around critical rejection of traditional national identity and emphasis on atonement places the Greens in the Remorse band.
