## Regulation

### Overview
The Green Party produces a lower Core 1B Regulation profile defined by major public ownership of utilities, expanded public investment vehicles, strong residual environmental mandates and net-zero targets, and expanded worker protections.

### Relevant Policies / Elements
* Bringing privatised utilities into public hands and expanded public coordination and investment vehicles.
* Expanded residual environmental mandates and strong net-zero targets; residual Climate Change Act architecture retained and intensified.
* Large public investment programmes directing capital and production toward environmental and social goals.
* Expanded worker protections and equality measures beyond residual baseline.
* Residual competition-law baseline continues but is subordinated to public-ownership and industrial-policy priorities.
* Residual secondary regulatory baseline continues and is deepened; no high-priority labour-market deregulation or residual green-architecture repeal is locked.

### Expanded Analysis
The Green Party final set advances major public ownership of utilities and expanded public investment vehicles. Residual environmental mandates and net-zero targets are intensified. Large public investment programmes direct capital and production. Residual labour protections are expanded. Residual competition rules remain but are secondary to public-ownership and industrial-policy priorities. The institutional pattern is substantial expansion of residual green-industrial and public-ownership direction combined with expanded labour protections, significantly increasing state control over investment, production and resource allocation.

### Regulation Score
Major public ownership of utilities, expanded public investment vehicles, intensified residual environmental mandates and net-zero targets, and expanded worker protections produce a Regulation Score of 35.
