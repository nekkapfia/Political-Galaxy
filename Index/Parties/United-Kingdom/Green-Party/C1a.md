# Cultural 1A: United Kingdom – Green Party
## Foundation Analysis
**Score: 81**

### Overview
The Green Party presents ecological breakdown and intergenerational injustice as the defining moral crises of the age. Its core aims are systemic economic and social transformation to protect the living world and future generations. Organising moral sources are strongly transcendent: duties to the planet and to persons not yet living that regularly override present democratic majorities and short-term material preferences. Residual baseline of transcendent moral justification: higher-order ideological, sacred or civilisational sources organise legitimacy and subordinate pure demotic or welfare claims.

### Moral Justification – Relevant Elements
* Ecological and climate duties framed as binding moral requirements that can and should constrain present consumption, growth and democratic preference.
* Intergenerational justice treated as an independent ethical claim on behalf of future persons, not merely as stewardship of a specific national community’s inheritance.
* Economic arrangements criticised in moral terms for their destructive impact on ecosystems and the vulnerable, not only for inefficiency or unfair distribution among the living.
* Scientific evidence used extensively but as diagnostic support for a prior ethical imperative rather than as the ultimate source of obligation.
* Progressive social justice and equality claims operate alongside the ecological core, reinforcing duty-based rather than purely demos-based legitimacy.
* Conventional growth and majoritarian economic politics treated as morally inadequate where they conflict with planetary boundaries.
* Residual baseline of transcendent moral justification: higher-order ideological, sacred or civilisational sources organise legitimacy and subordinate pure demotic or welfare claims.

### Expanded Analysis
The Greens organise political justification through duties that outrank present preference. Climate and ecological limits are not optional policy preferences of the current demos; they are moral constraints that legitimate transformation even when costly to living majorities. Intergenerational language treats future persons as independent moral patients whose claims bind the present, rather than as heirs of a particular national community’s empirical inheritance. Science is invoked constantly, yet functions primarily to establish the factual basis of a duty already treated as ethically primary. Progressive social claims reinforce rather than dilute this pattern. The institutional profile is therefore strong transcendent lean: present democratic will and economic interest are regularly subordinated to higher-order ecological and intergenerational obligations.

### Why this produces a Final Score of 81
The transcendent source is primary and organising. Present democratic will and short-term economic interests are regularly subordinated to ecological and intergenerational claims, producing a strong transcendent lean short of totalising ideological extremes. The score is 81.
