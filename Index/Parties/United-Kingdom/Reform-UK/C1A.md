# Cultural 1A: United Kingdom – Reform UK
## Foundation Analysis
Final Score: 0 (50M)
### Overview
Reform UK organises moral legitimacy around democratic control, concrete interests of existing citizens, national self-preservation and delivery. Transcendent higher-law sources do not organise its claims.

### Moral Justification – Relevant Elements

#### Mundane Justifications
* Democratic control, secure borders and the concrete interests of existing citizens are presented as primary political goods.
* Mass immigration is criticised as an override of popular will and a source of pressure on housing, wages and services.
* National self-preservation and reciprocity are framed as ordinary responsibilities of a living political community.
* Competence and delivery are treated as central tests of government legitimacy.
* Electoral mandate and direct popular preference are cited as the proper sources of authority.
* Quantifiable harms to citizens and institutional failure are used as evidence.

### Expanded Analysis
Democratic control, secure borders, concrete interests of existing citizens, national self-preservation as ordinary responsibility, competence, delivery and electoral mandate organise legitimacy. Quantifiable harms to citizens are used as evidence. No divine command, Cosmic or Natural law, worship-level deification or sacred Ideal appears. The foundation is Mundane: demos will, concrete interests and performance.
