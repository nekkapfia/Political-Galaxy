# Cultural 1A: United Kingdom – Reform UK
## Foundation Analysis
**Score: 4**

### Overview
Reform UK grounds political legitimacy almost exclusively in the democratic will and concrete interests of living British citizens. Immigration control, institutional reform and national priority are justified through popular sovereignty, reciprocity and measurable outcomes for the existing population. Transcendent sources—sacred authority, higher-law justice, ecological override, or ideological historical necessity—are effectively absent from its organising moral grammar.

### Moral Justification – Relevant Elements
* Immigration and border control justified by the right of the British people to decide who enters and by the duty of government to prioritise housing, wages, services and safety of existing citizens.
* Net-zero and climate policy criticised where they impose costs on living citizens without democratic consent or clear reciprocal benefit; ecological claims are not treated as binding overrides of present preference.
* Institutional reform and “drain the swamp” language framed as restoring democratic accountability and practical competence, not as installing higher ideological authority.
* National priority and reciprocity presented as ordinary political obligations of a sovereign demos to its own members.
* Rejection of elite and progressive orthodoxy as overrides of popular will and empirical common sense.
* Electoral mandate and concrete delivery treated as primary legitimating sources.

### Expanded Analysis
Reform UK organises justification through popular sovereignty, citizen interest and reciprocity. Hard trade-offs—immigration volume, climate costs, institutional power—are resolved by reference to what living British citizens want and what measurably serves them, not by reference to duties that bind the demos from outside. Ecological language, where it appears, is subordinated to cost, consent and national interest rather than elevated as intergenerational higher law. Progressive institutional claims are rejected as elite projects that have failed democratic and empirical tests. There is no residual sacred, natural-law or revolutionary-ideological source that regularly overrides present majorities. The pattern is therefore extreme mundane lean, only marginally above the absolute floor occupied by pure continuity-restoration projects such as Restore Britain.

### Why this produces a Final Score of 4
The transcendent source is almost entirely absent across core domains. All major claims are grounded in popular sovereignty, citizen interest, reciprocity and national self-preservation of the living demos, producing an extremely strong mundane lean. The score is 4.
