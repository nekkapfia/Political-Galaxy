# Cultural 1A: United Kingdom – Scottish National Party
## Foundation Analysis
**Score: 28**

### Overview
The SNP grounds political legitimacy in the democratic right of the Scottish people to self-determination and in social-democratic justice delivered through competent government. Independence is framed as the constitutional choice of a living political community, not as sacralised historical destiny. Organising sources remain predominantly mundane—demos, equality, evidence and delivery—with progressive rights language present but secondary and secular. Residual baseline of strongly mundane justification: legitimacy is organised through demotic procedure, welfare outcomes or pragmatic performance rather than higher-order ideological or sacred sources.

### Moral Justification – Relevant Elements
* Scottish independence justified as the democratic right of the Scottish demos to choose its constitutional future through consent and electoral mandate.
* Social-democratic justice, public services and equality framed through fairness, evidence-based delivery and contemporary moral consensus rather than sacred or higher-law authority.
* Progressive social policy justified through rights, dignity and evolving secular norms, not through divine command or natural law as override.
* National identity treated as political community and democratic subject—the people entitled to decide—rather than as blood, sacred mission or destiny independent of preference.
* Criticism of Westminster framed as democratic deficit, policy failure and lack of consent, not as violation of higher sacred or historical law.
* Competence, fiscal responsibility and electoral mandate retained as central mundane legitimating sources alongside self-determination.
* Residual baseline of strongly mundane justification: legitimacy is organised through demotic procedure, welfare outcomes or pragmatic performance rather than higher-order ideological or sacred sources.

### Expanded Analysis
Scored as an individual party rather than as part of a regional-nationalist bloc, the SNP organises justification through mundane sources: popular sovereignty of the Scottish demos, social-democratic fairness and governing competence. Independence is a claim of democratic self-determination by a living territorial political community, not a sacralised historical destiny that would bind Scots against their own clear preference or elevate nationhood above consent. Progressive social positions draw on rights and dignity within a secular frame. National identity functions as the boundary of the demos entitled to decide, not as transcendent substance. Criticism of the UK state is cast in democratic-deficit and delivery-failure language. Transcendent sources—divine command, ecological override as higher law, revolutionary historical necessity—do not organise the moral conversation. The pattern is clear mundane lean, consistent with other secular national-democratic parties that treat self-determination as demos right rather than sacred mission.

### Why this produces a Final Score of 28
Democratic self-determination, social-democratic justice and competence as organising sources, with negligible sacred or higher-law content, produce a clear mundane lean. Treated as a unique party, the score is 28.
