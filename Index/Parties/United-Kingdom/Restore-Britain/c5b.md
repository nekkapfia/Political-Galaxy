# Cultural 5B: United Kingdom – Restore Britain
## Radicalism (Pace of Change) Analysis
**Score: 80**

### Overview

Restore Britain advocates compressed, high-intensity, and total transformation on core priorities, especially immigration and institutional control. Its platform specifies day-one implementation of the largest deportation scheme in British history, abolition of the asylum system, retrospective removals, and explicit rejection of legal and deliberative delay. The party has published detailed implementation material and frames extended process as an obstacle to be overridden rather than a legitimate constraint.

### Observable Pace and Intensity

* Explicit commitment to day-one detention and removal of the illegal migrant population, described as non-negotiable and immediate.
* Published detailed (100+ page) mass-deportation policy document setting out operational steps, capacity requirements, and legal changes required for rapid execution.
* Call to abolish the asylum system in its entirety and to end use of immigration tribunals for illegal entrants, removing sequential legal layers that currently slow removal.
* Stated intention to leave or override ECHR / Human Rights Act constraints and related judicial review mechanisms that produce extended timelines.
* Framing of “legal paralysis,” activist judges, and prolonged process as barriers that a Restore Britain government would remove in order to achieve compressed implementation.
* Platform language presenting constant rapid transformation on borders, institutions, and selected cultural rules as the required operating mode rather than sequenced or consultative reform.
* Limited governing record; score rests on the clarity, detail, and anti-deliberative character of the stated implementation design.

### Expanded Analysis

Under the scoring guide, detailed platform language that specifies compressed timelines and rejects deliberative or legal sequencing can produce high scores even without a long national governing record. Restore Britain meets that threshold. The combination of day-one operational commitments, a published implementation plan, and open hostility to the procedural layers that currently slow change places it in the high-radicalism band (80–89). The score reflects pace and institutional design against delay, not the substantive direction of the immigration or cultural policies (which belong on other axes).

### Why this produces a Final Score of 80

Explicit day-one total transformation design, detailed operational planning, and systematic rejection of deliberative and legal delay mechanisms produce a strong institutional preference for rapid and disruptive change, placing Restore Britain at 80.
