# Core 1A: United Kingdom – Workers Party of Britain
## Personal Autonomy Analysis
**Score: 33**

### Overview

The Workers Party’s governing programme produces a mixed Core 1A profile built from retention of the Online Safety Act, affirmative support for public-order and hate-speech provisions targeting racist, anti-Muslim and anti-immigrant expression, and retention of core non-violent speech offences, combined with resistance to some gender-identity institutional controls. Identity-speech enforcement around race and Islam remains actively supported; the densest gender-orthodoxy layers and mandatory attitude-correction training are absent. A limited Endos/Xenos differential is recorded.

### Core 1A Relevant Policies / Elements

* Online Safety Act 2023 retained; no high-priority repeal agenda and no platform-duty liberalisation at the centre of the programme.
* Affirmative support for public-order and hate-speech provisions addressing racist, anti-Muslim or anti-immigrant expression, with explicit defence of enforcement in these areas.
* Core non-violent speech offences and communications provisions criminalising certain forms of expression, retained.
* Criticism of selective or progressive-leaning applications of hate-speech and institutional speech codes, particularly around gender identity; this resistance means some gender-identity orthodoxy layers present under Green/SNP programmes are absent.
* Opposition to some forms of institutional DEI-driven speech regulation while defending other identity-based restrictions (especially race and religion).
* No election-period platform amplification duties of the Labour type.

### Expanded Analysis

The Workers Party retains the Online Safety Act and actively defends hate-speech and public-order enforcement around race, Islam and immigration. These layers therefore remain and exert downward weight. At the same time the party resists parts of the gender-identity institutional package; self-identification speech constraints and related orthodoxy measures that appear under Green and SNP programmes are not part of its final set.

The result is a selective rather than comprehensive identity-speech architecture. Criminal and regulatory controls on speech characterised as racist or anti-Muslim are affirmed; parallel institutional intensification around gender identity is resisted. Rhetorical criticism of progressive speech codes is relevant only insofar as it means certain restrictive layers are absent.

Speech critical of Islam, immigration or related topics attracts selectively heightened enforcement risk. This is a modest but observable intensity differential, satisfying a limited Endos/Xenos split.

The architecture sits above the Green/SNP/Sinn Féin/Plaid cluster because mandatory attitude-correction training and the densest gender-orthodoxy layers are absent, and below pure baseline-retention parties because identity-speech enforcement around race and Islam remains actively supported.

### Why this produces Endos 38 / Xenos 33

Online Safety Act retained, affirmative enforcement of race/Islam/immigration-related hate-speech and public-order provisions, core communications offences retained, but without mandatory attitude-correction training and without the densest gender-identity orthodoxy layers, place Endos at 38. Heightened application to specific identity-critical speech produces Xenos 33.
