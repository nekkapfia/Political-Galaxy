# Cultural 1A: Workers-Party-Of-Britain
## Foundation Analysis
Final Score: 0 (50M)

### Overview
The Workers Party of Britain organises moral legitimacy around working-class material interest, systemic critique of capitalist structures, and electoral competition. These are secular this-worldly claims.

### Moral Justification – Relevant Elements

#### Mundane Justifications
* Working-class material interest and social provision are framed as primary moral and political requirements.
* Capitalist and imperial structures are criticised in systemic terms as producing structural illegitimacy.
* Socialist economic direction is presented as a necessary correction of the current order.
* Electoral competition and material delivery for ordinary people are treated as legitimating sources.
* Concrete harms to living standards, housing and services are cited as evidence.
* Opposition to progressive identity orthodoxy is framed as defence of working-class cohesion and democratic majority preference.
* Historical socialist tradition is invoked as continuity and inheritance for present political duty.
* Anti-imperialism and opposition to financial power are advanced as political and moral claims.

### Expanded Analysis
Working-class material interest, systemic critique, electoral competition, delivery, and concrete harms organise the moral conversation. These are secular this-worldly claims. No divine command, Cosmic law, worship-level deification, or sacralised Ideal organises legitimacy. Transcendent sources are entirely absent from the moral justifications. The foundation is Mundane.
