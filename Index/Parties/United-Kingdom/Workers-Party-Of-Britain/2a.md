# Core 2A: United Kingdom – Workers Party of Britain
## National Sovereignty Analysis
**Score: 61**

### Overview
The Workers Party rejects EU re-integration, seeks national control over borders and industrial strategy, and treats the ECHR and NATO constraints as negotiable. External pooling of authority is resisted where it reconstitutes legal supremacy.

### National Sovereignty Relevant Policies / Elements
* Opposes EU membership and any form of re-accession or residual regulatory alignment that reconstitutes external legal supremacy.
* Supports a more independent British foreign and defence policy with reduced NATO operational constraints.
* Supports national control over borders, trade policy and industrial strategy.
* Seeks to limit or renegotiate constraints imposed by international financial and trade institutions where they conflict with domestic industrial and social priorities.
* Treats the ECHR as a negotiable framework subject to national review and possible reform or exit; residual external pooling of authority is generally resisted.

### Expanded Analysis
The Workers Party rejects EU re-accession and residual regulatory alignment that would reconstitute external legal supremacy. It seeks national control over borders, trade and industrial strategy, treats the ECHR as negotiable, and favours a more independent defence posture with reduced NATO operational constraints. Post-Brexit pooling of authority is not accepted as permanent baseline. The institutional pattern on Core 2A is retention of independent statehood combined with systematic resistance to the re-pooling of authority through European or dense multilateral legal regimes. Primary drag from external pooling of authority is deliberately reduced relative to continuity parties.

### Why this produces a Final Score of 61
Rejection of EU re-accession, national control over borders and industry, and a negotiable stance on the ECHR reduce external legal constraint. Constraints that continue are limited and treated as subject to review. The final set therefore sits inside the 60–69 Mid band. The score of 61 follows from recovery of unilateral decision-making capacity rather than acceptance of external pooling of authority as the preferred baseline.
