# Core 1A: United Kingdom – Labour Party
## Personal Autonomy Analysis
**Score: 34**

### Overview

Labour’s governing programme, weighted toward the recent Starmer government and Andy Burnham’s faction, produces a restrictive Core 1A profile built from full retention of the Online Safety Act, election-period and political-content platform duties, an official anti-Muslim hostility definition, the complete Public Order / Racial and Religious Hatred / communications suite, and further under-16 social-media and device-level measures. Burnham does not reverse this direction. Cumulative weight sits in the **28–36 — Ideological enforcement and police state** band. Heightened risk on speech critical of Islam is content-triggered, not a stable group dual structure; no Endos/Xenos split applies.

### Core 1A Relevant Policies / Elements

* Online Safety Act 2023 fully retained, defended and actively implemented (illegal-content duties, age-assurance, platform accountability under Ofcom). Neither Starmer nor Burnham advances repeal or substantial narrowing.
* Election-period and political-content platform duties imposing obligations on amplification and visibility of political information, analogous to broadcast impartiality rules. Burnham-linked signals include openness to stronger press and platform regulation.
* Official definition of “anti-Muslim hostility” guiding institutional and potentially legal responses, raising practical restriction risk on speech critical of Islam, Islamic doctrine or Islamist politics.
* Public Order Act 1986 (Section 5 and stirring-up), Racial and Religious Hatred Act, Communications Act 2003 and Malicious Communications Act retained in full; no narrowing programme.
* Under-16 social-media restrictions and support for device-level scanning or access controls — direct constraints on communication and information access.
* Institutional equality and hate-speech frameworks continuing to subject speech critical of protected characteristics to hostile-environment framing and disciplinary risk.
* No mandatory attitude-correction training as a central national programme of the Green type, though EDI frameworks remain embedded in public-sector practice.

### Expanded Analysis

The Starmer government has defended and operationalised the Online Safety Act, added concrete platform and under-16 measures, and institutionalised an anti-Muslim hostility definition. Andy Burnham’s soft-left faction offers no liberalising counter-weight on speech; signals point toward continuity or mild intensification of platform and press controls. Residual Corbynite positions are not decisive for this score.

The anti-Muslim hostility definition functions as an institutional lens that heightens restriction risk on a discrete category of religious and political criticism. Election-period platform duties extend content control into the visibility of political speech. Under-16 and device-level measures add further direct constraints on access and communication.

Speech critical of Islam or Islamism faces heightened institutional scrutiny; that intensity is content-triggered and does not create a separate legal dual structure for a closed out-group.

The architecture includes concrete expansions beyond pure baseline retention (Online Safety Act operationalisation, under-16 measures, anti-Muslim hostility definition). Mandatory attitude-correction training is not a central national feature.

### Why this produces a Score of 34

Online Safety Act retention, election-period platform duties, anti-Muslim hostility definition, full public-order and communications architecture, and under-16 measures place the regime inside **28–36 — Ideological enforcement and police state** at **34**.
