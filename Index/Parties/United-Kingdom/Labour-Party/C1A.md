### Overview
Labour organises moral legitimacy around fairness and social justice as secular requirements of a decent society, competence, delivery, electoral mandate, and the capacity to improve living standards.

### Moral Justification – Relevant Elements

#### Mundane Justifications
* Inequality and poverty are framed as injustices that government has a duty to correct within a secular social-democratic frame.
* Fairness, equality and social justice are presented as requirements of a decent society.
* Public services and economic policy are justified through fairness claims and through the practical capacity to deliver results.
* Competence, fiscal responsibility and evidence-based government are treated as central sources of political legitimacy.
* Rights and dignity language appears within broader justice and managerial frameworks.
* Electoral mandate and the capacity to improve living standards for ordinary people are cited as core sources of authority.
* National renewal and the restoration of trust in government are advanced as practical and moral goals.
* Socialist and labour-movement tradition is invoked as continuity and inheritance for present political duty.

### Expanded Analysis
Fairness, equality and social justice as requirements of a decent society, competence, delivery, electoral mandate and living-standards improvement organise the moral conversation. Under the locked lists, secular social-democratic fairness language is Mundane — the same strictness applied to secular revolutionary language and human-necessity ecology. No divine command, Cosmic or Natural law, worship-level deification, or sacralised Ideal organises legitimacy. Transcendent sources are entirely absent from the moral justifications. The foundation is Mundane.
