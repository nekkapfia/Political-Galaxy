# Core 1A: United Kingdom – Sinn Féin
## Personal Autonomy Analysis
**Endos Score: 27**  
**Xenos Score: 23**

### Overview

Sinn Féin’s governing programme produces a restrictive Core 1A profile built from full retention of the Online Safety Act, public-order and hate-speech provisions, institutional measures against hate speech, sectarianism and discrimination, and communications offences criminalising non-violent expression. The Northern Ireland equality and anti-sectarian institutional layer adds further constraint. Cumulative weight sits inside the ideological-enforcement range. Heightened focus on identity-critical speech justifies a limited Endos/Xenos split.

### Core 1A Relevant Policies / Elements

* Online Safety Act 2023 framework retained in full, including illegal-content duties and platform accountability. No repeal or substantial narrowing agenda.
* Public-order and hate-speech provisions addressing identity-hostile expression, including speech characterised as sectarian, racist or otherwise hostile to protected groups, retained and affirmatively defended.
* Institutional and educational measures aimed at combating hate speech, sectarianism and discrimination. These extend into public-sector, educational and community settings and treat speech critical of protected characteristics or of the equality framework as potentially harmful.
* Communications offences and related statutes criminalising a range of non-violent expression, accepted and retained.
* Equality and anti-discrimination frameworks generating secondary institutional pressure on expression, including hostile-environment and potential disciplinary risk for speech that conflicts with institutional orthodoxy.
* No proposal to repeal or substantially narrow the core hate-speech, public-order or online-safety architecture.

### Expanded Analysis

Sinn Féin treats the existing architecture of platform duties, public-order offences and hate-speech provisions as necessary and retains them in full. The Northern Ireland context adds a further layer: anti-sectarian and equality-duty frameworks have long operated as institutional constraints on expression, and the party’s programme reinforces rather than relaxes them.

Institutional and educational measures against hate speech and sectarianism function as orthodoxy requirements in public and quasi-public settings. Equality frameworks generate secondary but real constraints on speech that challenges protected characteristics or the prevailing institutional consensus.

Speech critical of certain protected characteristics attracts heightened scrutiny. This is a stable, discretely targetable intensity differential, narrower than formal dual legal systems but consistent with the party’s priorities and Northern Ireland equality practice.

Mandatory attitude-correction training is not evidenced as a central party-defining feature of the Green type, keeping the score marginally above Green while remaining inside the same broad band.

### Why this produces Endos 27 / Xenos 23

Online Safety Act retention, public-order and hate-speech provisions, institutional orthodoxy measures against hate speech and sectarianism, communications offences, and equality-framework pressure place Endos at 27. Heightened application to identity-critical speech produces Xenos 23.
