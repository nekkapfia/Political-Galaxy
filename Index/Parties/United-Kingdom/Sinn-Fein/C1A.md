### Overview
Sinn Féin organises moral legitimacy around democratic self-determination and unfinished national liberation, with equality, public provision and electoral mandate central. Historical injustice framing elevates the national claim beyond pure administration.

### Moral Justification – Relevant Elements

#### Mundane Justifications
* Irish unity and self-determination are justified as the democratic right of the Irish people and as the completion of unfinished national liberation.
* Progressive social and economic policy is framed through equality, fairness and public provision.
* Consent and electoral mandate are treated as central legitimating sources in the post-Good Friday institutional context.
* National and democratic claims are advanced together as the proper basis of political authority.
* Concrete improvements in living standards and public services appear as supporting goals.
* Historical injustice and anti-colonial framing supply moral colour that elevates the national claim beyond pure administrative preference.

### Expanded Analysis
Irish unity as democratic right of the Irish people, equality and public provision, consent and electoral mandate, and concrete improvements in living standards organise the moral conversation. Historical injustice and anti-colonial framing elevate the national claim beyond pure administration but do not establish divine command, Cosmic law or a sacralised Ideal independent of demos will. The operational foundation is Mundane: demos mandate, fairness and delivery, with historical-injustice language present.
