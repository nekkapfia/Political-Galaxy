# Cultural 1A: United Kingdom – Sinn Féin
## Foundation Analysis
**Score: 43**

### Overview
Sinn Féin grounds political legitimacy in Irish national self-determination, anti-colonial justice narratives and progressive social-democratic goals pursued through electoral and institutional means. Historical republican destiny language remains part of the party’s moral colour; organising sources in contemporary practice are mixed between demos claims of the Irish people and residual historical-justice framing that stops short of closed revolutionary higher law.

### Moral Justification – Relevant Elements
* Irish unity and self-determination justified as the democratic right of the Irish people and as completion of unfinished national liberation.
* Historical injustice and anti-colonial framing provide moral colour that elevates the national claim beyond pure administrative preference.
* Progressive social and economic policy framed through equality, fairness and public provision within a secular social-democratic register.
* Consent and electoral mandate increasingly central as legitimating sources in the post-Good Friday institutional landscape.
* Criticism of partition and residual British authority cast in both democratic-deficit and historical-justice terms.
* No current organising claim of armed struggle or closed ideological necessity independent of popular consent.

### Expanded Analysis
Contemporary Sinn Féin operates in a mixed justificatory field. The Irish nation as political community entitled to self-determination is the primary demos claim; historical republican and anti-colonial narratives supply transcendent-leaning colour by treating partition as unfinished injustice rather than mere constitutional arrangement. Progressive social policy is largely secular and fairness-based. Since the shift to exclusively peaceful and electoral means, consent and mandate have become organising constraints: the party does not claim authority to impose unity against clear majority preference in the North through higher historical law alone. The result is a mixed profile—stronger historical-justice colour than pure liberal nationalism, weaker than totalising revolutionary ideologies—placing Sinn Féin near the centre of the Cultural 1A band.

### Why this produces a Final Score of 43
Democratic self-determination and progressive fairness organise alongside residual historical-justice framing. Transcendent colour is real but does not displace consent as constraint. The score is 43.
