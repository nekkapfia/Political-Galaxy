## Regulation

### Overview
Alba produces a mid-range Core 1B Regulation profile shaped by Scottish independence ambitions combined with residual UK labour protections and residual green architecture that continue during any transitional phase, plus preference for a social-democratic settlement after independence.

### Relevant Policies / Elements
* Primary objective of Scottish independence; residual UK economic-regulation architecture continues during any transitional phase until replaced by new-state arrangements.
* Retention of ordinary labour protections (unfair dismissal, minimum wage architecture, working-time residual, health-and-safety residual); no high-priority deregulation is locked.
* Retention of residual green/net-zero architecture and major delivery instruments during transition; post-independence preference for continued high environmental and industrial standards rather than repeal.
* Support for stronger collective and social-democratic labour market institutions in an independent Scotland.
* Residual competition-law baseline continues until replaced.
* No high-priority full repeal of residual UK green or labour architecture is locked as the transitional or post-independence pattern.

### Expanded Analysis
During any transitional independence process the residual UK labour-protection and residual green architecture continue. Post-independence the preferred settlement retains strong labour protections and high environmental/industrial standards rather than a deregulatory break. Social-democratic collective institutions are advanced. The institutional pattern is residual continuity of labour and green restrictions during transition, followed by a social-democratic rather than liberalising economic-regulation settlement in the new state.

### Regulation Score
Retention of residual labour protections and residual green architecture, combined with preference for stronger collective institutions rather than deregulation, produces a Regulation Score of 52.
