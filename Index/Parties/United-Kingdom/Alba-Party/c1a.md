# Cultural 1A: United Kingdom – Alba Party
## Foundation Analysis
**Score: 26**

### Overview
Alba grounds political legitimacy in Scottish independence as a democratic national right and in criticism of both Westminster authority and SNP progressive institutional orthodoxy. Organising sources are mundane: demos, national-democratic self-government and practical governing critique. Transcendent sacred, ecological-override or closed ideological claims are absent.

### Moral Justification – Relevant Elements
* Scottish independence justified strictly as democratic self-determination of the Scottish people through consent and mandate.
* Criticism of SNP governance framed through competence, democratic accountability, policy failure and neglect of independence priority—not through sacred or higher-law categories.
* Social and cultural positions argued through democratic preference and national interest rather than sacred authority or systemic justice as higher law.
* Rejection of Westminster final authority as a democratic and constitutional claim of the Scottish demos.
* No organising role for divine command, natural law as override, ecological duty as demos-binding higher law, or revolutionary historical necessity.
* Electoral mandate and practical delivery retained as central legitimating sources.

### Expanded Analysis
Scored as an individual party, Alba is a pure national-democratic independence vehicle. Independence is the right of the living Scottish demos to decide its constitutional future; critique of the SNP is competence, priority and accountability language; critique of Westminster is democratic deficit. Social and cultural positions are demos- and interest-framed. There is no residual transcendent architecture—no sacred nation, no ecological higher law, no closed socialist historical necessity—that organises moral justification independently of Scottish popular preference. The pattern is therefore strong mundane lean, slightly more austere than the SNP’s social-democratic colouring, and fully consistent with scoring self-determination claims as ordinary popular sovereignty of a territorial demos rather than as particularist-transcendent mission.

### Why this produces a Final Score of 26
Democratic self-determination and governing competence as sole organising sources, without sacred or ideological higher law, produce a strong mundane lean. Treated as a unique party, the score is 26.
