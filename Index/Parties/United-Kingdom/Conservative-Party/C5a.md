# Cultural 5A: United Kingdom – Conservative Party
## Tradition vs Progress Analysis
**Score: 52**

### Overview
The Conservatives combine selective traditionalist signalling on heritage and national history with acceptance of the post-1960s progressive settlement in education content, family status and institutional culture.

### Cultural 5A Relevant Policies / Elements
* Current platform retains post-Brexit institutional sovereignty commitments while accepting the broader post-1960s settlement on education content frameworks and media regulation.
* Heritage protection and selective national-history content appear in platform language without systematic reversal of progressive curriculum frameworks.
* Family and status rules largely leave the post-1990s modernisation settlement in place.
* Rhetorical and some programmatic pushback on progressive education and cultural orthodoxy without dismantling primary progressive transmission mechanisms.
* Residual progressive settlement on curriculum framing, family-status design and public-sector cultural guidance remains largely operative.
* Continuity pressure is selective (heritage, some history content, sovereignty language) rather than baseline restoration across education and family law.

### Expanded Analysis
The Conservatives occupy contested mid ground. Heritage protection and selective national-history language raise preservationist signal, and post-Brexit sovereignty is framed as cultural self-government capacity. At the same time, primary progressive transmission mechanisms—curriculum frameworks, family-status settlement, much public-sector cultural guidance—are not systematically dismantled. The residual progressive settlement remains the operative default in education content and family status. Domain hierarchy shows mixed primary domains: limited continuity pressure in history and heritage, acceptance of progressive residual frameworks in family law and curriculum architecture. That is more continuity-oriented than Labour/Lib Dems/Greens, less so than Reform or Restore Britain’s systematic reversal demands.

### Why this produces a Final Score of 52
Selective traditionalist signalling on heritage and national history, combined with acceptance of the post-1960s settlement in education content and family status and without systematic dismantling of progressive transmission mechanisms, places the Conservatives in the contested mid band (51–59). Mid-high placement would require traditional transmission to be institutionalised as the protected default in curriculum and family-status law.
