# Core 2A: United Kingdom – Conservative Party
## National Sovereignty Analysis
**Score: 48**

### Overview
The Conservatives pursue stronger border enforcement and higher removals while remaining inside the ECHR and Windsor Framework, offering incremental rather than structural recovery of control. External legal pooling of authority continues to constrain unilateral sovereign capacity.

### National Sovereignty Relevant Policies / Elements
* Supports increased enforcement against small-boat crossings and higher deportation numbers.
* Favours reform of the UK’s relationship with the ECHR to reduce barriers to removal while remaining a signatory; no high-priority full withdrawal is locked.
* Maintains the Windsor Framework with only limited adjustments; Northern Ireland external layer continues.
* Operates a points-based legal immigration system with relatively high volumes in key sectors.
* Supports continued NATO membership and most existing international trade arrangements; residual post-Brexit assimilated law and TCA constraints continue unless actively rewritten.

### Expanded Analysis
Conservative policy improves operational enforcement against small-boat crossings and raises deportation numbers while leaving the ECHR and the Windsor Framework in place as standing external constraints. Reform of the ECHR relationship is sought to ease removals, but withdrawal is not proposed and jurisdiction continues. The points-based legal immigration system continues at relatively high volumes in key sectors. NATO membership and existing major trade arrangements are retained. Post-Brexit external pooling of authority is not dismantled. The institutional pattern on Core 2A is incremental operational recovery of control on irregular routes without structural removal of the principal external legal and territorial constraints. ECHR and Windsor Framework remain primary drag on sovereign capacity.

### Why this produces a Final Score of 48
Stronger operational enforcement against irregular crossings is paired with retention of the ECHR and Windsor Framework. External legal pooling of authority and continued high managed inflows limit unilateral sovereign control, placing the final set inside the 41–50 Low–Mid band. Constraints that continue are explicit in the locked analysis. The score of 48 reflects limited recovery of operational control offset by standing external pooling of authority rather than structural exit from the principal architectures.
