# Core 1A: United Kingdom – Conservative Party
## Personal Autonomy Analysis
**Score: 57**

### Overview

The Conservative Party’s governing programme produces a mid-range Core 1A profile built from retention of the Online Safety Act’s illegal-content and child-protection architecture, Public Order Act Section 5, Racial and Religious Hatred Act provisions, and communications offences, without new identity-specific speech offences or election-period platform amplification duties. The final set is essentially the post-2023 UK baseline without significant intensification and without major liberalisation. No Endos/Xenos split applies.

### Core 1A Relevant Policies / Elements

* Online Safety Act 2023 retained for illegal content and child-protection duties, with emphasis on age assurance and platform accountability for clearly illegal material. No full repeal agenda of the Reform UK type; core regulatory architecture remains.
* Public Order Act 1986 Section 5 (harassment, alarm or distress) and Racial and Religious Hatred Act stirring-up offences maintained as baseline legal order.
* Communications Act 2003 and Malicious Communications Act 1988 offences accepted where they target intentional threats or false communications intended to cause harm; discomfort signalled with over-broad application to non-threatening speech.
* Support for reform or curtailment of non-crime hate-incident recording; administrative narrowing rather than repeal of primary speech offences.
* Opposition to new election-period social-media amplification or “trusted news” prominence duties.
* No new identity-based speech offences; no expansion of the existing stirring-up framework.
* No wholesale repeal of core hate-speech or public-order statutes.

### Expanded Analysis

The party retains the decisive post-2023 layers: Online Safety Act illegal-content and child-protection duties, Section 5 and related public-order speech offences, and communications offences criminalising certain non-violent expression. It does not add Labour’s concrete expansions (election-period platform duties, anti-Muslim hostility definition, under-16 measures as central push) and does not add Green-style mandatory attitude-correction or dense identity-orthodoxy layers. It also does not remove the major speech-restriction layers in the manner of Reform UK.

Administrative discomfort with non-crime hate-incident recording is modest process-level push-back; it does not eliminate underlying primary offences. Opposition to election-period amplification duties prevents further densification but does not lighten the existing set.

No stable, discretely targetable out-group differential is formalised by Conservative policy, so no Endos/Xenos split applies.

The architecture sits above the progressive densification cluster and below Reform UK / Restore Britain. Cumulative weight is mid-range — post-2023 baseline without major movement in either direction.
