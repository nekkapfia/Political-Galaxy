# Cultural 5A: United Kingdom – Democratic Unionist Party
## Tradition vs Progress Analysis
**Score: 67**

### Overview
The DUP defends traditional marriage definitions, conventional family authority and Ulster-Protestant cultural continuity against progressive education and status reforms.

### Cultural 5A Relevant Policies / Elements
* Opposition to redefinition of marriage and to progressive family-status reforms; conventional family authority defended in law and public policy.
* Resistance to progressive education content on gender and sexuality where it conflicts with religious and communal norms.
* Public ritual and commemoration treated as markers of Ulster-Protestant cultural continuity requiring protection.
* Operates inside the post-Good Friday institutional settlement while treating traditional family and confessional forms as baselines requiring protection in high-weight domains.
* Residual progressive UK-wide settlement on marriage and gender education is contested in Northern Ireland policy and party programme rather than accepted as permanent default.
* Confessional and family transmission prioritised in primary domains; material heritage secondary.

### Expanded Analysis
The DUP treats conventional family authority and confessional-cultural continuity as baselines requiring protection in primary domains. Marriage definition and family-status rules are defended against progressive redesign; education content on gender and sexuality is resisted where it conflicts with religious and communal norms; public ritual marks Ulster-Protestant continuity. The residual progressive UK-wide settlement is not simply accepted—it is contested in the domains the party prioritises. Domain hierarchy places family-status law and education content first, with ritual and communal continuity supporting. Operating inside the post-Good Friday settlement constrains full restorationist scope, but on high-weight domains the pattern is mid-high preservation rather than selective signalling only.

### Why this produces a Final Score of 67
Opposition to marriage redefinition and progressive family-status reforms, resistance to progressive gender/sexuality education content, and treatment of ritual and confessional continuity as protected baselines place the DUP in the mid-high band (66–79). The residual progressive settlement is contested in primary domains. Lower contested placement would understate institutional defence of traditional family and confessional transmission.
