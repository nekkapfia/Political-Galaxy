# Core 2A: United Kingdom – Democratic Unionist Party
## National Sovereignty Analysis
**Score: 56**

### Overview
The DUP prioritises removal of the Windsor Framework and restoration of UK internal market integrity while supporting continued ECHR and NATO membership. External pooling of authority outside the NI-specific layer continues.

### National Sovereignty Relevant Policies / Elements
* Strong opposition to the Windsor Framework and the Irish Sea border; demands its removal.
* Demands restoration of unfettered internal UK trade and the removal of residual EU regulatory alignment requirements for Northern Ireland.
* Supports the Union and resists any arrangements that differentiate Northern Ireland from the rest of the UK.
* Supports continued UK membership of NATO and existing major international organisations.
* Supports continued UK membership of the ECHR while prioritising removal of the Windsor Framework; ECHR jurisdiction continues.

### Expanded Analysis
The DUP’s primary sovereignty claim is removal of the Windsor Framework and restoration of an undivided UK internal market and regulatory space between Great Britain and Northern Ireland. Continued membership of the ECHR and of NATO is accepted; the party does not organise around a broader programme of exit from external legal regimes. The focus is territorial integrity of the existing United Kingdom rather than external institutional disentanglement across the board. Post-Brexit pooling of authority outside the NI layer continues. The institutional pattern on Core 2A is concentrated recovery of internal territorial and regulatory unity, without structural challenge to other standing external constraints. ECHR remains primary drag outside the Windsor-specific recovery.

### Why this produces a Final Score of 56
Scrapping the Windsor Framework restores internal territorial and regulatory unity while the ECHR and other external architectures remain. External pooling of authority outside the NI-specific layer is explicit. The final set therefore sits inside the 51–59 Mid–Lower band. The score of 56 reflects partial recovery of sovereign control over the UK’s internal space offset by continued external legal pooling of authority.
