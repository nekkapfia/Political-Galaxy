# Core 2A: United Kingdom – Plaid Cymru
## National Sovereignty Analysis
**Score: 52**

### Overview
Plaid Cymru prioritises Welsh independence and the establishment of a fully sovereign Welsh state, with European membership or alignment as the preferred external framework after the change of status. The pattern is high formal sovereignty through new statehood, immediately followed by deliberate re-pooling of authority through European architecture.

### National Sovereignty Relevant Policies / Elements
* Primary objective is Welsh independence and the establishment of a fully sovereign Welsh state ending Westminster control over Welsh affairs.
* Supports European alignment / EU membership as the preferred post-independence external architecture.
* UK external constraints (ECHR/Human Rights Act architecture, residual assimilated law, Trade and Cooperation Agreement obligations, Windsor Framework residual where relevant) continue during any transitional process until replaced by the new state’s arrangements.
* No high-priority programme of external isolation or non-European alternative framework is locked; European pooling of authority is the intended post-change pattern.
* NATO membership and multilateral treaty residual continue unless explicitly altered.

### Expanded Analysis
Plaid Cymru’s final set removes Westminster control through Welsh independence and the creation of a new sovereign state. During any transitional phase the continuing UK external architecture (ECHR jurisdiction, residual assimilated law, TCA constraints and Windsor Framework residual where applicable) remains in force until the new state replaces it. Once the change of status is achieved the preferred external framework is European membership or alignment, which immediately re-pools significant authority over law, borders, trade, monetary policy and institutional decision-making. The institutional pattern on Core 2A is therefore high formal sovereignty of new statehood offset by deliberate re-pooling through European architecture. Transitional external constraints and post-change European pooling of authority form the locked final set; neither is omitted. Primary drag arises from the re-pooling step rather than from incomplete formal independence.

### Why this produces a Final Score of 52
Independence produces high formal sovereignty. Immediate re-pooling through European architecture constitutes primary drag that places the final set inside the 51–59 Mid–Lower band. Transitional UK external constraints and post-change European pooling of authority are explicit in the locked analysis. The score of 52 follows from high formal sovereignty immediately followed by deliberate external pooling of core authority rather than sustained high closure.
