# Core 2A: United Kingdom – Liberal Democrats
## National Sovereignty Analysis
**Score: 37**
**Status: FULLY LOCKED**

### Overview
The Liberal Democrats prioritise expanded safe and legal asylum routes and retention of the ECHR and Windsor Framework, accepting lower unilateral control in favour of managed international cooperation. Residual external legal pooling of authority is treated as the preferred baseline rather than a target for removal.

### National Sovereignty Relevant Policies / Elements
* Core policy is significant expansion of safe and legal asylum routes as the primary mechanism for managing inflows and reducing irregular crossings.
* Supports continued UK membership of the ECHR and its domestic incorporation; residual external legal jurisdiction continues.
* Supports maintenance of the Windsor Framework; residual Northern Ireland external regulatory layer continues.
* Supports closer UK alignment with European regulatory and rights frameworks; residual post-Brexit pooling of authority is retained and in places extended.
* Supports continued NATO membership and existing major trade arrangements; residual alliance and treaty architecture continues.

### Expanded Analysis
The Liberal Democrats make expansion of safe and legal asylum routes the core migration policy and retain both the ECHR and the Windsor Framework as standing residual external constraints. Irregular crossings are expected to fall mainly through substitution into legal channels rather than through new high-intensity deterrent enforcement. Closer alignment with European regulatory and rights frameworks is supported. Post-Brexit residual external pooling of authority is not dismantled and is treated as the preferred settlement. The institutional pattern on Core 2A is deliberate retention and pooling of the main residual external legal and territorial constraints, combined with higher managed migration volumes. Residual ECHR jurisdiction and the residual Windsor Framework constitute primary drag on unilateral sovereign capacity; secondary residual NATO and trade architecture is overpowered while those dominant residual constraints remain.

### Why this produces a Final Score of 37
Expanded legal asylum routes and retention of the residual ECHR and residual Windsor Framework emphasise managed international cooperation over unilateral border and legal control. Residual external legal pooling of authority is explicit and preferred. Primary drag from the continuing residual external architectures places the final set inside the 31–40 Low band. The score of 37 follows from prioritisation of residual international integration and higher managed inflows rather than structural recovery of residual unilateral control.
