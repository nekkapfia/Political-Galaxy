# Cultural 5A: Fascism – Garda de Fier
## Tradition vs Progress Analysis
**Score: 71**

### Overview
The Iron Guard opposed liberal and secular cultural modernisation while elevating Orthodox Christianity and peasant-folk moral forms as the required public standard.

### Cultural 5A Relevant Policies / Elements
* Organised movement programme around Orthodox Christian doctrine and ritual as the required moral framework for political life.
* Promoted peasant social forms, folk culture and rural norms as the legitimate moral standard against urban liberal and secular models.
* Treated liberal, secular and cosmopolitan institutions and cultural currents as targets for removal from public authority.
* Directed political organisation and violence against liberal-parliamentary and secular elites rather than against Orthodox or peasant institutional forms.
* Rejected independent liberal-secular cultural authority in doctrine and practice.
* Left Orthodox Church authority and peasant customary forms outside the movement's dismantling programme; opposition focused on liberal modernisation and secular state culture.
* Residual liberal-secular modernisation project was the enemy; Orthodox and rural-traditional norms were the intended public standard.

### Expanded Analysis
On Cultural 5A the Iron Guard's primary domains were oriented toward Orthodox Christian and peasant-folk moral transmission as the required public standard. Education and cultural life were to be framed by religious doctrine and rural customary norms; liberal-secular and urban cosmopolitan authority was targeted for removal. Political organisation and violence were directed at liberal-parliamentary and secular elites, not at Church or village institutional forms. The residual liberal-secular modernisation project was treated as the problem to be displaced, not as a baseline to conserve. Domain hierarchy places confessional and customary transmission first: Orthodoxy and peasant norms were the intended operating system of public morality, not instruments subordinated to a secular party ideology. That distinguishes the pattern from regimes that used traditional imagery while extinguishing autonomous traditional authority. Material heritage and folk performance support the pattern but do not define it; education framing and moral authority structure do.

### Why this produces a Final Score of 71
Elevation of Orthodox Christianity and peasant-folk norms as the required public standard, combined with systematic opposition to liberal-secular modernisation and protection of Church and rural institutional forms from the movement's dismantling programme, places the Iron Guard in the mid-high band (66–79). Contested mid placement would understate the treatment of traditional religious and customary transmission as the operative public baseline. The pattern is traditionalist-religious restoration against progressive-secular modernisation, not regime substitution of tradition.
