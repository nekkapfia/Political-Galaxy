# Core 2B: Fascism – Garda de Fier
## International Sovereignty Analysis
**Score: 50**

### Overview
The Iron Guard / Legionary movement exercised limited and brief state power. External military projection and systematic interference in other states’ internal orders were minimal. Higher placement than full state fascist regimes reflects constrained capacity, duration, and primary focus on domestic transformation. Residual capacity for sustained cross-border control was never institutionalised.

### Relevant Policies / Elements
* Brief and contested periods of influence or power within Romania (notably the National Legionary State period 1940–41).
* Primary focus on domestic political violence, ideological purification and internal transformation inside Romania.
* Limited independent capacity for cross-border occupation, sustained proxy control or large-scale foreign regime-change operations.
* Alignment with Axis currents and participation in the broader wartime coalition without independently designing multi-country occupation systems.
* Residual baseline of domestic priority; external projection remained secondary and capacity-constrained.
* Self-defence of Romanian territory is out of scope for Core 2B.

### Expanded Analysis
Core 2B measures interference in other states’ internal affairs. The Iron Guard’s short and contested periods of power did not produce a sustained architecture of external control comparable to Nazi or later Soviet satellite systems. Domestic purification and internal violence dominated the record. Axis alignment existed, but the movement did not institutionalise independent multi-country occupation or permanent proxy regimes. Capacity and duration therefore limit the downward pull relative to longer-ruling, higher-capacity fascist states.

### Why this produces a Final Score of 50
Constrained duration, limited independent external-projection capacity, and primary domestic focus produce a mid-range score above the floor-level occupation regimes.
