## Regulation

### Overview
The Iron Guard in its periods of influence and brief governing participation pushed for radical nationalisation rhetoric, confiscation from designated groups, and subordination of economic activity to legionary political goals, within a residual Romanian state still partially constrained by royal and later military authority.

### Relevant Policies / Elements
* Romanianisation as forced transfer of property and businesses from designated groups to the “nation” (confiscatory, not ordinary regulation)
* Hostility to independent large-scale private and foreign capital
* Subordination of economic activity to national-political goals
* Suppression of independent economic organisation of designated groups
* Corporatist and command elements in the proposed economic order
* Residual private activity only under tight political and ethnic-national constraints

### Expanded Analysis
The Iron Guard in its periods of influence and brief governing participation pushed for radical nationalisation rhetoric, confiscation from designated groups, and subordination of economic activity to legionary political goals, within a residual Romanian state still partially constrained by royal and later military authority. Corporatist or syndicalist instruments directed labour and association. Administrative controls limited firm-level choices in strategic sectors. Independent unions were suppressed. Where confiscation from designated groups was locked, it operated as a confiscatory mechanism rather than ordinary regulation. Residual private title and market activity continued under political constraint but did not restore independent decision rights in the commanding heights. The institutional pattern is extensive state and party direction organised around national-political objectives, short of total extinction of private title.

### Regulation Score
The locked regulation instruments produce a Regulation Score of 18.
