## Regulation

### Overview
Italian Fascism organised the economy through the corporate state, state holding companies (IRI and related entities after the early-1930s banking crisis), autarky drives, compulsory fascist syndicates, and extensive regulation of labour and production. Private ownership formally persisted, but independent decision-making in the commanding heights and in collective labour organisation was removed.

### Relevant Policies
- Corporatist organisation of industry and labour under state oversight (Corporazioni; Charter of Labour)
- IRI and related state holdings — decisive state ownership/control in banking, steel, shipping, telecommunications and other strategic sectors after the early 1930s
- Compulsory membership in fascist syndicates; independent unions and collective bargaining abolished
- Autarky drives and import-substitution campaigns with foreign-exchange and investment controls
- Wage and price controls
- Production campaigns (e.g. Grain Battle) with administrative pressure on agricultural output
- Priority of regime political and military objectives in investment and production
- Formal private ownership retained outside the state-holding core, subject to corporate and regulatory direction

### Expanded Analysis
IRI’s weight after the banking crisis made the state the dominant owner/controller in large segments of industry and finance. Compulsory syndicate membership eliminated independent labour organisation. Autarky and exchange controls limited firm-level trade and investment choices. Production campaigns added administrative pressure on agriculture. Relative to lower bands: Italy did not abolish private title or money, and residual market activity in non-strategic sectors remained larger than under Stalinism or wartime Nazism. The combination of commanding-heights state ownership, syndicate monopoly and autarkic controls nonetheless places the package inside extensive state direction organised around regime goals, not at the top of that range.
