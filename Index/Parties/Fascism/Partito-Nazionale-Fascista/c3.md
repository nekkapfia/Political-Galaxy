### Overview
Italian Fascism prioritised political loyalty and Italian cultural conformity over blood for most of its history. Only in 1938 did the Manifesto of Race and subsequent laws introduce antisemitic ancestral criteria, later and milder than the German model. Full trusted membership required immersion in Fascist political culture, loyalty to the Duce, and abandonment of anti-Fascist and liberal identities. Immigration and deportation remain out of scope.

### Relevant Policies / Elements
* Pre-1938: membership defined by political loyalty and Italian cultural alignment; no systematic racial blood bar.
* 1938 Manifesto of Race and racial laws: introduced ancestral criteria targeting Jews, elevating ethnic barriers without full one-drop exclusion across all domains.
* Compulsory youth organisations, public ritual, and cultural institutions requiring Fascist political-cultural immersion.
* Anti-Fascist, liberal and socialist identities treated as incompatible with full membership.
* Immigration and deportation out of scope.

### Expanded Analysis
