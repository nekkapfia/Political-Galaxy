# Cultural 2B: Fascism – FET y de las JONS
## National Pride Analysis
**Score: 86**

### Overview
FET y de las JONS organised Spanish public life around the affirmation of the nation, its Catholic and imperial past, and the Francoist order. National pride was the clear institutional default; systematic shame frameworks were absent. Residual baseline of strong national affirmation: symbols, education and commemorations treat national continuity and achievement as the default frame rather than an object of systematic critique.

### Relevant Policies / Elements
* Education and media celebrated Spanish national continuity, empire and Catholic identity.
* Symbols, monuments and rituals affirmed the nation as central and legitimate.
* Criticism of the national project was constrained or punished.
* Cultural and historical narrative subordinated to national-Catholic affirmation.
* Traditional and imperial history treated as sources of pride rather than of obligatory atonement.
* Residual baseline of strong national affirmation: symbols, education and commemorations treat national continuity and achievement as the default frame rather than an object of systematic critique.

### Expanded Analysis
Francoist Spain under FET produced a high Pride-band score. The nation was treated as a primary source of legitimacy and meaning. Organising shame narratives toward the Spanish nation itself were absent; critical frames, where they existed, were directed at internal enemies or external threats rather than at the nation as such.

### Why this produces a Final Score of 86
Systematic affirmation of national continuity and rejection of organising national shame place FET high in the Pride band.
