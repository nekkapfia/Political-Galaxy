## Regulation

### Overview
Francoist Spain under FET combined authoritarian corporatism, a long autarkic phase (roughly 1939–1959) with extreme licensing and multiple exchange rates, state enterprises (INI), and vertical syndicates that banned independent unions. After 1959 controlled liberalisation increased residual market activity while authoritarian labour controls and political constraints remained.

### Relevant Policies
- Vertical syndicates — compulsory corporatist labour organisation; independent unions banned for the duration of the regime
- INI and related state enterprises — major state presence in industry and energy
- Early autarky (1939–1959): extreme import licensing, multiple exchange rates, investment controls, import-substitution
- Suppression of independent unions and political economic organisation throughout
- Extensive licensing and regulatory controls on enterprise (especially pre-1959)
- Post-1959 controlled opening to foreign investment and markets while retaining vertical-syndicate labour control
- Private ownership significant but under political and (especially early) regulatory constraint

### Expanded Analysis
The early autarkic decades featured high state direction, extreme licensing and vertical-syndicate monopoly over labour organisation. INI gave the state a major industrial footprint. Independent unions never returned. Later liberalisation expanded residual market activity and foreign capital but did not restore independent collective labour organisation or remove political constraints on economic association. Relative to lower bands: Spain retained a large private sector and never abolished money or ordinary private title. Averaging the full regime softens the severity of 1939–1959; if the scored character weights the long autarkic phase, the package sits more clearly inside extensive state direction rather than at the boundary with lighter corporatism.
