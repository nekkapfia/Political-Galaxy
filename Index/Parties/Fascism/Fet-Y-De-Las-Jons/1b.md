## Regulation

### Overview
FET y de las JONS under early Francoism organised the Spanish economy around autarky, syndicalist direction of labour, price and trade controls, and expanding state intervention in strategic sectors, while formal private title remained in many areas under heavy administrative constraint.

### Relevant Policies / Elements
* Vertical syndicates — compulsory corporatist labour organisation; independent unions banned for the duration of the regime
* INI and related state enterprises — major state presence in industry and energy
* Early autarky (1939–1959): extreme import licensing, multiple exchange rates, investment controls, import-substitution
* Suppression of independent unions and political economic organisation throughout
* Extensive licensing and regulatory controls on enterprise (especially pre-1959)
* Post-1959 controlled opening to foreign investment and markets while retaining vertical-syndicate labour control
* Private ownership significant but under political and (especially early) regulatory constraint

### Expanded Analysis
FET y de las JONS under early Francoism organised the Spanish economy around autarky, syndicalist direction of labour, price and trade controls, and expanding state intervention in strategic sectors, while formal private title remained in many areas under heavy administrative constraint. Corporatist or syndicalist instruments directed labour and association. Administrative controls limited firm-level choices in strategic sectors. Independent unions were suppressed. Where confiscation from designated groups was locked, it operated as a confiscatory mechanism rather than ordinary regulation. Residual private title and market activity continued under political constraint but did not restore independent decision rights in the commanding heights. The institutional pattern is extensive state and party direction organised around national-political objectives, short of total extinction of private title.
