# Cultural 4A: Fascism – Fet Y De Las Jons
## Social Determinism Analysis
**Score: 54**

### Overview
Francoist Spain under FET y de las JONS combined corporatist organisation with traditional status elements and limited hereditary or origin-based advantages. Formal mobility channels remained open relative to the radical racial regimes; political reliability and regime loyalty mattered more than pure birth assignment. The system produced moderate residual constraint characteristic of the noticeable-shift range.

### Relevant Policies / Elements
* Corporatist and traditional status structures that preserved certain inherited advantages in land, profession, and local hierarchy.
* Political reliability and regime loyalty as gates on advancement within state and Movement structures.
* Formal educational and occupational pathways that remained relatively open compared with totalising hereditary systems.
* Residual class and family advantages in access to elite education and professional networks.
* Absence of a comprehensive racial or multi-generational political classification system of the Nuremberg or songbun type.

### Expanded Analysis
Cultural 4A scores the degree to which birth circumstances constrain outcomes. FET/Francoism retained traditional status elements and used political loyalty as a filter, but it did not institutionalise locked hereditary occupational assignment across the population. Formal mobility through education and professional routes continued. The pattern fits the 51–59 band: inter-class and intra-class mobility were realistic yet still shaped by early advantages, family background, and regime loyalty. Soft statistical class correlation without institutional gates would score lower; the combination of residual traditional status and political filters supplies the moderate determinism.

Cultural 4B equity diagnostics are out of scope.

### Why this produces a Final Score of 54
Traditional status residues and political-loyalty gates, combined with relatively open formal mobility channels, align with the 51–59 band. Score 54.
