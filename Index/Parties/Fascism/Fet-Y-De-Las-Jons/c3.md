# Cultural 3: Fascism – FET y de las JONS (Francoist Spain)
## Ethnic and Cultural Exclusivity Analysis
**Score: 3A 52 | 3B 72**

### Overview
Francoist Spain defined membership primarily through National-Catholic and Spanish cultural criteria rather than systematic blood laws. Some racial and antisemitic elements existed, especially early and under Falange influence, but never reached Nuremberg-style ancestral exclusion. Full trusted membership required strong cultural and religious conformity to the Movimiento and Catholic orthodoxy, including suppression of regional languages and cultures. Immigration and deportation remain out of scope.

### Relevant Policies / Elements
* National-Catholic definition of Spanishness; preference for Spanish ancestry and Catholic culture without formal one-drop racial legislation.
* Limited racial and antisemitic elements under Falange influence, insufficient to create permanent ethnic bars on the German model.
* Requirement of political loyalty to the Movimiento and Catholic cultural conformity for full trusted membership.
* Suppression of Catalan, Basque and other regional cultural expression as part of cultural homogenisation.
* Immigration and deportation out of scope.

### Expanded Analysis
The Exclusion Test is only weakly met. No ethnicity was permanently barred by a formal blood code comparable to Nuremberg. Ancestral and cultural preference elevated barriers for some groups, but naturalisation and incorporation remained possible under National-Catholic terms. This places 3A in the Minimal Ancestry Threshold to soft preference range (52).

3B is predominantly demanding. Trusted membership required demonstrable alignment with National-Catholic culture, political loyalty, and acceptance of cultural homogenisation. Partial adoption was insufficient for full standing within the regime’s core community.

### Why this produces 3A 52 / 3B 72
3A aligns with 51–59 (minimal ancestry threshold / soft preference without systematic blood exclusion). 3B aligns with 70–79 (predominantly demanding cultural and religious conformity).
