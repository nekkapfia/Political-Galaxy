## Regulation

### Overview
The Vaterländische Front under the Austrian Ständestaat organised the economy around corporatist chambers, suppression of independent unions and parties, and authoritarian direction of labour and association, while private ownership and market mechanisms remained more extensive than under full Fascist or Nazi models.

### Relevant Policies / Elements
* Ständestaat corporatist organisation of economic life
* Suppression of independent trade unions and socialist/social-democratic economic organisation
* Estate-based professional chambers under regime oversight
* Significant state regulation of labour, prices and enterprise
* Private ownership retained under political constraints
* No material-balance central planning of the Stalinist type; residual market allocation in many sectors
* No systematic mass forced labour or ethnic confiscation programme on the Nazi/Arrow Cross scale

### Expanded Analysis
The Vaterländische Front under the Austrian Ständestaat organised the economy around corporatist chambers, suppression of independent unions and parties, and authoritarian direction of labour and association, while private ownership and market mechanisms remained more extensive than under full Fascist or Nazi models. Corporatist or syndicalist instruments directed labour and association. Administrative controls limited firm-level choices in strategic sectors. Independent unions were suppressed. Where confiscation from designated groups was locked, it operated as a confiscatory mechanism rather than ordinary regulation. Residual private title and market activity continued under political constraint but did not restore independent decision rights in the commanding heights. The institutional pattern is extensive state and party direction organised around national-political objectives, short of total extinction of private title.
