## Regulation

### Overview
Austrofascism under the Vaterländische Front (1934–1938) organised economic life through the Ständestaat: independent unions and socialist economic organisation were suppressed; estate-based professional chambers operated under regime oversight; labour, prices and enterprise were subject to significant state regulation. A large formal private sector remained.

### Relevant Policies
- Ständestaat corporatist organisation of economic life
- Suppression of independent trade unions and socialist/social-democratic economic organisation
- Estate-based professional chambers under regime oversight
- Significant state regulation of labour, prices and enterprise
- Private ownership retained under political constraints
- No material-balance central planning of the Stalinist type; residual market allocation in many sectors
- No systematic mass forced labour or ethnic confiscation programme on the Nazi/Arrow Cross scale

### Expanded Analysis
Independent collective labour organisation was eliminated and replaced by regime-controlled chambers. Price and labour regulation constrained firm and worker choices. The private sector remained large and money/markets continued. Relative to lower bands: the system lacked near-total state ownership, forced labour systems, and confiscatory capital transfers of the more severe regimes. The mechanical core is authoritarian corporatist constraint on voluntary organisation, not command ownership of the productive apparatus. Placement at the upper edge of extensive control / lower edge of lighter corporatist constraint remains coherent; no floor-band mechanism is present.
