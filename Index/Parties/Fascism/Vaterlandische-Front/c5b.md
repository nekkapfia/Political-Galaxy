# Cultural 5B: Fascism – Vaterländische Front
## Radicalism (Pace of Change) Analysis
**Score: 40**

### Overview

The Vaterländische Front and Austrofascist regime emphasised authoritarian stabilisation, corporatist order, and defence of a particular traditional-authoritarian settlement more than continuous revolutionary acceleration. While it suppressed opposition and restructured political life, its characteristic tempo was closer to stabilising authoritarianism than to high-radicalism permanent transformation.

### Observable Pace and Intensity

* Rapid suppression of socialist and Nazi opposition organisations in the foundational phase.
* Construction of a single-party/corporatist framework that replaced pluralist competition.
* Subsequent emphasis on order, continuity, and controlled institutionalisation rather than repeated disruptive campaigns.
* Limited evidence of continuous high-volume transformative initiatives after the initial consolidation.
* Observable pattern is authoritarian consolidation followed by relative stabilisation of tempo.

### Expanded Analysis

Austrofascism lacks the sustained campaign intensity or continuous institutional rupture that defines scores in the 80s and 90s. Its primary orientation was defence and stabilisation of a new authoritarian order. That places it in the low-to-mid band, consistent with systems that use rapid means for initial consolidation but then prioritise continuity over ongoing radical acceleration.

### Why this produces a Final Score of 40

Initial rapid consolidation followed by stabilising authoritarian governance, with limited continuous disruptive tempo, places the Vaterländische Front at 40.
