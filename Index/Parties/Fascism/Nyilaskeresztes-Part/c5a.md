# Cultural 5A: Fascism – Nyilaskeresztes Párt
## Tradition vs Progress Analysis
**Score: 41**

### Overview
The Arrow Cross attacked liberal and established institutional arrangements during a short period of violent rule while using Christian and national rhetoric without stabilising church or customary structures through sustained policy.

### Cultural 5A Relevant Policies / Elements
* Seized state institutions during a brief period of power and directed eliminationist violence against Jews and against political opponents including liberal and established conservative figures.
* Used Christian and national-historical language in propaganda while subjecting existing institutional structures to radical personnel purge and terror.
* Did not enact a sustained legislative programme stabilising pre-existing church, family or customary institutions as autonomous legal baselines.
* Removed liberal and established conservative office-holders; disrupted inherited administrative continuity through short-run violent restructuring.
* Treated prior liberal-parliamentary and Horthy-era institutional arrangements as targets for takeover rather than as frameworks to conserve.
* Residual institutional continuity from the prior order was broken by purge and terror rather than protected as the public default.

### Expanded Analysis
On Cultural 5A the Arrow Cross presents rhetoric without consolidation. Christian and national language pointed at a longer Hungarian baseline, but practice during its short rule did not stabilise church, family or customary institutions through education content, family-status law or durable institutional design. Primary domains were disrupted: administrative continuity was purged; established conservative and liberal office-holders were removed; no sustained programme made confessional or customary transmission the operative legal baseline. The residual prior order—Horthy-era and liberal-parliamentary arrangements—was treated as material for takeover, not as continuity to defend. Domain hierarchy therefore shows attack without restoration: education and family transmission were not reconstructed as traditional baselines; institutional design was chaotic and eliminationist. Traditionalist rhetoric cannot outweigh the absence of institutional stabilisation on the high-weight domains.

### Why this produces a Final Score of 41
Brief violent disruption of liberal and established arrangements, combined with Christian/national rhetoric but no sustained stabilisation of church, family or customary institutions as legal baselines, places the Arrow Cross in the contested low-mid band (41–50). Higher mid-high placement would require evidence that traditional transmission was actually institutionalised as the public default; the locked analysis shows the opposite.
