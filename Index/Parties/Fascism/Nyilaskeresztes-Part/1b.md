## Regulation

### Overview
The Arrow Cross in its brief governing period organised the Hungarian economy around radical confiscation from designated groups, forced labour, and subordination of production to wartime and regime goals, extinguishing residual private decision-making for targeted populations.

### Relevant Policies / Elements
* Radical and rapid confiscation of economic assets from designated groups
* Forced labour and administrative/militia labour allocation
* Subordination of residual production to wartime and regime goals
* Collapse of ordinary commercial law and private contracting in practice
* Suppression of any remaining independent economic organisation
* Near-extinction of residual private economic decision-making during the regime window

### Expanded Analysis
The Arrow Cross in its brief governing period organised the Hungarian economy around radical confiscation from designated groups, forced labour, and subordination of production to wartime and regime goals, extinguishing residual private decision-making for targeted populations. Corporatist or syndicalist instruments directed labour and association. Administrative controls limited firm-level choices in strategic sectors. Independent unions were suppressed. Where confiscation from designated groups was locked, it operated as a confiscatory mechanism rather than ordinary regulation. Residual private title and market activity continued under political constraint but did not restore independent decision rights in the commanding heights. The institutional pattern is extensive state and party direction organised around national-political objectives, short of total extinction of private title.
