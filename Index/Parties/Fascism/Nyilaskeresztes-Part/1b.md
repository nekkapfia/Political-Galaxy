## Regulation

### Overview
The Arrow Cross regime (October 1944 – early 1945) operated under wartime collapse. It combined radical confiscation of property from targeted groups, forced labour, militia-directed allocation of remaining resources, and the effective extinction of ordinary private commercial decision-making for the duration of its rule.

### Relevant Policies
- Radical and rapid confiscation of economic assets from designated groups
- Forced labour and administrative/militia labour allocation
- Subordination of residual production to wartime and regime goals
- Collapse of ordinary commercial law and private contracting in practice
- Suppression of any remaining independent economic organisation
- Near-extinction of residual private economic decision-making during the regime window

### Expanded Analysis
The Arrow Cross period was short but extreme. Confiscation was rapid and targeted. Forced labour and militia control replaced ordinary labour markets. Private commercial decision-making ceased to function as a meaningful system. Relative to the floor bands: money and formal title were not universally abolished as under the Khmer Rouge, but for the duration of the regime the practical distinction between private economic activity and state/militia command collapsed. The package sits at the boundary between extensive state control and near-absolute control for the period scored.
