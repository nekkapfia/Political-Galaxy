# Cultural 4B: Fascism – Nazism
## Equity Lens Analysis
**Score: 18**

### Overview
Nazism rejects the progressive equity lens. Racial or national groups are held to differ in nature or historical role; gaps are not read as proof that opportunity was unfair in the progressive sense. Equal formal opportunity is not expected to produce equal group outcomes. Residual baseline of rejection or absence of progressive equity lens: group outcome gaps are not treated as presumptive evidence of unfair opportunity; essentialist, hierarchical or pure individual-responsibility frames predominate.

### Relevant Policies / Elements
* Racial or ethno-national essentialism: groups would diverge even under identical rules.
* Positional group struggle and hierarchy rather than parity among groups.
* Out-group success explained as exploitation or nature, not as equal-opportunity variance alone.
* Not a liberal merit system for all persons; still low on the equity lens because equalization is rejected.
* Absence of progressive gap-as-proof diagnostic.
* Residual baseline of rejection or absence of progressive equity lens: group outcome gaps are not treated as presumptive evidence of unfair opportunity; essentialist, hierarchical or pure individual-responsibility frames predominate.

### Expanded Analysis
Cultural 4B is the opportunity→outcome claim, not individuality. Hierarchical racial systems score low because they deny that equal opportunity would equalise groups. They also fail pure liberal merit for out-groups. Low equity lens here means absence of the progressive gap-proves-unfairness theory, not a claim that Nazis were individualist liberals. This placement is independent of Cultural 2A (unit of measure) and Cultural 3 (membership gates).

### Why this produces a Final Score of 18
Essentialist group divergence under equal rules and explicit rejection of the progressive gap-as-proof diagnostic produce a low equity-lens score. The system expects hierarchical outcomes rather than parity even under identical formal opportunity, placing it firmly in the low band.
