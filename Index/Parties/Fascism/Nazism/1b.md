# Core 1B: Fascism – Nazism
## Economic Autonomy Analysis
**Final Score: 27**
**Regulation Score: 15**
**Taxation Score: 75**

## Regulation

### Overview
Nazism organised the German economy around rearmament, autarky and regime political goals. Private ownership formally remained in many sectors, but production, investment, labour allocation and prices were subject to extensive state direction. Systematic confiscation of capital from targeted groups, compulsory agricultural delivery, expanding state and party industrial combines, and mass forced labour as a standing allocation system further reduced residual private decision-making.

### Relevant Policies / Elements
* Extensive state direction of production and investment toward rearmament and autarky (Four-Year Plan; Göring apparatus).
* Compulsory cartels, price controls and material allocation (raw materials, foreign exchange, investment permits).
* Systematic Aryanisation — forced transfer of Jewish-owned businesses and capital under state pressure and law.
* Reich Food Estate — compulsory delivery quotas, crop plans and administrative control of agricultural marketing.
* Expanding state and party industrial combines (e.g. Reichswerke Hermann Göring) increasing direct ownership in strategic sectors.
* Forced labour as a standing labour-allocation system (concentration-camp labour; foreign forced workers; labour conscription).
* Suppression of independent trade unions (DAF; strikes illegal).
* Formal private title retained in many sectors without independent decision rights in the commanding heights.

### Expanded Analysis
Formal private title did not preserve independent economic decision-making in decisive sectors. The Four-Year Plan directed investment, materials and foreign exchange. Compulsory cartels and price controls limited firm-level choices. Aryanisation systematically transferred capital from targeted groups — a confiscatory mechanism, not ordinary regulation. The Reich Food Estate imposed compulsory agricultural delivery and crop direction. State and party combines expanded direct ownership in steel, mining and related sectors. Independent unions were abolished. Forced labour became a structural labour-allocation system rather than a marginal emergency measure. Residual private activity in less strategic consumer sectors remained under heavy constraint. Ownership concentration, confiscation, forced labour and administrative direction of production and agriculture place the package in the lower half of extensive state control organised around national-military objectives.

### Regulation Score
Extensive state direction of production and investment, compulsory cartels and price controls, Aryanisation confiscation, compulsory agricultural delivery, expanding state combines, and structural forced labour as allocation produce a Regulation Score of 15.

## Final Score

A = (15 + 75) / 2 = 45.0
L = min(15, 75) = 15
d = |15 − 75| / 100 = 0.60
F = (1 − 0.60) × 45.0 + 0.60 × 15 = 18.0 + 9.0 = 27.0 → **27**

**Final Score: 27**

### Why this produces a Final Score of 27
Regulation at 15 reflects extensive state direction, compulsory cartels and price controls, Aryanisation confiscation, compulsory agricultural delivery, state combines and structural forced labour. Taxation at 75 is the pure algebraic inverse of the stated 25% effective fiscal claim under the Alternative Method. The A/L/d combination produces Final Score 27.
