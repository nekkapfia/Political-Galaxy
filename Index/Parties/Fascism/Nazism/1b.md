## Regulation

### Overview
Nazism organised the German economy around rearmament, autarky and regime political goals. Private ownership formally remained in many sectors, but production, investment, labour allocation and prices were subject to extensive state direction. Systematic confiscation of capital from targeted groups, compulsory agricultural delivery, expanding state/party industrial combines, and mass forced labour as a standing allocation system further reduced residual private decision-making.

### Relevant Policies
- Extensive state direction of production and investment toward rearmament and autarky (Four-Year Plan; Göring apparatus)
- Compulsory cartels, price controls and material allocation (raw materials, foreign exchange, investment permits)
- Systematic Aryanisation — forced transfer of Jewish-owned businesses and capital to non-Jewish owners under state pressure and law
- Reich Food Estate — compulsory delivery quotas, crop plans and administrative control of agricultural marketing
- Expanding state/party industrial combines (e.g. Reichswerke Hermann Göring) increasing direct ownership in strategic sectors
- Forced labour as a standing labour-allocation system (concentration-camp labour; millions of foreign forced workers by the war years; labour conscription)
- Suppression of independent trade unions (DAF; strikes illegal)
- Priority of military and regime objectives over private firm decision-making
- Formal private title retained in many sectors without independent decision rights in the commanding heights

### Expanded Analysis
Under Nazism formal private title did not preserve independent economic decision-making in decisive sectors. The Four-Year Plan directed investment, materials and foreign exchange. Compulsory cartels and price controls limited firm-level choices. Aryanisation systematically transferred capital from targeted groups — a confiscatory mechanism, not ordinary regulation. The Reich Food Estate imposed compulsory agricultural delivery and crop direction. State/party combines expanded direct ownership in steel, mining and related sectors. Independent unions were abolished. Forced labour became a structural labour-allocation system, especially during the war, rather than a marginal emergency measure. Residual private activity in less strategic consumer sectors remained under heavy constraint. Relative to the lower bands: the system did not reach total extinction of private title or complete abolition of money/markets, but ownership concentration, confiscation, forced labour and administrative direction of production and agriculture place it firmly in the lower half of extensive state control organised around national-military objectives.
