# Cultural 4B: Communism – Castroism
## Equity Lens Analysis
**Score: 76**

### Overview

Castroism explains group outcome differences primarily through class exploitation, imperialist extraction and counter-revolutionary obstruction, and responds with systematic group-based restructuring: expropriation, preferential advancement of workers and peasants, and political exclusion of designated enemy categories. The framework is equity-of-class-outcome rather than liberal identity-equity, but it is still a strong systemic-attribution and corrective-intervention lens.

### Framework and Mechanisms

* Official attribution of pre-revolutionary poverty and inequality to capitalist and imperialist structures, not primarily to individual failure.
* Agrarian Reform (1959) and subsequent urban/property reforms as early corrective interventions.
* Expropriation and redistribution of productive assets (1960 nationalisation waves; 1968 Revolutionary Offensive) as hard equity mechanisms transferring assets by class and foreign-ownership category.
* Preferential policies for workers, peasants and revolutionaries in education, employment and political office.
* Designation of bourgeois, gusano and counter-revolutionary categories as structurally antagonistic groups requiring containment rather than equal competition under neutral rules.
* Rejection of pure individual-merit explanations when they conflict with revolutionary class analysis.
* Residual soft mechanisms (mass organisations, political education) reinforcing the systemic class narrative in daily institutions.
* Distinguish from liberal identity-equity (race/gender self-ID quotas): Castroist equity is class-revolutionary.

### Expanded Analysis

Anti-discrimination law of the liberal type is not the operative tool; class-revolutionary equity is. Hard mechanisms (expropriation, category-based exclusion, state monopoly of advancement paths) dominate soft ones. The score sits in the strong equity-lens band without reaching theoretical absolute equity enforcement across every domain. Individual responsibility language appears in labour discipline and productivity campaigns but does not displace systemic class attribution as the primary explanatory frame.

### Why this produces a Final Score of 76

Systemic class-and-imperialism attribution plus hard corrective interventions (expropriation, category preference and exclusion) place Castroism in the mid-to-high 70s on the equity lens.
