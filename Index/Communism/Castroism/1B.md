# Core 1B: Communism – Castroism
## Economic Autonomy Analysis
**Regulation Score: 21**
**Taxation Score: 58**
**Final Score: 33**

### Overview

Castroism replaces markets as the primary allocation mechanism with state ownership, central planning, administrative labour direction and long-run rationing. Private productive property is residual and politically contingent. Taxation Score is derived strictly from formal tax revenue as a share of GDP, not from total state appropriation of surplus through ownership.

### Regulation – Relevant Policies / Elements

* Agrarian Reform Law (May 1959): land ceilings and expansion of state farms; first major restructuring of rural property.
* 1960 nationalisation waves: US oil, electricity, telephone and sugar interests; Laws 890 and 891 (13 October 1960) nationalising major industrial and commercial companies and private banking (hundreds of firms across sugar, textiles, retail, logistics and finance).
* Further US property resolutions in 1960.
* Revolutionary Offensive (March 1968): nationalisation of remaining private retail and service businesses (small farmers and some transport excepted).
* Central planning of production targets, investment and labour allocation through ministries and the Party.
* Libreta rationing system as the primary distribution mechanism for basic goods over long periods, displacing price-mediated consumer choice for essentials.
* Administrative assignment of employment; independent contracting and self-employment periodically suppressed, then partially re-legalised under licence, tax and political controls.
* Currency and exchange controls; dual-currency periods that segmented access to goods by institutional and political status.
* Official unions only (CTC and related); independent labour organisation prohibited.
* Post-Soviet and later limited openings (self-employment categories, agricultural usufruct, small private services) remain reversible and do not restore markets as the primary mechanism.

### Taxation Score – Method and Application

Taxation Score uses the locked formula:

`(1 − Tax Revenue / GDP) × 100`

Only formal tax revenue enters the ratio. Characteristic Cuban tax-to-GDP across the long pre-2021 data run sits in the high-30s to mid-40s percent range; a working central value of ~42% yields Taxation Score **58**. Direct appropriation of surplus through state enterprise ownership is scored under Regulation, not double-counted here. Post-2021 statistical breaks after monetary reform are treated as measurement discontinuity, not as the system’s characteristic take.

### Expanded Analysis

Subjected to a 100-year hypothetical of continuous application, the regulatory package is extensive state control of production, investment and labour: markets are secondary, private ownership is contingent, and rationing plus administrative assignment structure daily economic life. That locks Regulation in the 17–27 band, placed at **21** given the density of ownership, planning and libreta institutions across the formative and long-run decades.

Taxation at **58** is restrictive relative to low-tax systems but not at the floor, because formal tax/GDP is not equivalent to 80–90% extraction ratios. The Final Score formula pulls toward the more restrictive component.

### Why this produces Regulation 21 / Taxation 58 / Final 33

Nationalisation sequence through 1968, central planning, libreta rationing and contingent private activity place Regulation at 21. Formal tax revenue near 42% of GDP places Taxation at 58. With A = 39.5, L = 21, d = 0.37, Final ≈ 32.55 → **33** (rounded up).
