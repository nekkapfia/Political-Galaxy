# Core 2A: Communism – Castroism
## National Sovereignty Analysis
**Score: 55**

### Overview

Castroism maintains formal and practical command of Cuban territory, borders, domestic law and the Revolutionary Armed Forces, while operating for long periods under strategic economic and security dependence on external patrons (primarily the USSR, later Venezuela and others) and under continuous external pressure from the United States. Formal sovereignty is intact; practical autonomy is repeatedly constrained.

### Relevant Policies / Elements

* One-party state with exclusive domestic legislative and enforcement authority; no transfer of law-making power to a supranational legislature comparable to EU membership.
* Full formal control of borders, internal security forces and the Revolutionary Armed Forces.
* 1962 missile crisis configuration: Soviet nuclear forces on Cuban territory under Soviet operational control — extreme temporary dependency without formal cession of statehood.
* Long-term Soviet military advisory and economic presence without treaty transfer of territorial sovereignty.
* US embargo and related external constraints treated as hostile pressure, not accepted shared governance.
* Guantánamo Bay: US base on Cuban territory under historical lease/occupation dispute — persistent formal grievance; limited practical Cuban control over the base area.
* Post-1991 Special Period: loss of Soviet-bloc support as a sovereignty stress test (economic survival under external dependency collapse).
* Venezuelan oil and finance relationship in the 2000s–2010s as a successor patronage pattern.
* Rejection of OAS and related hemispheric institutional constraints when they conflicted with revolutionary policy.

### Expanded Analysis

Primary-drag logic focuses on the most severe real limits on independent decision-making. Formal control of territory, borders, law and forces remains high. The dominant practical drags are sustained patronage dependence (Soviet subsidies historically large relative to GDP in peak years; later Venezuelan support) and the structural effects of US embargo pressure. These do not equal formal surrender of domestic law-making or border command, so the score stays above systems that have transferred core internal competences to external institutions. They do pull the score below systems whose economic and security choices are not structurally tied to a single external patron.

Guantánamo is a real sovereignty anomaly but does not redefine the overall territorial control pattern. The 1962 episode is treated as an extreme temporary configuration, not the long-run baseline.

### Why this produces a Final Score of 55

Intact formal control of territory, borders, law and forces, combined with sustained patronage dependence and external economic coercion as primary practical drags, places Castroism in the mid-50s on national sovereignty.
