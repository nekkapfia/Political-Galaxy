# Cultural 4A: Communism – Castroism
## Social Determinism Analysis
**Score: 34**

### Overview

Castroism substantially expanded formal access to education and healthcare across birth classes while creating new political-birth and loyalty constraints on life trajectories. Birth into a bourgeois or politically unreliable household remained a disadvantage; revolutionary merit and loyalty could open pathways. The net pattern is mixed: universalised basic pathways with residual and new forms of birth-and-category constraint.

### Relevant Policies / Elements

* National Literacy Campaign (1961, “Year of Education”): official figures of ~707,000 alphabetised; residual illiteracy proclaimed ~3.9%; instructor force on the order of ~271,000 (popular alphabetisers, Conrado Benítez brigadistas, Patria o Muerte brigades, teachers); UNESCO-linked “territory free of illiteracy” narrative (22 December 1961). Pre-campaign adult illiteracy commonly cited in the low-to-mid 20% range depending on series.
* Universalisation of basic and secondary schooling — genuine widening of access relative to pre-1959 stratified provision.
* Expanded higher education and professional training, with political criteria affecting admission and progression.
* Nationalisation and suppression of private schooling and much private professional practice, reducing one quality gradient while concentrating allocation in the state.
* Class-origin scrutiny and preferential advancement for workers and peasants in early revolutionary decades.
* Political reliability and revolutionary service as alternative mobility channels that partially override pure family background.
* Housing, rationing and employment allocation mediated by workplace and CDR structures, transmitting political-category advantages.
* Limited later private openings that reintroduce resource gradients without restoring pre-revolutionary birth-caste closure.

### Expanded Analysis

Under the access-versus-quality rule, universal practical access to schooling pulls downward relative to systems that blocked education by birth. Political and class-category filters on higher pathways and careers pull upward. Historical scale remains in view: Castroism is not a hereditary caste order, nor a pure individual-mobility system. Layered pipeline judgment (basic schooling → further education → elite credentials) places the overall pattern in the low-to-mid 30s — constrained mobility with real universal access at the base and political-birth effects higher up.

Political reliability as ongoing performance is partly adjacent to C2A allocation; the 4A weight here is limited to the extent that origin and early political labelling function as birth-adjacent constraints on life trajectories.

### Why this produces a Final Score of 34

Universalised basic education and healthcare reduce pure birth closure; political-class category constraints on advancement and the suppression of independent pathways keep the score in the mid-30s rather than the low 20s.
