# Cultural 2A: Communism – Castroism
## Individuality Analysis
**Score: 23**

### Overview

Castroism subordinates individual merit, contract and status to collective revolutionary and class categories. Rights, opportunities and political standing are routinely allocated according to group membership — worker/peasant versus bourgeois origin, revolutionary versus counter-revolutionary, loyal participant in mass organisations versus outsider.

### Allocation Mechanisms

* Class origin and revolutionary reliability as recurring criteria in education access, professional advancement and political trust, especially in early revolutionary decades.
* Mass organisations (CDR, CTC, FMC, UJC, ANAP) mediating access to goods, housing queues and social recognition.
* Political loyalty and participation treated as group-performance criteria for full standing, not as optional individual association.
* Suppression of independent civil association; individuals cannot form status-bearing groups outside Party-approved structures.
* Residual private economic activity (when permitted) framed as concession to necessity, not as recognition of individual economic sovereignty.
* Housing allocation and libreta access entangled with workplace and CDR standing.
* Legal and institutional equality rhetoric operating inside a system that still sorts people by revolutionary-political category.
* Contrast with liberal self-ID or demographic quota systems: Castroist group allocation is class-and-loyalty, not gender-identity self-declaration.

### Expanded Analysis

The dominant allocation principle is collective-political rather than individual-meritocratic. Even where formal education expansion and healthcare access reduce some birth-class barriers, the political category of counter-revolutionary or unreliable systematically withholds status and opportunity. Self-ID style identity allocation is not the mechanism; class-and-loyalty allocation is. The result is strong group primacy without requiring liberal identity-politics forms.

### Why this produces a Final Score of 23

Routine subordination of individual standing to revolutionary-class and loyalty categories, mediation of goods and recognition through mass organisations, and the absence of independent individualist allocation as the default place Castroism in the low 20s on individuality.
