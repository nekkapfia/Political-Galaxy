# Cultural 2B: Communism – Castroism
## Pride / National Identity Analysis
**Score: 73**

### Overview

Castroism cultivates a strong affirmative stance toward Cuban revolutionary national identity. Independence, anti-imperialist struggle and the post-1959 state are celebrated as collective achievement. Pride is real and institutionally enforced, but it is pride in a revolutionary-national construct that deliberately breaks with much of the pre-revolutionary social order rather than continuous traditional nationalism alone.

### Observable Stance – Relevant Elements

* Official pantheon: José Martí, independence wars, Moncada and revolutionary heroes; 26 July commemorations as founding myth.
* Continuous anti-imperialist framing of Cuban identity against US power as a source of collective pride and solidarity.
* Public rituals, education and media celebrating the revolution, the 1961 literacy campaign, internationalist missions (including Angola) and resistance to embargo.
* Internationalist missions narrated as national glory and anti-apartheid / anti-imperialist solidarity.
* Suppression of narratives that treat the revolution as national catastrophe or illegitimate rupture.
* Limited space for pre-revolutionary elite or liberal-republican identity as a positive national frame.
* National pride tightly fused with loyalty to the socialist state; opposition to the regime framed as anti-national.
* Post-1959 public art and monument practice oriented to revolutionary memory rather than restoration of pre-revolutionary elite symbolism.

### Expanded Analysis

Castroism does not score at the absolute pride pole because national identity is reconstructed around the revolutionary break and remains hostile to large parts of the pre-1959 heritage. It does not score in guilt/shame bands because the institutional default is affirmative celebration of the revolutionary nation, not atonement for Cuban history as such. Comparative anti-imperialist superiority claims and sustained investment in revolutionary memory keep the score in the honour / strong-positive range.

### Why this produces a Final Score of 73

Institutionally enforced revolutionary-national pride, anti-imperialist identity, Moncada/Martí pantheon and suppression of regime-critical national narratives place Castroism at 73.
