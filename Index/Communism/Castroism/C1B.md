# Cultural 1B: Communism – Castroism
## Dogmatism Analysis
**Orthodoxy: 93**
**Tribalism: 91**
**Final Score: 92**

### Overview

Castroism combines extreme internal closure with comprehensive external rejection of competing moral frameworks. Inside the Party and regime, deviation from the revolutionary line is institutionally punished; outside, liberalism, capitalism and counter-revolutionary currents are treated as illegitimate and hostile rather than as debatable alternatives.

### Mechanisms of Protection and Rejection

* Monopoly of the Communist Party of Cuba; no legal factional competition or organised internal opposition currents.
* Democratic centralism and Party discipline making public dissent from the line career- and liberty-threatening.
* CDR network (founded 1960; multi-million membership in later decades) extending surveillance and reporting into neighbourhood and workplace life.
* Criminal and administrative categories of counter-revolutionary activity and “ideological diversionism” covering non-violent political expression and organisation.
* State monopoly of media and education ensuring competing frameworks lack institutional platforms.
* Exit controls (for most of the system’s history) and professional exclusion as enforcement tools against ideological nonconformity.
* Official discourse treating capitalist, liberal-democratic and “imperialist” frameworks as enemy systems rather than legitimate rivals.
* Exile communities and external opposition framed as illegitimate poles rather than recognised alternative political communities.

### Expanded Analysis

Orthodoxy is institutionalised through Party monopoly, security organs and mass organisations; revision of foundational political principles is not a recognised internal process comparable to open factional contestation. Tribalism is equally strong: alternative moral-political systems are framed as existential threats or moral failures. The formula weights the higher aspect when the two diverge; here both sit at the extreme end.

Intensity is not uniform across every decade — Special Period and post-2010 economic debates opened limited managerial space — but the bounding rule remains Party-line closure and external delegitimisation of liberal and counter-revolutionary frameworks.

### Why this produces Orthodoxy 93 / Tribalism 91 / Final 92

Systematic internal discipline (Party monopoly, CDR, diversionism categories) and comprehensive external delegitimisation of rival frameworks place both aspects at the extreme end; the formula yields Final 92.
