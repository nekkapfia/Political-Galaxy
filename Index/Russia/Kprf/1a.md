# Core 1A: Russia – Communist Party of the Russian Federation
## Personal Autonomy Analysis
**Endos Score: 22**  
**Xenos Score: 16**

### Overview

The Communist Party of the Russian Federation, scored on the restrictions implied by its programme and systemic role, produces a restrictive Core 1A profile built from defence of state control over political expression, support for laws against “extremism,” “discrediting” the armed forces and related speech offences, prioritisation of state ideology and historical orthodoxy, and acceptance of the wider Putin-era speech and association architecture. As a systemic opposition party it does not govern alone, but its programme does not liberalise the restrictive surface and in key areas affirms it. Heightened intensity against designated ideological and identity out-groups justifies a limited Endos/Xenos split.

### Core 1A Relevant Policies / Elements

* Affirmative support for criminal and administrative restrictions on “extremist” expression, rehabilitation of Nazism, and related historical-speech offences; no programme to narrow the expansive extremism framework.
* Support for or acceptance of laws penalising discrediting of the armed forces, spreading “false information” about state operations, and analogous wartime/political speech controls.
* Defence of state media dominance and limits on independent political media; no agenda to restore open political broadcast or platform competition.
* Ideological prioritisation of Soviet historical orthodoxy and opposition to “anti-Soviet” or liberal-Western narrative contestation in education and public culture.
* Acceptance of the broader association and protest restriction regime; KPRF operates as permitted systemic opposition within managed boundaries rather than as a free-speech liberalising force.
* Nationalist and identity-inflected rhetoric that can intensify pressure on liberal, Western-aligned, or certain minority-associated expression.
* No comprehensive programme to dismantle foreign-agent, undesirable-organisation, or extremism registries that constrain speech and association.
* Retention of ordinary direct-harm criminal prohibitions.

### Expanded Analysis

KPRF is not a European progressive densification party. It is a systemic left-national party operating inside an already authoritarian speech environment. Its programme affirms rather than challenges the core restrictive layers: extremism law, historical-speech controls, limits on independent media, and managed opposition boundaries.

Where it adds distinctive weight is ideological orthodoxy (Soviet history, anti-liberal cultural posture) and willingness to support or tolerate expansive political speech criminalisation when framed as defence of the state or victory narrative. It does not offer a liberalising final set.

For Endos (core population under a KPRF-compatible order), public political speech remains heavily constrained by extremism and wartime/political speech rules; organised dissent outside permitted channels is not protected. Placement in the low 20s reflects ideological enforcement plus acceptance of the surrounding authoritarian architecture.

For Xenos — liberals, Western-aligned voices, and groups targeted by extremism or foreign-agent frameworks — intensity is higher. Designated organisational and speech disabilities pull the out-group score lower.

Framing as patriotism, anti-fascism, or stability is discarded. Only the observable restriction set is scored.

### Why this produces Endos 22 / Xenos 16

Extremism and political-speech criminalisation affirmed, historical orthodoxy enforced, independent media and association limits accepted, and no liberalising agenda place Endos at 22 (ideological enforcement / upper thought-control border). Heightened application to designated ideological out-groups produces Xenos 16.
