## Regulation

### Overview
SPD defends a social-market framework with continued climate and industrial-transformation steering, strong labour protections, and targeted state investment support. Markets remain the primary mechanism of exchange, but economic activity is shaped by environmental rules, strategic industrial policy, and dense employment regulation.

### Relevant Policies
- Continuity of climate targets and green industrial policy with targeted investment incentives (Made-in-Germany bonus, Deutschlandfonds)
- Strong labour-market protections and higher minimum-wage trajectory
- Co-determination and collective-bargaining framework maintained and defended
- Industrial electricity price and selective energy-cost relief for strategic sectors
- Bureaucracy reduction present but secondary to transformation and social goals
- Maintain ordinary Kündigungsschutz, standard competition rules, and residual non-Net-Zero environmental standards

### Expanded Analysis
Under an SPD-led package the German baseline retains its dense climate and labour layer while adding targeted industrial-policy instruments and investment incentives. Private ownership and market allocation continue to operate across most of the economy, yet production and investment decisions in energy-intensive and strategic sectors are shaped by state direction, subsidies and conditional relief. Labour allocation remains constrained by strong dismissal protections, co-determination and wage-setting institutions. Pragmatic elements (CCS openness, industrial electricity price, limited bureaucracy relief) exist but do not remove the overall density of environmental, industrial and labour rules.

The ordinary framework of Kündigungsschutz, co-determination and competition rules is maintained as a single residual block; the decisive downward pressure comes from the combination of sustained green industrial steering and strong employment regulation.

### Final Score
The decisive mechanical effects are sustained climate and industrial-transformation steering, strong labour protections, and targeted state investment direction. Residual market mechanisms and limited pragmatic relief are present, but the density of green and labour rules remains high. This produces a Regulation Score of 36.

## Taxation

These proposals are scored via the separate Taxation Score method (not included in the Regulation Score above).

- Relief for lower and middle incomes (Grundfreibetrag, Kinderfreibetrag, Kindergeld, Arbeitnehmerpauschbetrag, progression flattening): ~€8–12 billion cost
- Higher top-end rates and earlier application of Reichensteuer (45 % from €250 000, 47 % from €280 000): ~€4–7 billion gain
- Targeted investment incentives / Made-in-Germany tax premium: ~€10–18 billion cost (depending on take-up)
- Continuation of climate-related levies and CO₂ pricing path with Klimageld compensation: roughly neutral to modest net cost
- Selective industrial energy-cost relief: ~€3–6 billion cost

**Overall estimated net fiscal position**: modest net cost of ~€15–30 billion per year relative to a pure status-quo baseline, partially offset by higher top rates.
