Germany - Die Grünen - Core 1B
Final Score: 39
Regulation score: 29, Taxation score: 55

## Regulation

### Overview
Die Grünen prioritise the acceleration and deepening of the green industrial and climate framework. Markets remain the primary mechanism of exchange, but economic activity is heavily shaped by environmental rules, technology mandates, emissions pricing, and directed support for green technologies and infrastructure. Bureaucracy-reduction measures exist but remain secondary to the density of climate and industrial-steering requirements.

### Relevant Policies
- Retention and strengthening of the core Klimaschutzgesetz architecture and sectoral climate targets
- Continuation and acceleration of renewable expansion mandates, electricity network and storage build-out, and green hydrogen infrastructure
- Retention of the Gebäudeenergiegesetz 65 % renewable-heating rule and the combustion-engine phase-out trajectory
- Emissions trading positioned as the central steering instrument, supplemented by climate contracts, green lead markets and targeted industrial support
- Maintenance and in places tightening of sustainability reporting and supply-chain due-diligence frameworks
- Limited bureaucracy reduction via digitalisation and systematic “Praxischecks”, without dismantling core climate or labour standards
- Maintain ordinary Kündigungsschutz and co-determination
- Maintain standard competition rules and residual planning constraints

### Expanded Analysis
Under a Die Grünen platform the existing dense layer of climate targets, technology mandates and industrial-steering instruments would be retained and in key areas accelerated. Firms would continue to face binding expansion paths for renewables, heating and mobility phase-out rules, and extensive compliance obligations tied to the green transition. Targeted investment support and limited administrative streamlining would operate inside this framework rather than replacing it. The overall regulatory environment remains heavily shaped by environmental rules and directed green industrial policy.

The package leaves in place ordinary labour-market protections (Kündigungsschutz and co-determination), standard competition rules, and residual planning constraints. These residual elements are secondary; the dominant mechanical character is the continuation and intensification of the green-mandate and industrial-steering layer.

### Final Score
The decisive mechanical effects are the retention and acceleration of climate targets, technology mandates (heating, engines, renewables), emissions-trading centrality and associated industrial-steering tools. Limited bureaucracy-reduction measures do not offset the density of these requirements. This produces a Regulation Score of 29.

## Taxation

These proposals are scored via the separate Taxation Score method (not included in the Regulation Score above).

- Higher taxation of high incomes, large fortunes and closing of inheritance/wealth loopholes: ~€10–20 billion gain
- Removal or reduction of fossil-fuel subsidies and environmentally harmful tax preferences: ~€5–12 billion gain
- Temporary investment premium / targeted green investment relief: ~€8–15 billion cost
- Income-tax relief focused on lower and middle earners and families: ~€5–10 billion cost
- Energy-tax and levy adjustments (Stromsteuer reductions offset by broader climate-related fiscal measures)

**Overall estimated net fiscal effect of the tax package**: ~€0–15 billion net gain per year  
**Current GDP reference**: ≈ €4.6 trillion  
**Taxation Score: 55**
