Germany - BSW - Core 1B
Final Score: 49
Regulation score: 42, Taxation score: 58

## Regulation

### Overview
BSW combines selective removal of green technology mandates and bureaucracy with stronger labour protections, higher state involvement in energy pricing and infrastructure, and a preference for domestic industrial preservation. It rejects the most coercive elements of the Energiewende (heating law, combustion-engine ban, CO₂ price) while retaining a framework of social regulation and public investment.

### Relevant Policies
- Abolition of the national CO₂ price
- Withdrawal of the Gebäudeenergiegesetz (heating law) and the combustion-engine phase-out
- Significant reduction of sustainability reporting and reform of the Lieferkettengesetz to ease burdens on SMEs
- Bureaucracy reduction focused on planning, permitting and documentation obligations for medium-sized firms
- Technology-open promotion of innovation instead of binding technology bans
- Preference for pipeline gas (including Russian supplies) and rejection of high-cost LNG substitution as the primary industrial energy solution
- Strengthening of Tarifbindung and raising the statutory minimum wage to €15
- Stronger cartel and competition rules directed at large digital platforms and dominant firms
- Maintain ordinary Kündigungsschutz and co-determination
- Maintain core public infrastructure and network regulation, with greater openness to public ownership or rekommunalisierung in energy networks

### Expanded Analysis
Under a BSW platform the densest layer of green technology mandates and reporting requirements would be stripped back, lowering compliance costs and restoring greater choice in heating, transport and industrial energy use. This produces a clear reduction in directed regulatory pressure relative to the current German baseline. At the same time the party would tighten labour-market rules (higher minimum wage, stronger collective-bargaining coverage) and increase the state’s role in energy pricing, infrastructure investment and competition policy toward large platforms.

The package leaves in place the core elements of ordinary labour-market protection (Kündigungsschutz and co-determination), standard competition rules for ordinary firms, and basic environmental standards that are not tied to Net Zero mandates. These residual constraints, together with the additional labour and industrial-policy interventions, remain, while the dominant mechanical change is the selective removal of the densest green-mandate layer.

### Final Score
The decisive mechanical effects are the removal of the CO₂ price, heating law and engine ban together with bureaucracy relief for SMEs, offset by stronger labour regulation and a more directive stance on energy and competition policy. The net result is a modest improvement over the current heavily green-steered baseline but still a clear distance from a lightly regulated market order. This produces a Regulation Score of 42.

## Taxation

These proposals are scored via the separate Taxation Score method (not included in the Regulation Score above).

- Higher taxation of capital gains, stock buybacks and large fortunes / reintroduction of a wealth tax on very high assets: ~€15–30 billion gain
- Alignment of capital income taxation more closely with labour income: ~€5–12 billion gain
- Income-tax and contribution relief for lower and middle earners, including tax-free pensions up to €2 000: ~€10–18 billion cost
- Minimum-wage increase to €15 (secondary fiscal and labour-cost effects)

**Overall estimated net fiscal effect of the tax package**: ~€5–20 billion net gain per year  
**Current GDP reference**: ≈ €4.6 trillion  
**Taxation Score: 58**
