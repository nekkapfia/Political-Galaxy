Communism - Castroism - Core 1B
Final Score: 28
Regulation score: 22, Taxation score: 35

## Regulation

### Overview
Castroism realises a classical command economy. The state owns the decisive majority of the means of production, sets production and investment targets through central planning, and allocates labour and key resources by administrative decision. Private and cooperative activity exists only within tightly constrained limits and remains subordinate to state direction.

### Relevant Policies
- Dominant state ownership of industry, banking, wholesale and foreign trade, large agriculture and strategic services
- Central planning of production targets, investment allocation and material balances
- Administrative wage setting and labour allocation in the state sector
- Strict limits on private enterprise size, activities and hiring
- Rationing systems and state control over distribution of basic goods
- State and military conglomerates exercising extensive commercial control
- Maintain residual private and cooperative activity only as a regulated supplement to the state sector

### Expanded Analysis
Under Castroism the primary mechanism of economic organisation is state ownership combined with central planning. Firms in the decisive sectors do not operate as independent market actors; production, investment and often labour are directed by plan. Private and cooperative activity is quantitatively and legally subordinate, subject to negative lists, size caps and administrative oversight. Consumption is shaped by rationing and state distribution. The residual market elements do not alter the dominant character of extensive state control over production, investment and labour allocation.

The package leaves only a regulated residual private sphere; the decisive mechanical features are ownership, planning and administrative allocation.

### Final Score
The decisive mechanical effects are dominant state ownership, central planning of production and investment, administrative labour allocation, and restricted residual private activity. This produces a Regulation Score of 22.

## Taxation

These proposals are scored via the separate Taxation Score method (not included in the Regulation Score above).

Castroism is an extreme command economy. The bulk of the state’s claim on resources occurs through ownership of enterprises, administered prices and wages, and direct appropriation of surplus rather than through formal tax instruments. The standard tax-to-GDP formula is therefore inappropriate and would produce misleading results.

**Alternative Method applied**  
Effective tithe rate (best estimate of the proportion of output, labour and resources compulsorily extracted or directed by the governing authority): **65 %**  
Taxation Score = 100 − 65 = **35**

Justification for the estimate rests on state-sector employment shares (~67–72 %), low non-state contribution to GDP in the classical model, high enterprise-to-budget transfers, and the comparative ranking of Cuba among the purest remaining plan-dominant systems.

**Taxation Score: 35**

## Final Score

A = (22 + 35) / 2 = 28.5  
L = min(22, 35) = 22  
d = |22 − 35| / 100 = 0.13  
F = (1 − 0.13)·28.5 + 0.13·22 = 24.795 + 2.86 = 27.655 → **28** (beneficial rounding)

**Final Score: 28**
