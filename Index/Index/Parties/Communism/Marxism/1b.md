## Regulation

### Overview
Theoretical Marxism (higher phase of communism) abolishes private ownership of the means of production, commodity production, market exchange and money. Production and distribution are organised entirely through social planning by the associated producers. There is no residual sphere of private economic decision-making.

### Relevant Policies
- Complete abolition of private property in the means of production
- Abolition of commodity production and market exchange
- Abolition of money
- Social ownership and planned allocation of the entire social product
- Distribution according to need
- No independent private production, investment, contracting or labour allocation

### Expanded Analysis
In the pure theoretical model every economically significant decision — what is produced, how resources are allocated, how labour is directed, and how the product is distributed — is made socially. Independent agents do not enter voluntary contracts, own productive assets, or retain a privately controlled share of output. Personal consumption occurs only as the receipt of an allocated bundle. The distinction between private economic activity and social allocation is eliminated by design. This matches the theoretical floor of the scale: complete collective economic control with no private economic activity possible under any circumstances.

### Final Score
The decisive mechanical effects are the total abolition of private productive property, markets, money and independent economic decision-making. These place the package at the theoretical floor.
