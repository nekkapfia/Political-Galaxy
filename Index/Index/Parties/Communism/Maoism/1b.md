Communism - Maoism - Core 1B
Final Score: 26
Regulation score: 22, Taxation score: 30

## Regulation

### Overview
Maoism, especially in its Great Leap and commune phases, organised the economy around forced industrialisation financed by high extraction from agriculture. Collectivisation, state monopsony procurement and administrative direction of labour and resources were the central mechanisms.

### Relevant Policies
- Collectivisation and people’s communes maximising state claim on agricultural surplus
- Compulsory grain procurement at below-market prices
- High forced investment rates prioritising heavy industry
- Administrative mobilisation of rural labour for industrial and infrastructure projects
- Periodic restriction or abolition of private plots and rural markets
- State control of the “commanding heights” of industry, trade and finance

### Expanded Analysis
Under peak Maoism the state extracted a large surplus from agriculture through procurement and the price scissors to finance rapid industrialisation. Communes enabled direct mobilisation of labour. Consumption, especially rural consumption, was subordinated to accumulation targets. The intensity of extraction during the Great Leap produced mass famine. Residual private plots and markets were periodically suppressed. The package exhibits high forced extraction, collectivisation and administrative labour mobilisation.

### Final Score
The decisive mechanical effects are high forced extraction from agriculture, collectivisation/commune organisation, administrative labour mobilisation, and priority of accumulation over consumption. This produces a Regulation Score of 22.

## Taxation

These proposals are scored via the separate Taxation Score method (not included in the Regulation Score above).

Maoism is an extreme command economy. Formal tax instruments were secondary to procurement, price scissors and direct surplus appropriation. The standard tax-to-GDP formula is inappropriate for the peak periods.

**Alternative Method applied**  
Effective tithe rate: **70 %**  
Taxation Score = 100 − 70 = **30**

Justification rests on grain-procurement intensity, documented price-scissors transfers 1958–1978, forced accumulation rates, and the human cost of the Great Leap as an indicator of extraction beyond sustainable levels.

**Taxation Score: 30**

## Final Score

A = (22 + 30) / 2 = 26  
L = min(22, 30) = 22  
d = |22 − 30| / 100 = 0.08  
F = (1 − 0.08)·26 + 0.08·22 = 23.92 + 1.76 = 25.68 → **26** (beneficial rounding)

**Final Score: 26**
