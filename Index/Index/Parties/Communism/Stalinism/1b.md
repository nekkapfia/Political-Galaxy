Final Score: 26
Regulation score: 22, Taxation score: 30

## Regulation

### Overview
Stalinism organised the Soviet economy around forced industrialisation financed by a sharp shift of resources out of private consumption into investment and government uses. Collectivisation, near-total state ownership of industry, and central planning of material balances were the core mechanisms.

### Relevant Policies
- Near-total state and cooperative-collective ownership of productive capital by the mid-1930s
- Collectivisation of agriculture and compulsory grain deliveries
- Central planning of production, investment and material balances
- Administrative direction of labour and suppression of independent economic organisation
- High forced accumulation rates (investment share roughly doubled 1928–1937)
- Priority of heavy industry and military capacity over consumption

### Expanded Analysis
Under Stalinism the state claimed the decisive share of national product through ownership and procurement. Between 1928 and 1937 roughly 30 percentage points of GNP were shifted out of private consumption into investment and government purchases. Collectivisation enabled the extraction of agricultural surplus. Residual private activity was reduced to personal plots and the narrowest margins. The package exhibits extensive ownership, planning, labour direction and subordination of consumption to political-industrial objectives.

### Final Score
The decisive mechanical effects are near-total state ownership, collectivisation and compulsory procurement, central planning, and the large forced shift from consumption to accumulation. This produces a Regulation Score of 22.

## Taxation

These proposals are scored via the separate Taxation Score method (not included in the Regulation Score above).

Stalinism is an extreme command economy. Formal tax instruments existed but were secondary to ownership-based appropriation and procurement. The standard tax-to-GDP formula understates the true compulsory claim.

**Alternative Method applied**  
Effective tithe rate: **70 %**  
Taxation Score = 100 − 70 = **30**

Justification rests on Bergson and subsequent reconstructions of the 1928–1940 end-use shift, ownership shares approaching 98–99 % of fixed production capital, high accumulation rates, and the intensity of grain collections.

**Taxation Score: 30**

## Final Score

A = (22 + 30) / 2 = 26  
L = min(22, 30) = 22  
d = |22 − 30| / 100 = 0.08  
F = (1 − 0.08)·26 + 0.08·22 = 23.92 + 1.76 = 25.68 → **26** (beneficial rounding)

**Final Score: 26**
