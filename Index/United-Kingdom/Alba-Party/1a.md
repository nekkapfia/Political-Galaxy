### Core 1A Relevant Policies / Elements

* Hate Crime and Public Order (Scotland) Act 2021 retained without high-priority repeal. The Act expands protected characteristics beyond the English framework (including transgender identity and age) and, in important formulations, incorporates a lowered threshold relative to intent-based English stirring-up offences; these features remain operative.
* UK Online Safety Act architecture retained as it applies in Scotland, including illegal-content duties and platform accountability. No comprehensive repeal agenda.
* Greater scepticism than the SNP toward expansive institutional and educational measures focused on gender identity, including resistance to self-identification rules that convert misgendering into hostile-environment or potential hate-incident matters and that generate secondary constraints on association and access to single-sex spaces.
* Defence of women’s sex-based rights and associated expression — relevant only insofar as it means certain restrictive institutional layers present under the SNP are absent under Alba; not scored as a positive.
* No wholesale narrowing of public-order or communications offences as they operate in Scotland.
* Limited engagement with the more expansive forms of non-crime hate-incident recording supported by the SNP; administrative push-back is modest and does not eliminate underlying primary offences.

### Expanded Analysis

Alba does not repeal the Hate Crime and Public Order (Scotland) Act 2021 or the Online Safety Act; both therefore remain and continue to exert downward weight. The Scottish Act’s expanded characteristics and lowered threshold relative to English stirring-up offences stay operative. Platform content duties under the Online Safety Act likewise remain fully present.

The decisive difference from the SNP is resistance to gender-identity institutional expansions. Self-identification rules that treat affirmed gender as operative — converting speech asserting biological sex (misgendering) and association decisions around single-sex spaces into hostile-environment or potential hate-incident questions — are not part of Alba’s programme. Their absence lightens the final set relative to the SNP.

Defence of sex-based rights is not an upward force; it is evidence that specific restrictive institutional layers are absent. No stable, discretely targetable dual structure of expression controls is formalised by Alba policy, so no Endos/Xenos split applies.

The architecture sits above the SNP/Green/Sinn Féin/Plaid cluster because gender-identity speech and association constraints are resisted, and below pure baseline-retention parties because the Scottish Hate Crime Act and Online Safety Act remain fully in force.
