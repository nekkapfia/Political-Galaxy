# Core 1A: United Kingdom – Liberal Democrats
## Personal Autonomy Analysis

### Overview

The Liberal Democrats’ programme produces a mid-range Core 1A profile built from retention of the Online Safety Act’s core illegal-content and child-protection duties, Public Order Act speech offences, Racial and Religious Hatred Act stirring-up provisions, and communications offences, without new identity-specific speech offences or election-period platform amplification duties of the Labour type. Formal civil-liberties language is discarded as credit. The final set is essentially the post-2023 UK baseline without significant intensification. No Endos/Xenos split applies.

### Core 1A Relevant Policies / Elements

* Online Safety Act core duties retained (illegal content, child protection, platform accountability); emphasis on Ofcom enforcement of both restriction and free-expression safeguards, but the restrictive duties remain.
* Public Order Act 1986 (including Section 5) and Racial and Religious Hatred Act stirring-up offences retained as necessary limits on speech that incites hatred or causes harassment, alarm or distress.
* Communications Act and related offences retained where they target intentional threats or harmful false communications.
* Acceptance of platform-level content moderation for illegal material and certain categories of harmful content.
* Opposition to wholesale repeal of the Online Safety Act or core hate-speech statutes.
* No proposal for new identity-based speech offences or election-period platform amplification duties that would treat social media as broadcast media.
* Anti-SLAPP and transparency measures are process/protective of expression and do not constitute new direct restrictions.
* Retention of ordinary direct-harm criminal prohibitions.

### Expanded Analysis

The Liberal Democrats conserve the post-2023 speech-restriction baseline. Online Safety Act duties, Section 5-style public-order offences, and stirring-up provisions remain part of the governing architecture. The party does not add Labour’s concrete expansions (election-period platform duties, anti-Muslim hostility definition as institutional lens) and does not add Green-style mandatory attitude-correction training or dense identity-orthodoxy layers.

Civil-liberties rhetoric generates no upward credit. Resistance to further densification prevents additional downward weight but does not remove the existing restrictive surface. Under pure final-set scoring only what remains is counted: the baseline is still present and fully weighted.

No stable, discretely targetable out-group differential is formalised by Liberal Democrat policy, so no Endos/Xenos split applies.

The architecture sits above the progressive densification cluster (Green, SNP, Sinn Féin, Plaid, Labour) because intensifying identity-orthodoxy and training layers are absent, and below Reform UK / Restore Britain because the major Online Safety Act and public-order speech layers remain. Cumulative weight is mid-range baseline retention.
