# Core 1A: Fascism – Repubblica Sociale Italiana
## Personal Autonomy Analysis
**Endos Score: 15**  
**Xenos Score: 8**

### Overview

The Repubblica Sociale Italiana (RSI), as the political entity that held power in northern Italy from 1943 to 1945, represents the radicalised final expression of Italian Fascism. After the armistice and the German occupation of northern and central Italy, Mussolini was restored as head of a client regime that abandoned many of the residual moderating features of the earlier Fascist state. Political repression intensified, collaboration in the deportation of Jews increased, and the practical space for ordinary private life narrowed under conditions of war, occupation and civil conflict. The result is the lowest autonomy score in the Italian Fascist sequence, with a clear Endos/Xenos differential.

### Core 1A Relevant Policies / Elements

* Intensified political repression against anti-Fascists, partisans and former regime figures judged insufficiently loyal.
* Active collaboration with German forces in the identification, arrest and deportation of Jews.
* Expanded use of special tribunals, summary procedures and extrajudicial violence.
* Total-war controls on movement, labour, speech and association in the territory under RSI and German authority.
* Suppression of residual independent cultural and religious expression that had survived in attenuated form under the earlier regime.
* Retention of core criminal prohibitions on ordinary private violence, though subordinated to political and military priorities.
* Radicalisation of Fascist ideology toward a more explicitly revolutionary and punitive model.

### Expanded Analysis

The restrictive profile of the Italian Social Republic is defined by wartime mobilisation combined with the radicalisation of repressive practice after 1943. The earlier Fascist apparatus of one-party monopoly, press control and special tribunals remained, but it operated under conditions of military occupation, civil war and heightened German influence. Political expression and association outside the RSI framework were met with greater violence and less procedural constraint than in the mature Fascist period. Under Direct Restriction Only these are scored as direct, intensified prohibitions. Negative-Only Weighting records the full cumulative weight; the temporary and geographically limited character of the RSI does not generate upward credit while the regime lasted.

The Endos/Xenos differential remains clear. Jews faced systematic deportation and the practical destruction of remaining legal protections. Political opponents and partisans faced summary repression. Both groups experienced markedly worse treatment than the core population that continued to function within RSI or German administrative structures, satisfying the optional-split rule.

**Key Shift:** Descent into wartime radicalisation, intensified political repression and active collaboration in deportations, producing the lowest autonomy scores of the Italian Fascist sequence.

### Why this produces Endos 15 / Xenos 8

Maximum intensity of the Fascist period under conditions of war, occupation and radicalisation produces an Endos score of 15. The Xenos score of 8 reflects the additional measures directed at Jews and political opponents. Negative-Only Weighting applies throughout. The scores sit below Mature Italian Fascism and remain above the absolute floor occupied by the most extreme genocidal systems.
