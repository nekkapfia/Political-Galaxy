# Core 1A: Fascism – Partito Nazionale Fascista – Early
## Personal Autonomy Analysis
**Endos Score: 45**  
**Xenos Score: 35**

### Overview

The Partito Nazionale Fascista (PNF) in its early phase of power (roughly 1922 to late 1925) covers the transitional period from the March on Rome to the consolidation of the dictatorship. The liberal constitutional forms of the Italian state still existed on paper, yet they were progressively hollowed out through a combination of legal manoeuvre, administrative pressure, squadrist violence and selective intimidation. Opposition parties and newspapers remained formally possible for a time, but the practical space for voluntary political expression, association and independent journalism contracted rapidly. The full institutional apparatus of the later totalitarian regime was not yet complete; the period is therefore scored as a partial but already material reduction from the preceding liberal baseline.

### Core 1A Relevant Policies / Elements

* Systematic pressure on opposition newspapers through violence, intimidation, administrative harassment and occasional seizures.
* Acerbo Law (1923) and subsequent electoral manipulation that undermined genuine competitive multi-party politics.
* Early use of squadrist violence and administrative sanctions against socialist, communist, liberal and Catholic opponents.
* Progressive restriction of independent trade-union and associational activity.
* Residual liberal legal forms (parliament, courts, press law) continued to exist but were increasingly subordinated to Fascist political will.
* Differential intensity already visible against political opponents on the left and, to a lesser extent, against other anti-Fascist forces.
* Ordinary criminal law continued to operate for non-political matters.

### Expanded Analysis

The restrictive architecture of Early Italian Fascism is defined by the beginning of the destruction of the liberal competitive system rather than its completed replacement. Political speech and association were not yet subject to the comprehensive one-party monopoly and special-tribunal system of the later period, but the cost of opposition had already risen sharply. Under Direct Restriction Only the scored weight is the combination of extralegal intimidation, administrative pressure and the early legal measures that narrowed the practical space for voluntary political expression. Negative-Only Weighting records these additions relative to the pre-1922 liberal baseline; residual formal liberties generate no upward credit.

A clear Endos/Xenos differential is present. Political opponents (socialists, communists, and other organised anti-Fascists) faced markedly higher levels of violence, intimidation and administrative sanction than the general population that accepted or accommodated the new regime. This constitutes a stable, discretely targetable divergence satisfying the optional-split rule.

**Key Shift:** Beginning of the destruction of the liberal multi-party and independent-press environment through a combination of legal and extralegal means, producing the first durable downward movement from the pre-Fascist baseline.

### Why this produces Endos 45 / Xenos 35

Partial but already material restrictions on political expression, association and the press, combined with clear differential targeting of opponents, produce an Endos score of 45 and a Xenos score of 35. The scores reflect a transitional regime that had not yet completed the totalitarian institutional architecture of 1926 onward, while already imposing significant direct costs on voluntary political activity.
