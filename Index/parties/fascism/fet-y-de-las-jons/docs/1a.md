# Core 1A: Fascism – FET y de las JONS
## Personal Autonomy Analysis
**Endos Score: 22**  
**Xenos Score: 12**

### Overview

Early Francoism covers the period from the Nationalist victory in the Spanish Civil War (1939) through the mid-1940s, when the regime still operated under the influence of the Falange and wartime alignment with the Axis. It combined one-party monopoly, systematic political repression, press control and the severe punishment of the defeated Republican side with a more traditional authoritarian and Catholic framework than either Italian Fascism or National Socialism. The result is a highly restrictive profile, particularly for the defeated and for political opponents, with a pronounced Endos/Xenos differential. After the mid-1940s the regime gradually moderated some of its more radical fascist features, but the early phase remains the relevant scoring window for the “Fascism” country set.

### Core 1A Relevant Policies / Elements

* Establishment of a single-party system under FET y de las JONS and the suppression of all independent political parties and free trade unions.
* Comprehensive press censorship and state control of information.
* Systematic repression of the defeated Republican population through military tribunals, imprisonment, executions and economic discrimination (the “Law of Political Responsibilities” and related measures).
* Forced labour battalions and other forms of compulsory labour imposed on political prisoners.
* Severe restrictions on regional languages, cultures and autonomist expression (especially Catalan and Basque).
* Strong Catholic confessional influence on education, marriage and public morality, producing additional controls on personal and associational life.
* Retention of core criminal law for ordinary private matters alongside a parallel political-repressive jurisdiction.
* Absence of a biological-racial extermination programme of the National Socialist type, though anti-Masonic and anti-leftist campaigns were intense.

### Expanded Analysis

The restrictive profile of Early Francoism is defined by the comprehensive elimination of political pluralism, the severe and prolonged punishment of the Civil War losers, and the imposition of a confessional-authoritarian order on public and associational life. Under Direct Restriction Only these are scored as direct prohibitions and compulsions. Negative-Only Weighting records the full weight of the one-party system, press monopoly, political tribunals and the differential repression of the defeated. Residual private and religious spheres existed but were heavily conditioned by the regime’s moral and political requirements; they generate no upward credit.

The Endos/Xenos differential is pronounced. The defeated Republican population, leftists, regional nationalists and other designated opponents faced systematically worse treatment—imprisonment, economic exclusion, forced labour and, in the early years, execution—than the core population that accepted or benefited from the Nationalist victory. This constitutes a clear, stable and discretely targetable divergence satisfying the optional-split rule.

**Key Shift:** Consolidation of a victorious authoritarian regime that combined one-party monopoly with systematic, long-term repression of the defeated, producing one of the more severe non-genocidal restriction profiles in the Fascism-country set.

### Why this produces Endos 22 / Xenos 12

The one-party system, press controls, political tribunals and systematic repression of the defeated produce an Endos score of 22. The Xenos score of 12 reflects the markedly higher intensity of coercion directed at the Republican side and other designated opponents. Negative-Only Weighting applies throughout. The scores sit below Austrofascism and Mature Italian Fascism, and above the genocidal floor occupied by National Socialism.
