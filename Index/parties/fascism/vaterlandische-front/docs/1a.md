# Core 1A: Fascism – Vaterländische Front
## Personal Autonomy Analysis
**Endos Score: 32**  
**Xenos Score: 22**

### Overview

Austrofascism (1933–1938), associated with Engelbert Dollfuss and Kurt Schuschnigg, constructed an authoritarian, clerical-corporatist regime that suppressed parliamentary democracy, banned opposition parties, and imposed significant controls on speech, association and the press. It differed from both Italian Fascism and National Socialism in its stronger reliance on Catholic corporatist ideology, its rejection of biological racism as a central organising principle, and its comparatively less totalising penetration of private life. The result is a restrictive profile that sits between Mature Italian Fascism and the more extreme National Socialist system, with a clear but narrower Endos/Xenos differential directed primarily at political opponents and, to a lesser extent, at National Socialists and leftists.

### Core 1A Relevant Policies / Elements

* Suspension of parliament and the progressive dismantling of the democratic constitution after 1933.
* Banning of the Communist Party, the Social Democratic Party (after the February 1934 fighting), and later the Austrian National Socialists.
* Establishment of a one-party Fatherland Front monopoly on political organisation.
* Significant press controls and censorship directed against opposition and critical voices.
* Corporatist restructuring of economic and social association under state and Catholic supervision.
* Use of detention, administrative measures and political policing against opponents.
* Retention of a stronger residual role for the Catholic Church and traditional institutions than in the Italian or German cases.
* Absence of a comprehensive biological-racial legal system comparable to the Nuremberg Laws.

### Expanded Analysis

The restrictive architecture of Austrofascism is defined by the replacement of parliamentary democracy with an authoritarian corporatist order and by the systematic suppression of organised political opposition. Political expression and association outside the Fatherland Front were prohibited or severely constrained. Under Direct Restriction Only these are scored as direct prohibitions on voluntary political activity. Negative-Only Weighting records the cumulative weight of the one-party framework, press controls and political policing. Residual spheres of religious and private life, together with the absence of a full totalitarian mobilisation apparatus, prevent an even lower score.

A clear Endos/Xenos differential exists. Socialists, Communists and later National Socialists faced markedly higher levels of repression, detention and exclusion than the core population that accepted the Fatherland Front order. Jews were not subjected to a systematic racial-legal regime of the German type, so the differential is driven primarily by political rather than racial criteria. The divergence is stable and discretely targetable, satisfying the optional-split rule at a moderate level.

**Key Shift:** Replacement of Austrian parliamentary democracy with a clerical-authoritarian, corporatist one-party order and the systematic suppression of organised opposition, producing a durable reduction in political autonomy without the full totalitarian or genocidal intensity of National Socialism.

### Why this produces Endos 32 / Xenos 22

The authoritarian one-party system, press controls and political repression produce an Endos score of 32. The Xenos score of 22 reflects the additional intensity directed at political opponents. Negative-Only Weighting applies. The scores sit above Mature Italian Fascism’s later phase and well above National Socialism, consistent with a less totalising and non-genocidal authoritarian model.
