# Core 1A: Communism – Castroism
## Personal Autonomy Analysis
**Endos Score: 24**  
**Xenos Score: 14**

### Overview

Castroism, as the political entity that held power in Cuba under Fidel Castro (and the early institutionalisation of the Cuban Communist regime from 1959 into the 1970s and beyond), constructed a one-party system that eliminated independent political expression, association and press while maintaining a somewhat less totalising grip on everyday private life than High Stalinism or Maoism. Political opposition was extinguished, independent media and civil society were suppressed, and designated opponents faced imprisonment and exile. At the same time, residual spheres of private and informal economic activity persisted longer than in the most extreme Communist cases, and mass terror on the Stalinist or Khmer Rouge scale was not the continuous centrepiece of rule. The result is a restrictive but not floor-level profile with a clear Endos/Xenos differential.

### Core 1A Relevant Policies / Elements

* Establishment of a one-party monopoly under the Cuban Communist Party and the elimination of competitive politics.
* Comprehensive controls on press, publishing and independent cultural expression.
* Suppression of independent trade unions, professional associations and civil-society organisations outside regime structures.
* Political imprisonment, forced labour and exile for opponents, including long-term incarceration of dissidents.
* Restrictions on movement and exit from the country, including the criminalisation of unauthorised departure.
* Surveillance and neighbourhood-level monitoring through Committees for the Defence of the Revolution.
* Residual informal economic and private spheres that remained larger than under High Stalinism or Maoism, though still politically conditioned.
* Targeting of designated political and social categories (former regime elements, independent socialists, later human-rights activists) for differential repression.

### Expanded Analysis

Castroism as a governing political force replaced the pre-revolutionary order with a single-party system backed by political policing and the suppression of independent organisation. Political expression and association outside the regime were prohibited. Under Direct Restriction Only these are scored as direct prohibitions on voluntary political and civic activity. Negative-Only Weighting records the cumulative architecture of one-party rule, press monopoly and political imprisonment; residual private and informal spheres that survived generate no upward credit but do prevent an even lower score.

A clear Endos/Xenos differential exists. Political opponents, dissidents and those labelled as counter-revolutionary faced markedly higher levels of imprisonment, harassment and exclusion than the core population that accepted or worked within regime structures. This constitutes a stable, discretely targetable divergence satisfying the optional-split rule.

**Key Shift:** Replacement of residual pluralism with a one-party system that extinguished independent political and civic life while retaining a somewhat larger residual private sphere than the most extreme Communist regimes, producing a mid-to-low restrictive profile within the Communism-country set.

### Why this produces Endos 24 / Xenos 14

The one-party monopoly, press controls, political imprisonment and exit restrictions produce an Endos score of 24. The Xenos score of 14 reflects the additional intensity directed at opponents and designated categories. Negative-Only Weighting applies throughout. The scores sit above Leninism’s early phase in some respects and well above Stalinism, Maoism and Juche, consistent with a repressive but not fully totalitarian-mobilisational model.
