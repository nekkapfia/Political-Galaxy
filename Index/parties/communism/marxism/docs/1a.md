# Core 1A: Communism – Marxism
## Personal Autonomy Analysis
**Endos Score: 35**  
**Xenos Score: 25**

### Overview

Marxism, treated here as the political-ideological core that informed later governing Communist entities rather than as a regime that itself held state power in pure form, contains a characteristic set of implications for personal autonomy. Classical Marxist theory subordinates individual rights and independent association to the requirements of class struggle and the dictatorship of the proletariat. When taken as a governing programme, it justifies the suppression of “bourgeois” political expression, the elimination of independent civil society rooted in private property, and the differential treatment of class enemies. Because pure classical Marxism never administered a state, the score reflects the restrictive logic inherent in its political programme as later operationalised by Leninist and other Communist parties, rather than a fully realised totalitarian system. The result is a moderately restrictive theoretical-political profile that sits above the historical governing entities that claimed to implement it.

### Core 1A Relevant Policies / Elements

* Theoretical subordination of individual political rights to the requirements of class struggle and proletarian dictatorship.
* Justification for the suppression of “bourgeois” parties, press and associations as instruments of class domination.
* Differential treatment of class enemies (bourgeoisie, residual exploiting classes) as legitimate targets of political restriction and expropriation.
* Rejection of permanent independent civil society and constitutional limitations on revolutionary power.
* Emphasis on collective over individual claims in the political and economic spheres.
* Absence, in classical form, of the fully developed totalitarian institutional apparatus (secret police, camps, continuous mass mobilisation) that later regimes added.
* Residual theoretical space for a future “withering away” of the state that was never operationalised by governing Communist parties.

### Expanded Analysis

Marxism as a political-ideological core supplies the justificatory framework for one-party dictatorship and the suppression of independent political and civil organisation once a revolutionary party holds power. Under Direct Restriction Only the scored weight is the set of restrictions that the programme itself legitimates: the elimination of bourgeois political expression, the rejection of permanent constitutional limits, and the differential treatment of class enemies. Negative-Only Weighting records these inherent restrictive implications; the theoretical promise of later emancipation generates no upward credit. Because classical Marxism did not itself construct the Cheka, the Gulag or the Cultural Revolution, the score remains higher than those of the governing entities that claimed to implement it.

A moderate Endos/Xenos differential is inherent in the class framework. The bourgeoisie and residual exploiting classes are explicitly designated as legitimate targets of political restriction and expropriation, producing a stable, discretely targetable divergence that satisfies the optional-split rule at a theoretical-political level.

**Key Shift:** Provision of a political-ideological programme that legitimates one-party dictatorship, the suppression of independent “bourgeois” organisation, and differential treatment of class enemies, without itself constructing the full totalitarian institutional apparatus of later Communist regimes.

### Why this produces Endos 35 / Xenos 25

The inherent restrictive logic of proletarian dictatorship, the suppression of bourgeois political forms, and class-based differential treatment produce an Endos score of 35. The Xenos score of 25 reflects the explicit targeting of class enemies. Negative-Only Weighting applies. The scores sit well above Leninism, Stalinism and the other governing Communist entities, consistent with a theoretical-political core that supplies justification rather than a fully realised totalitarian system.
