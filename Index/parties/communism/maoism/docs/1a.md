# Core 1A: Communism – Maoism
## Personal Autonomy Analysis
**Endos Score: 11**  
**Xenos Score: 6**

### Overview

Maoism as a governing political force produces a near-floor Core 1A profile defined by total Party control of speech and association, mass political campaigns (including the Cultural Revolution), laogai forced-labour camps, class-label systems, mandatory ideological struggle, and designated enemy categories subject to struggle sessions, imprisonment, exile and killing. Cumulative weight sits in the extreme-restriction / upper floor bands. A clear Endos/Xenos split applies.

### Core 1A Relevant Policies / Elements

* Total monopoly of political expression, press, education and association under Party control; no legal independent political speech or organisation.
* Mass campaigns (Anti-Rightist Movement, Great Leap Forward political coercion, Cultural Revolution) mobilising coercion against designated ideological and class enemies through struggle sessions, public humiliation, imprisonment and killing.
* Laogai and laojiao forced-labour and re-education-through-labour systems as standing instruments of political and economic coercion.
* Class-label and political-file systems that permanently marked individuals and families, constraining education, work, movement and marriage prospects.
* Mandatory ideological study, self-criticism and “thought reform” programmes designed to correct incorrect beliefs and enforce alignment with official ideology — explicit attitude-correction architecture.
* People’s commune and collectivisation campaigns enforcing work discipline, movement control and political command over daily household life (collective ownership structure itself is out of scope for Core 1A).
* Designated Xenos categories (landlords, rich peasants, rightists, “ox demons and snake spirits,” and certain ethnic or religious targets in campaign phases) subject to intensified violence, expropriation and deprivation.
* Near-elimination of residual private political space; family and personal relations repeatedly politicised during peak campaign years.
* Ordinary violent-crime prohibitions exist but do not define placement.

### Expanded Analysis

Maoism combined Leninist-Stalinist one-party monopoly with distinctive campaign mobilisation and thought-reform intensity. Political speech outside the Party line was impossible. During the Cultural Revolution and earlier mass campaigns, coercion extended into workplaces, schools and families through struggle sessions and permanent class labels.

For Endos — ordinary people not labelled as class or political enemies — public expression was limited to prescribed ideology; participation in study and campaigns was compulsory; daily work and household life were placed under political and communal command. Placement at 11 reflects extreme multi-domain restriction with periodic surges of mobilised violence that still operated as system policy rather than breakdown.

For Xenos — class enemies, rightists, campaign targets — struggle sessions, camps, exile and killing were routine instruments. That differential places Xenos at the upper floor.

Framing as continuous revolution or mass line is discarded. Only the observable restriction architecture is scored.

### Why this produces Endos 11 / Xenos 6

Speech and association monopoly, mass political campaigns, laogai, class labels, mandatory thought reform and commune-level personal command place Endos at 11. Designated enemy categories under struggle, camp and lethal policies produce Xenos 6.
