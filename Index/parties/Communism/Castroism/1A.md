# Core 1A: Communism – Castroism
## Personal Autonomy Analysis
**Endos Score: 15**
**Xenos Score: 10**

### Overview

Castroism builds a durable, multi-domain restriction architecture: one-party monopoly of speech and association, block-level CDR surveillance, long-run exit controls, mandatory political education and mass-organisation participation, state media monopoly, and designated counter-revolutionary categories subject to imprisonment, forced labour and professional destruction. The system is denser and more continuous than episodic terror regimes, but short of hereditary total physiological control. A stable Endos/Xenos split applies.

### Core 1A Relevant Policies / Elements

* Communist Party of Cuba as sole legal party; no competitive politics or independent political organisation.
* State monopoly of press, broadcasting and publishing; independent media prohibited by system design.
* Committees for the Defense of the Revolution (CDR), founded 28 September 1960: block-level “eyes and ears” with vigilance, ideology and community roles; membership later reported in the multi-million range (order of ~8 million of ~11 million population in some official figures); functions historically included night patrol, neighbour reporting, listing possessions of would-be emigrants, and support for security enforcement.
* Exit-permit regime (tarjeta blanca) and invitation-letter requirements for decades; unauthorised departure criminalised in earlier periods; legal exit treated as a politically allocated privilege until Decree-Law 302 (effective 14 January 2013) abolished the ordinary exit permit for most citizens, with residual limits on “vital” professionals and cadres.
* Mandatory political education and mass-organisation participation (CDR, CTC, FMC, UJC and related bodies) in workplaces, schools and neighbourhoods.
* UMAP (Unidades Militares de Ayuda a la Producción), November 1965–July 1968, mainly Camagüey: agricultural forced-labour camps for categories including religious nonconformists (e.g. Jehovah’s Witnesses), homosexuals, “antisocials,” some intellectuals and others labelled deviant or counter-revolutionary; scale commonly estimated 25,000–35,000 internees; closed amid criticism.
* Later prison, labour and professional-exclusion systems for designated counter-revolutionaries and dissidents.
* Professional, educational and rationing consequences for political nonconformity beyond formal criminal penalties.
* Ordinary violent-crime prohibitions exist but do not define placement.

### Expanded Analysis

The restriction set is systematic rather than episodic. Public political speech outside the Party line is closed for the general population; CDR and workplace structures make local association and household political reliability continuously observable; travel abroad was, for most of the system’s history, a controlled privilege rather than a default right. Mandatory education and mass organisations extend orthodoxy into daily institutional life.

For Endos — citizens not actively labelled counter-revolutionary — the cumulative architecture (speech monopoly, CDR density, exit controls for the long formative decades, mandatory political participation) sits in the extreme-restriction / upper ideological-enforcement region. The 2013 travel reform removes one major instrument for the later period but does not erase the multi-decade final-set pattern that defines Castroism as a governing system.

For Xenos — active dissidents, designated counter-revolutionaries, and groups targeted in particular campaigns including UMAP categories — imprisonment, camps, professional destruction and sustained harassment are routine instruments. That differential places Xenos lower while remaining above absolute floor bands reserved for systematic extermination or forced medical experimentation as the governing mode.

Framing as anti-imperialist defence is discarded. Only observable restriction architecture is scored. Residual law continues unless explicitly repealed; the long exit-control regime therefore remains in the system characterisation even after 2013 liberalisation of ordinary travel.

### Why this produces Endos 15 / Xenos 10

One-party speech and association monopoly, CDR surveillance at scale, multi-decade exit controls, mandatory political education, and suppression of independent media place Endos in the mid-teens. Designated counter-revolutionary and UMAP-era camp/exclusion policies pull Xenos to the low teens / upper floor border without requiring band-5 genocide characterisation as the system’s normal mode.
