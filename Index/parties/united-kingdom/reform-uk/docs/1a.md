# Core 1A: United Kingdom – Reform UK
## Personal Autonomy Analysis
**Endos Score: 78**  
**Xenos Score: N/A** (no split)

### Overview

Reform UK positions itself as the primary vehicle for restoring free speech by dismantling key elements of the post-1986 and post-2023 speech-restriction regime. Its platform centres on the repeal of the Online Safety Act and the substantial reduction of non-violent hate-speech and public-order speech offences. Among major British parties it proposes the largest net reduction in direct restrictions on voluntary expression and online communication. The residual baseline of direct-harm and intentional-threat offences remains, preventing a still-higher score, but the absence of new identity-based speech offences and the lack of any formal dual legal structure mean that no Endos/Xenos split is applied.

### Core 1A Relevant Policies / Elements

* Explicit commitment to repeal the Online Safety Act 2023 as a first-order priority, on the grounds that it suppresses freedom of speech and forces platform censorship of anti-government and other disfavoured speech.
* Advocacy for the repeal or radical narrowing of Section 5 of the Public Order Act 1986 (speech causing harassment, alarm or distress) and of non-violent “hate speech” / stirring-up offences.
* Support for replacing the current suite of communications offences with a narrower offence limited to intentionally menacing and targeted communications.
* Rejection of DEI-driven institutional speech codes and related administrative controls on expression.
* Defence of religious freedom within a framework that prioritises British cultural and Christian-heritage norms, without introducing new speech offences targeted at religious criticism.
* Opposition to election-period platform amplification duties or “trusted news” prominence requirements that would treat social media as broadcast media.
* No proposal for new content-based speech offences keyed to identity or ideology.
* Retention of core criminal prohibitions on violence, true threats and direct physical harm.

### Expanded Analysis

Reform UK’s profile is the least restrictive among the major parties examined. By proposing the removal of the Online Safety Act and the substantial narrowing of the Public Order and hate-speech apparatus, it would reduce the cumulative weight of direct restrictions on voluntary expression and online communication. Under Negative-Only Weighting the score rises relative to parties that retain or expand those frameworks; the rise is produced solely by the removal of existing restrictive layers rather than by any positive credit for free-speech rhetoric. The residual baseline of direct-harm and intentional-threat offences continues to exert downward weight and prevents a higher placement. The platform does not create a stable, discretely targetable outgroup differential in the remaining restrictions, so the optional Endos/Xenos split is not applied.

The practical effect is a clear upward movement from the current post-2023 baseline (approximately 58). The score of 78 sits at the top of the contemporary British party spectrum and is consistent with a programme that would reverse the densification that began with the Public Order Act 1986 and accelerated through the Communications Act and Online Safety Act layers.

**Key Shift relative to current baseline:** Major liberalisation through repeal of the Online Safety Act and substantial narrowing of non-violent public-order and hate-speech offences, producing the largest upward movement among major parties.
