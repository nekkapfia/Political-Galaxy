# Core 1A: United Kingdom – Scottish National Party
## Personal Autonomy Analysis
**Endos Score: 26**  
**Xenos Score: 22**

### Overview

The Scottish National Party, scored strictly on the restrictions that would exist under its governing programme, produces a restrictive Core 1A profile defined by the Hate Crime and Public Order (Scotland) Act 2021, the retained UK Online Safety Act, institutional measures that treat critical speech as potentially stirring up hatred, gender self-identification rules that generate secondary speech and association constraints, and robust enforcement of identity-hostile expression offences. Under pure final-set scoring only the restrictions that remain are counted. Framing and rights language are discarded. The denser Scottish hate-crime framework is the decisive instrument. The cumulative weight places the party inside the ideological-enforcement range, with a limited but justified Endos/Xenos differential arising from heightened scrutiny of speech critical of protected identity categories.

### Core 1A Relevant Policies / Elements

* Hate Crime and Public Order (Scotland) Act 2021, including the offence of stirring up hatred on the basis of race, religion, sexual orientation, transgender identity, age, disability and other characteristics. The Act expands protected characteristics beyond the English framework and, in important formulations, includes a “likely to” limb that lowers the threshold relative to intent-based English stirring-up offences; in some applications it reaches conduct in private settings.
* Retention of the broader UK Online Safety Act architecture as it applies in Scotland, with no Scottish opt-out or narrowing agenda.
* Institutional and educational measures — including public-sector equality duties, school and university guidance, and workplace policies — that treat certain forms of critical speech (especially on sex, gender and related topics) as potentially stirring up hatred or creating a hostile environment.
* Support for gender self-identification and associated institutional rules that generate secondary constraints on speech (misgendering treated as hostile or potentially hateful) and on association/access to single-sex spaces.
* Support for robust enforcement of existing public-order and communications offences against identity-hostile expression.
* Full retention of the Scottish hate-crime framework and the UK Online Safety Act with no proposal for substantial narrowing.
* Retention of Section 5-style and communications offences as they operate in Scotland.

### Expanded Analysis

Under pure final-set scoring only the restrictions listed above are counted. The Hate Crime and Public Order (Scotland) Act 2021 is the central densifying instrument. It expands the range of protected characteristics (explicitly including transgender identity and age) and lowers the threshold for criminal liability relative to the English and Welsh stirring-up offences by incorporating formulations that do not require the same degree of intent. Enforcement practice under Police Scotland guidance and recorded complaints has produced documented chilling effects on ordinary conversation, particularly around sex and gender. Together with the fully retained Online Safety Act and residual public-order offences, the Act produces a dense content-based speech control architecture. Framing is ignored under Direct Restriction Only and Negative-Only Weighting.

Gender self-identification policy, long supported by the SNP, adds a further layer. Once institutional rules treat affirmed gender as the operative category, speech that asserts biological sex (misgendering) and association decisions around single-sex spaces become subject to hostile-environment or potential hate-incident framing. These are direct secondary constraints on expression and association that form part of the final restriction set.

Institutional and educational measures extend the same logic into schools, universities and public-sector workplaces through equality duties and guidance that frame critical speech on protected characteristics as risk factors. These are not mere process; they function as enforceable orthodoxy requirements in institutional settings.

A limited Endos/Xenos differential is recorded. The Act’s protected characteristics, self-identification rules and associated enforcement practice create heightened scrutiny of speech critical of certain identity categories (particularly around race, religion, sexual orientation and transgender identity). This constitutes a stable, discretely targetable divergence in intensity of expression controls, satisfying the optional-split rule. The differential is modest rather than absolute, but it is consistent and observable in both statute and institutional practice.

Mandatory attitude-correction training is not evidenced as a central, party-defining feature in the same way as under the Green Party. This prevents a still lower placement while leaving the architecture firmly inside the ideological-enforcement band rather than the physical-coercion bands. The absence of any comprehensive repeal agenda means the dense Scottish and UK speech-restriction layers remain fully present and fully scored.

**Key Shift:** Affirmative operation of a denser Scottish hate-crime regime, gender self-identification speech/association constraints, and the UK Online Safety Act architecture as the characteristic governing programme of the party.

### Why this produces Endos 26 / Xenos 22

The cumulative weight of the final restriction set — expanded Scottish hate-crime offences with lowered threshold, Online Safety Act retention, institutional orthodoxy measures, self-identification speech/association constraints, and residual public-order offences — places Endos at 26 inside the ideological-enforcement range. Heightened identity-targeted intensity produces Xenos 22. Only the restrictions that remain are scored; framing is ignored. The placement is consistent with the progressive-party scoring guide: a dense identity-speech package without the additional mandatory attitude-correction layer still belongs in the mid-20s, not in the 40s.
