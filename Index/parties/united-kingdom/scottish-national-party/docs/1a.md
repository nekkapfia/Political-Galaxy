# Core 1A: United Kingdom – Scottish National Party
## Personal Autonomy Analysis
**Endos Score: 45**  
**Xenos Score: 42**

### Overview

The Scottish National Party operates within and strongly supports the Hate Crime and Public Order (Scotland) Act 2021 framework, which extends stirring-up-hatred offences and applies a broader set of expression controls than the equivalent English and Welsh statutes in certain respects. Its platform prioritises identity-based protections and institutional measures against hatred. The party retains the UK Online Safety Act architecture as it applies in Scotland and shows no appetite for narrowing either the Scottish hate-crime statute or the wider public-order and communications offences. The combination produces one of the more restrictive profiles among major British parties.

### Core 1A Relevant Policies / Elements

* Support for and continued operation under the Hate Crime and Public Order (Scotland) Act 2021, including the offence of stirring up hatred on the basis of race, religion, sexual orientation, transgender identity, age, disability, and other characteristics. In some formulations the Act reaches private settings.
* Retention of the broader UK Online Safety Act architecture as it applies in Scotland.
* Institutional and educational measures that treat certain forms of critical speech as potentially stirring up hatred or creating a hostile environment.
* Support for robust enforcement of existing public-order and communications offences against identity-hostile expression.
* Opposition to repeal or substantial narrowing of the Scottish hate-crime framework or the UK Online Safety Act.
* No proposal to reduce the scope of Section 5-style or communications offences as they operate in Scotland.
* Retention of core criminal prohibitions on violence, true threats and direct physical harm.

### Expanded Analysis

The SNP’s restrictive profile is shaped by the denser Scottish hate-crime statute plus the retained UK online-safety baseline. The Hate Crime and Public Order (Scotland) Act 2021 is the decisive instrument: it expands the range of protected characteristics and, in important respects, lowers the threshold for criminal liability relative to the English and Welsh framework. Under Direct Restriction Only these are scored as direct content-based prohibitions on voluntary expression. Negative-Only Weighting records the cumulative weight of the Scottish Act together with the Online Safety Act and residual public-order and communications offences. No upward credit is available for rights language or for the absence of still-further expansions.

A limited Endos/Xenos differential is recorded. The Act’s protected characteristics and the associated enforcement practice create heightened scrutiny of speech critical of certain identity categories (particularly around race, religion, sexual orientation and transgender identity). This constitutes a stable, discretely targetable divergence in the intensity of expression controls, satisfying the optional-split rule. The differential is modest rather than absolute, but it is consistent and observable in both statute and institutional practice.

**Key Shift relative to current baseline:** Affirmative defence and operation of a denser Scottish hate-crime regime on top of the UK Online Safety Act architecture, producing a lower score than parties that seek to narrow these frameworks and a limited but justified Endos/Xenos split.

### Why this produces Endos 45 / Xenos 42

The cumulative weight of the Hate Crime and Public Order (Scotland) Act 2021 together with the Online Safety Act and residual public-order offences yields an Endos score of 45 under Negative-Only Weighting. A limited Xenos differential (42) is recorded because the Act’s protected characteristics and enforcement practice create heightened scrutiny of speech critical of certain identity categories, constituting a stable outgroup differential in intensity of restriction. The scores sit at the more restrictive end of the contemporary British party spectrum, consistent with a preference for broader rather than narrower application of content-based speech controls.
