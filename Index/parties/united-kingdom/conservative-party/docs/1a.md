# Core 1A: United Kingdom – Conservative Party
## Personal Autonomy Analysis
**Endos Score: 68**  
**Xenos Score: N/A** (no split)

### Overview

The Conservative Party presents itself as the defender of ordered liberty and adult free expression within a framework of child protection and public order. Its current leadership emphasises that government should draw clear, enforceable lines to protect minors online while refusing to police adult speech or tell adults what to think. The party retains the existing statutory baseline of hate-speech and public-order offences while signalling greater scepticism toward expansive non-crime hate-incident recording and over-zealous enforcement of communications offences. The overall profile is one of managed retention of the post-1986 and post-2023 speech-restriction architecture rather than either wholesale repeal or further expansion.

### Core 1A Relevant Policies / Elements

* Retention of the Online Safety Act 2023 framework for illegal content and child-protection duties, with emphasis on highly effective age assurance rather than broad adult content controls.
* Explicit policy preference for a clear under-16 social-media age limit while protecting free choice and free speech for adults.
* Maintenance of existing Public Order Act 1986 (including Section 5 “harassment, alarm or distress”) and Racial and Religious Hatred Act provisions as part of the baseline legal order.
* Support for reform or curtailment of non-crime hate incident recording practices that have previously captured lawful speech.
* Opposition to new election-period social-media amplification or “trusted news” prominence duties that would treat platforms as broadcasters.
* Acceptance of communications offences under the Communications Act 2003 and Malicious Communications Act 1988 where they target intentional threats or false communications intended to cause harm, while criticising over-broad application to non-threatening speech.
* No proposal to introduce new identity-based speech offences or to expand the existing stirring-up framework.
* No proposal for wholesale repeal of the core hate-speech or public-order statutes.

### Expanded Analysis

The party’s restrictive profile is defined by continuity with the dense post-1986 and post-2023 baseline. It does not seek to dismantle the Online Safety Act’s illegal-content and child-protection architecture, nor does it propose the repeal of Section 5 or the racial-hatred provisions. At the same time it resists further densification: it opposes election-period platform duties that would treat social media as broadcast media, and it has signalled discomfort with the administrative expansion of non-crime hate-incident recording. Under Direct Restriction Only the scored weight is the set of prohibitions and regulatory duties the party would maintain. Negative-Only Weighting records that retained architecture; rhetorical commitments to adult free speech generate no upward credit. The absence of new identity-targeted speech offences and the lack of a formal dual legal structure mean that no Endos/Xenos split is warranted.

The practical effect is a mid-to-upper score that sits below parties proposing major repeals (Reform UK, Restore Britain) and above parties that seek to expand or intensify the existing toolkit (Labour, Greens, SNP). The score reflects the cumulative direct restrictions on expression and online communication that would remain in force under a Conservative government rather than any positive valuation of its free-speech language.

**Key Shift relative to current baseline:** Managed retention of the existing speech-restriction architecture with modest administrative push-back against non-crime recording and resistance to further platform-duty expansion, producing no major upward or downward movement from the post-2023 status quo.
