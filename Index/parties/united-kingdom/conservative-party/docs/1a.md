### Overview

The Conservative Party, scored strictly on the restrictions that would exist under its governing programme, produces a mid-range Core 1A profile defined by retention of the Online Safety Act’s illegal-content and child-protection architecture, retention of Public Order Act Section 5 and Racial and Religious Hatred Act provisions, and retention of communications offences, without the addition of new identity-specific speech offences or election-period platform amplification duties. Framing and free-speech rhetoric are discarded under Direct Restriction Only and Negative-Only Weighting. The cumulative weight of the final set is essentially the post-2023 UK baseline without significant intensification and without major liberalisation. No Endos/Xenos split is applied.

### Core 1A Relevant Policies / Elements

* Retention of the Online Safety Act 2023 framework for illegal content and child-protection duties, with emphasis on age assurance and platform accountability for clearly illegal material. The party does not advance a full repeal agenda of the Reform UK type; the core regulatory architecture remains.
* Maintenance of existing Public Order Act 1986 provisions, including Section 5 (speech causing harassment, alarm or distress), and Racial and Religious Hatred Act stirring-up offences as part of the baseline legal order.
* Acceptance of communications offences under the Communications Act 2003 and Malicious Communications Act 1988 where they target intentional threats or false communications intended to cause harm, while signalling discomfort with over-broad application to non-threatening speech.
* Support for reform or curtailment of non-crime hate-incident recording practices that have previously captured lawful speech; this is an administrative narrowing rather than a repeal of primary speech offences.
* Opposition to new election-period social-media amplification or “trusted news” prominence duties that would treat platforms as broadcasters.
* No proposal to introduce new identity-based speech offences or to expand the existing stirring-up framework.
* No proposal for wholesale repeal of the core hate-speech or public-order statutes.
* Retention of core criminal prohibitions on violence, true threats and direct physical harm.

### Expanded Analysis

Under pure final-set scoring only the restrictions listed above are counted. The Conservative Party retains the decisive post-2023 layers: the Online Safety Act’s illegal-content and child-protection duties, Section 5 and related public-order speech offences, and the communications offences that criminalise certain forms of non-violent expression. It does not add the concrete expansions characteristic of Labour (election-period platform duties, anti-Muslim hostility definition, under-16 measures as a central push) and it does not add the mandatory attitude-correction or dense identity-orthodoxy layers characteristic of the Green Party. At the same time it does not remove the major speech-restriction layers in the manner of Reform UK.

Rhetorical commitments to adult free speech and ordered liberty generate no upward credit. Administrative discomfort with non-crime hate-incident recording is a modest process-level push-back; it does not eliminate the underlying primary offences and therefore does not substantially lighten the final set. Opposition to election-period amplification duties prevents further densification but, under pure final-set logic, the absence of a new restriction is not itself a liberalising force that raises the score.

No Endos/Xenos split is applied. The platform does not create or intensify a stable, discretely targetable out-group differential in the application of expression restrictions. Differential enforcement patterns that exist in the wider system are not uniquely formalised or amplified by Conservative policy into an out-group divergence of the kind required by the optional-split rule.

The architecture sits above the progressive densification cluster (Green, SNP, Sinn Féin, Plaid, Labour) because it lacks their concrete expansions and identity-orthodoxy emphasis. It sits below Reform UK and Restore Britain because the major Online Safety Act and public-order speech layers remain fully present. The cumulative weight is therefore mid-range — the post-2023 baseline without major movement in either direction.

**Key Shift:** Managed retention of the existing Online Safety Act, public-order and communications architecture without significant intensification and without wholesale liberalisation, as the characteristic governing programme of the party.
