# Core 1A: United Kingdom – Workers Party of Britain
## Personal Autonomy Analysis
**Endos Score: 38**  
**Xenos Score: 33**

### Overview

The Workers Party of Britain, scored strictly on the restrictions that would exist under its governing programme, produces a mixed Core 1A profile defined by retention of the Online Safety Act, affirmative support for public-order and hate-speech provisions targeting racist, anti-Muslim and anti-immigrant expression, and retention of core non-violent speech offences, combined with resistance to some gender-identity institutional controls. Framing is discarded under Direct Restriction Only and Negative-Only Weighting. The final set is lighter than the Green/SNP/Sinn Féin/Plaid cluster because mandatory attitude-correction training and the densest gender-orthodoxy layers are absent, but heavier than pure baseline retention because identity-speech enforcement around race and Islam remains actively supported. A limited Endos/Xenos differential is recorded.

### Core 1A Relevant Policies / Elements

* Retention of the Online Safety Act 2023; the party has not placed platform-duty liberalisation at the centre of its programme and advances no high-priority repeal agenda.
* Affirmative support for public-order and hate-speech provisions that address racist, anti-Muslim or anti-immigrant expression, with explicit defence of enforcement in these areas.
* Retention of core non-violent speech offences and communications provisions that criminalise certain forms of expression.
* Criticism of selective or progressive-leaning applications of hate-speech and institutional speech codes, particularly around gender identity and certain cultural issues; this resistance means some gender-identity orthodoxy layers present under Green/SNP programmes are absent.
* Opposition to some forms of institutional DEI-driven speech regulation while defending other identity-based restrictions (especially race and religion).
* No proposal for election-period platform amplification duties of the Labour type.
* Retention of core criminal prohibitions on violence, true threats and direct physical harm.

### Expanded Analysis

Under pure final-set scoring only the restrictions that remain are counted. The Workers Party retains the Online Safety Act and actively defends hate-speech and public-order enforcement around race, Islam and immigration. These layers therefore stay on the list and exert downward weight. At the same time the party resists parts of the gender-identity institutional package; self-identification speech constraints and related orthodoxy measures that appear under the Green and SNP programmes are not part of its final set.

The result is a selective rather than comprehensive identity-speech architecture. Criminal and regulatory controls on speech characterised as racist or anti-Muslim are affirmed; parallel institutional intensification around gender identity is resisted. Framing is ignored. Rhetorical criticism of progressive speech codes generates no upward credit; it is relevant only insofar as it means certain restrictive layers are absent.

A limited Endos/Xenos differential is recorded. The party’s selective defence of restrictions on speech critical of Islam, immigration or related topics, combined with resistance to other identity controls, creates a modest but observable divergence in the intensity of expression controls. This satisfies the optional-split rule at a limited level. The differential is narrower than formal dual legal systems but is consistent with the party’s stated priorities.

The architecture sits above the Green/SNP/Sinn Féin/Plaid cluster because mandatory attitude-correction training and the densest gender-orthodoxy layers are absent, and below pure baseline-retention parties because identity-speech enforcement around race and Islam remains actively supported. The cumulative weight therefore sits in the high 30s.

**Key Shift:** Selective defence of existing hate-speech and public-order architecture (especially around race and Islam) combined with resistance to some progressive gender-identity institutional expansions as the characteristic governing programme of the party.

### Why this produces Endos 38 / Xenos 33

The cumulative weight of the final restriction set — Online Safety Act retained, affirmative enforcement of race/Islam/immigration-related hate-speech and public-order provisions, core communications offences retained, but without mandatory attitude-correction training and without the densest gender-identity orthodoxy layers — places Endos at 38. Heightened application to specific identity-critical speech produces Xenos 33. Only the restrictions that remain are scored; framing is ignored. The placement is consistent with the progressive-party scoring guide: selective identity-speech densification without the full Green-style package belongs in the high 30s.
