# Core 1A: United Kingdom – Workers Party of Britain
## Personal Autonomy Analysis
**Endos Score: 51**  
**Xenos Score: 47**

### Overview

The Workers Party of Britain, associated with George Galloway, combines a left-economic programme with a distinctive stance on speech, foreign policy and identity issues. On Core 1A it occupies a mid-to-lower position. The party is critical of certain progressive identity frameworks and of what it regards as selective enforcement of hate-speech rules, yet it does not advance a comprehensive repeal agenda for the Online Safety Act or the core public-order and stirring-up offences. It retains support for robust restrictions on speech it characterises as racist or imperialist while resisting some of the newer gender-identity and related institutional controls. The result is a mixed profile with a modest Endos/Xenos differential.

### Core 1A Relevant Policies / Elements

* No high-priority commitment to repeal the Online Safety Act 2023; the party has not placed platform-duty liberalisation at the centre of its programme.
* Retention of public-order and hate-speech provisions that address racist, anti-Muslim or anti-immigrant expression, with affirmative support for enforcement in these areas.
* Criticism of selective or progressive-leaning applications of hate-speech and institutional speech codes, particularly around gender identity and certain cultural issues.
* Opposition to some forms of institutional DEI-driven speech regulation while defending other identity-based restrictions.
* No proposal for election-period platform amplification duties of the Labour type.
* Retention of core criminal prohibitions on violence, true threats and direct physical harm.
* Limited engagement with non-crime hate-incident recording; the party has expressed scepticism toward administrative expansion in some contexts.

### Expanded Analysis

The Workers Party’s restrictive profile is mixed. It defends significant portions of the existing content-based speech architecture (especially around race and religion) while resisting other identity-based expansions. Under Direct Restriction Only the scored weight is the set of prohibitions the party would maintain or intensify. Negative-Only Weighting records the retained Online Safety Act and public-order baseline together with affirmative support for certain hate-speech enforcement; criticism of progressive institutional codes generates no upward credit. The absence of a comprehensive repeal agenda keeps the score well below Reform UK and Restore Britain.

A limited Endos/Xenos differential is recorded. The party’s selective defence of restrictions on speech critical of Islam, immigration or related topics, combined with resistance to other identity controls, creates a modest but observable divergence in the intensity of expression controls. This satisfies the optional-split rule at a limited level.

**Key Shift relative to current baseline:** Selective defence of existing hate-speech and public-order architecture with resistance to some progressive institutional expansions, producing a mid-to-lower score and a limited Endos/Xenos differential.

### Why this produces Endos 51 / Xenos 47

Negative-Only Weighting applied to the retained Online Safety Act and public-order architecture, plus affirmative support for certain identity-based speech restrictions, yields an Endos score of 51. A limited Xenos differential (47) is recorded because of the selective intensification of controls on particular forms of critical speech. The scores sit close to Labour and above the SNP/Green cluster, consistent with a mixed rather than systematically liberalising or densifying stance.
