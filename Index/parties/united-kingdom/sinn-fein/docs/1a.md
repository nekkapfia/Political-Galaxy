# Core 1A: United Kingdom – Sinn Féin
## Personal Autonomy Analysis
**Endos Score: 27**  
**Xenos Score: 23**

### Overview

Sinn Féin, scored strictly on the restrictions that would exist under its governing programme, produces a restrictive Core 1A profile defined by full retention and affirmative defence of the Online Safety Act, public-order and hate-speech provisions, institutional measures against hate speech, sectarianism and discrimination, and communications offences that criminalise a range of non-violent expression. Framing, equality language and anti-sectarian justifications are discarded under Direct Restriction Only and Negative-Only Weighting. The cumulative weight of the final set places the party inside the ideological-enforcement range, with a limited Endos/Xenos differential arising from heightened focus on identity-critical speech. The architecture is denser than pure baseline retention and lighter than the Green package because mandatory attitude-correction training is not evidenced as a central, party-defining feature.

### Core 1A Relevant Policies / Elements

* Support for the continued operation of the Online Safety Act 2023 framework as it applies across the United Kingdom, including illegal-content duties and platform accountability. There is no repeal or substantial narrowing agenda.
* Retention and affirmative defence of public-order and hate-speech provisions that address identity-hostile expression, including speech characterised as sectarian, racist or otherwise hostile to protected groups.
* Support for institutional and educational measures aimed at combating hate speech, sectarianism and discrimination. These measures extend into public-sector, educational and community settings and treat speech critical of protected characteristics or of the party’s equality framework as potentially harmful.
* Acceptance of communications offences and related statutes that criminalise a range of non-violent expression.
* Equality and anti-discrimination frameworks that generate secondary institutional pressure on expression in public and quasi-public settings, including hostile-environment and potential disciplinary risk for speech that conflicts with institutional orthodoxy.
* No proposal to repeal or substantially narrow the core hate-speech, public-order or online-safety architecture.
* Retention of core criminal prohibitions on violence, true threats and direct physical harm.

### Expanded Analysis

Under pure final-set scoring only the restrictions listed above are counted. Sinn Féin treats the existing architecture of platform duties, public-order offences and hate-speech provisions as necessary protections and retains them in full. The Northern Ireland context adds a further layer: anti-sectarian and equality-duty frameworks have long operated as institutional constraints on expression, and the party’s programme reinforces rather than relaxes them. Framing is ignored.

Each retained element is a direct prohibition or regulatory compulsion on voluntary expression. Institutional and educational measures against hate speech and sectarianism function as orthodoxy requirements in public and quasi-public settings; they are not mere process. Equality frameworks generate secondary but real constraints on speech that challenges protected characteristics or the prevailing institutional consensus.

A limited Endos/Xenos differential is recorded. The party’s emphasis on identity-based protections, anti-discrimination measures and institutional responses to hate speech creates heightened scrutiny of expression critical of certain protected characteristics. This produces a stable, discretely targetable divergence in the intensity of controls, satisfying the optional-split rule at a modest level. The differential is narrower than formal dual legal systems but is consistent with the party’s stated priorities and the operational practice of equality and anti-sectarian frameworks in Northern Ireland.

Mandatory attitude-correction training is not evidenced as a central, party-defining national feature of the Green type. This keeps the score marginally above the Green Party placement while remaining inside the same broad ideological-enforcement band. The absence of any comprehensive repeal agenda means the dense post-1986 and post-2023 speech-restriction layers, together with Northern Ireland-specific equality and anti-sectarian institutional constraints, remain fully present and fully scored.

**Key Shift:** Affirmative defence of the existing Online Safety Act, public-order and hate-speech architecture combined with equality- and anti-sectarian institutional emphasis as the characteristic governing programme of the party.

### Why this produces Endos 27 / Xenos 23

The cumulative weight of the final restriction set — Online Safety Act retention, public-order and hate-speech provisions, institutional orthodoxy measures against hate speech and sectarianism, communications offences, and equality-framework pressure on expression — places Endos at 27 inside the ideological-enforcement range. Heightened application to identity-critical speech produces Xenos 23. Only the restrictions that remain are scored; framing is ignored. The placement is consistent with the progressive-party scoring guide: dense identity-speech and institutional orthodoxy without the additional mandatory attitude-correction layer belongs in the mid-to-high 20s.
