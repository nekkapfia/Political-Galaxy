# Core 1A: United Kingdom – Sinn Féin
## Personal Autonomy Analysis
**Endos Score: 46**  
**Xenos Score: 42**

### Overview

Sinn Féin operates primarily within the Northern Ireland legal and political framework while engaging with the wider UK Online Safety Act and public-order architecture. Its platform prioritises equality, anti-discrimination and the protection of minority and identity groups, which in practice translates into retention and affirmative defence of the existing content-based speech architecture rather than any programme of narrowing. The party does not propose repeal of the Online Safety Act, Section 5-style public-order offences, or the stirring-up provisions. The result is a restrictive profile close to that of the SNP, Plaid Cymru and the Green Party, with a modest Endos/Xenos differential arising from heightened focus on identity-critical speech.

### Core 1A Relevant Policies / Elements

* Support for the continued operation of the Online Safety Act 2023 framework as it applies across the United Kingdom, including illegal-content duties and platform accountability.
* Retention and affirmative defence of public-order and hate-speech provisions that address identity-hostile expression.
* Support for institutional and educational measures aimed at combating hate speech, sectarianism and discrimination, including speech critical of protected characteristics.
* Acceptance of communications offences and related statutes that criminalise a range of non-violent expression.
* No proposal to repeal or substantially narrow the core hate-speech, public-order or online-safety architecture.
* Emphasis on equality and anti-discrimination frameworks that can generate secondary institutional pressure on expression in public and quasi-public settings.
* Retention of core criminal prohibitions on violence, true threats and direct physical harm.

### Expanded Analysis

Sinn Féin’s restrictive profile is driven by continuity with, and defence of, the dense post-1986 and post-2023 speech-restriction baseline. The party treats the existing architecture of platform duties, public-order offences and hate-speech provisions as necessary protections rather than as candidates for liberalisation. Under Direct Restriction Only the scored weight is the set of prohibitions and regulatory duties the party would maintain. Negative-Only Weighting records that retained architecture; equality and anti-sectarian language generates no upward credit. The absence of any repeal agenda places the party well below Reform UK and Restore Britain and in the same band as the SNP, Plaid Cymru and the Greens.

A limited Endos/Xenos differential is recorded. The party’s emphasis on identity-based protections, anti-discrimination measures and institutional responses to hate speech creates heightened scrutiny of expression critical of certain protected characteristics. This produces a stable, discretely targetable divergence in the intensity of controls, satisfying the optional-split rule at a modest level. The differential is narrower than formal dual legal systems but is consistent with the party’s stated priorities.

**Key Shift relative to current baseline:** Affirmative defence of the existing Online Safety Act, public-order and hate-speech architecture with an equality- and anti-sectarian institutional emphasis, producing a lower-to-mid restrictive score and a limited Endos/Xenos differential.

### Why this produces Endos 46 / Xenos 42
