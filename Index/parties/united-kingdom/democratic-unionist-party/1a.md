# Core 1A: United Kingdom – Democratic Unionist Party
## Personal Autonomy Analysis
**Endos Score: 64**  
**Xenos Score: N/A** (no split)

### Overview

The Democratic Unionist Party operates primarily within the Northern Ireland legal and political framework while engaging with the wider UK Online Safety Act and public-order architecture. Its platform combines a strong emphasis on traditional social and religious values with a generally sceptical stance toward expansive identity-based speech regulation and platform duties. The party has not placed repeal of the Online Safety Act or the core hate-speech statutes at the centre of its programme in the same way as Reform UK, yet it is more resistant to further densification than Labour, the Greens or the SNP. The result is a mid-to-upper score without a formal Endos/Xenos split.

### Core 1A Relevant Policies / Elements

* Retention of the Online Safety Act framework as it applies across the United Kingdom, without a high-priority repeal commitment of the Reform UK type.
* Maintenance of existing public-order and communications offences, including those that address threatening or abusive speech.
* Resistance to further expansion of identity-based hate-speech categories or institutional speech codes that the party regards as culturally progressive overreach.
* Defence of traditional religious expression and conscience, particularly in the Northern Ireland context, without introducing new content-based speech offences targeted at secular or progressive criticism.
* No proposal for election-period platform amplification duties that would treat social media as broadcast media.
* Retention of core criminal prohibitions on violence, true threats and direct physical harm.
* Limited engagement with non-crime hate-incident recording practices; the party has expressed scepticism toward administrative expansion of speech monitoring.

### Expanded Analysis

The DUP’s restrictive profile is defined by continuity with the existing statutory baseline tempered by cultural and religious conservatism that resists progressive expansion of identity-based controls. Under Direct Restriction Only the scored weight is the set of prohibitions and regulatory duties the party would maintain. Negative-Only Weighting records the retained Online Safety Act and public-order architecture; traditionalist language generates no upward credit. The absence of a high-priority repeal agenda keeps the score below Reform UK and Restore Britain, while resistance to further densification and identity-focused institutional measures keeps it above Labour, the Greens and the SNP.

No Endos/Xenos split is applied. Although the party’s religious and cultural priorities create a distinctive emphasis, they do not generate a stable, discretely targetable dual structure of expression controls of the kind required by the optional-split rule. Differential intensity that exists in the wider system is not uniquely amplified by DUP policy into a formal outgroup divergence.

**Key Shift relative to current baseline:** Continuity with the post-2023 speech-restriction architecture combined with resistance to progressive identity-based expansion, producing a mid-to-upper score without major liberalisation or further densification.
