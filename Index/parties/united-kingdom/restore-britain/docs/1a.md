# Core 1A: United Kingdom – Restore Britain
## Personal Autonomy Analysis
**Endos Score: 74**  
**Xenos Score: 49**

### Overview

Restore Britain, led by Rupert Lowe MP, positions itself to the right of Reform UK. Its Core 1A profile is defined by two decisive and opposing movements: a major liberalisation of speech restrictions through repeal of the Online Safety Act and related opinion-policing instruments, and the simultaneous introduction of new direct restrictions on religious expression and self-presentation via a ban on the burqa and niqab (with parallel positions reported on religious slaughter). The net result is a high Endos score driven by speech liberalisation, tempered by a clear Endos/Xenos differential arising from the new identity-based religious-expression controls.

### Core 1A Relevant Policies / Elements

* Explicit commitment to repeal the Online Safety Act 2023 in full, end the recording of non-crime hate incidents, repeal Communications Act provisions used against opinion, and release individuals imprisoned solely for expression of views.
* Ban on the burqa and niqab (full-face coverings) in public spaces. This is a direct restriction on religious expression and self-presentation.
* Reported support for restrictions on kosher and halal slaughter, constituting a further direct restriction on religious practice.
* Policing reforms framed around “equality before the law” and the removal of identity-specific doctrine; these are largely process-oriented and secondary for Core 1A.
* All mass-deportation, asylum-abolition, and net-negative immigration policies are Core 2A and are discarded for this slider.
* Death-penalty referendum and pure punishment measures are discarded.
* Retention of core criminal prohibitions on violence, true threats and direct physical harm.

### Expanded Analysis

The repeal of the Online Safety Act and the associated speech-policing architecture removes a major layer of direct restriction that currently weighs on the UK baseline (approximately 58 in the mid-2022 onward era). Under Negative-Only Weighting this produces a substantial upward movement for the core population. No new paternalistic or identity-based restrictions are applied to the Endos group, allowing the score to rise into the low-to-mid 70s.

The burqa/niqab ban (and any parallel religious-slaughter restriction) is a direct, content-based restriction on religious expression and self-presentation. It maps to the **44–50 Early ideological/identity-based controls** band. Because the restriction falls differentially and systematically on a clear, stable, discretely targetable group (primarily those for whom the practice is religiously or culturally required), an Endos/Xenos split is required under the authoritative Core 1A rules. The Xenos population still benefits from the speech liberalisation, which prevents the score from falling to the bottom of the 44–50 band. The net result is a mid-band placement within early ideological/identity controls.

Identical criteria and band language are applied to both columns. The split satisfies the rule requiring significant and consistent observable divergence. No Score 4 Floor issues arise. Punishment, process and chilling-effect elements have been discarded.

**Key Shift relative to current baseline:** Major speech liberalisation through Online Safety Act repeal combined with the introduction of new religious-expression restrictions that create a formal Endos/Xenos differential, producing a high Endos score and a substantially lower Xenos score.

### Why this produces Endos 74 / Xenos 49

- **Endos 74**: Removal of the Online Safety Act layer plus related opinion-policing tools raises the score from the current UK baseline of ~58 into the low-to-mid 70s. The absence of new restrictions on the core in-group keeps the score above the 60–69 betterment-of-society band.
- **Xenos 49**: The new religious-expression restrictions place the out-group inside the 44–50 band. Concurrent speech liberalisation provides a modest upward offset from the pure mid-point of that band, producing 49.

The scores are consistent with identical application of Core 1A criteria to both groups and with the optional-split rule.
