# Core 1A: United Kingdom – Liberal Democrats
## Personal Autonomy Analysis
**Endos Score: 62**  
**Xenos Score: N/A** (no split)

### Overview

The Liberal Democrats present themselves as the party of civil liberties and open society while accepting a regulated online environment and the enforcement of existing hate-speech and public-order laws. Their platform combines formal free-speech commitments with support for platform duties against illegal and harmful content. The party does not propose wholesale repeal of the Online Safety Act or the core hate-speech statutes, nor does it introduce new identity-specific speech offences or election-period amplification rules of the kind associated with Labour. The result is a mid-range score that sits between the higher-autonomy repeal parties and the more restrictive governing and Green/SNP positions.

### Core 1A Relevant Policies / Elements

* Support for the Online Safety Act’s core duties on illegal content and child protection, while emphasising the need for Ofcom to be properly resourced to enforce both restriction and free-expression safeguards.
* Retention of Public Order Act 1986, Racial and Religious Hatred Act, and communications offences as necessary limits on speech that incites hatred or violence.
* Advocacy for robust anti-SLAPP protections and other measures intended to shield journalists and whistleblowers from abusive litigation, which indirectly supports expression.
* Acceptance of platform-level content moderation for illegal material and certain categories of harmful content.
* Opposition to wholesale repeal of the Online Safety Act or core hate-speech statutes.
* Support for transparency and accountability mechanisms around online political advertising and content amplification.
* No proposal to introduce new identity-based speech offences or to expand the existing stirring-up framework in the manner of the anti-Muslim hostility definition.
* No proposal for election-period platform duties that would treat social media as broadcast media.

### Expanded Analysis

The party’s restrictive profile sits between the higher-autonomy parties and Labour/Greens. It accepts the continued operation of the Online Safety Act’s illegal-content and child-protection architecture and the long-standing Public Order and communications offences. At the same time it declines to support the further densification represented by election-period amplification rules or new identity-specific hostility definitions. Anti-SLAPP and transparency measures are process-oriented or protective of expression and do not themselves constitute new direct restrictions under Core 1A rules. Under Direct Restriction Only the scored weight is the set of prohibitions and regulatory duties the party would maintain. Negative-Only Weighting records that retained baseline; formal civil-liberties language generates no upward credit.

No Endos/Xenos split is applied. The platform does not create or intensify a stable, discretely targetable outgroup differential in the application of expression restrictions. Differential enforcement patterns that exist in the wider system are not uniquely amplified by Liberal Democrat policy in a manner that would satisfy the optional-split threshold.

**Key Shift relative to current baseline:** Continuity with the post-2023 speech-restriction architecture tempered by explicit free-expression safeguards and resistance to further expansionary measures, producing a mid-range score without major upward or downward rupture.
