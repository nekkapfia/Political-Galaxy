# Core 1A: United Kingdom – Green Party of England and Wales
## Personal Autonomy Analysis
**Endos Score: 26**  
**Xenos Score: 21**

### Overview

The Green Party of England and Wales, scored strictly on the restrictions that would exist under its governing programme, produces one of the more restrictive Core 1A profiles among contemporary British parties. Its final architecture combines identity-based criminal speech controls, platform content duties, institutional orthodoxy rules, mandatory attitude-correction training, self-identification rules that generate secondary speech and association constraints, and professional sanctions for deviant views. Framing, justice language and ecological justifications are discarded under Direct Restriction Only and Negative-Only Weighting. The cumulative weight of the final set places the party inside the 19–27 Ideological reprogramming / thought control band, with a clear Endos/Xenos differential arising from heightened intensity against speech critical of protected identity categories.

### Core 1A Relevant Policies / Elements

* Retention and active enforcement of stirring-up hatred offences and related Public Order Act speech provisions targeting speech characterised as racist, Islamophobic, antisemitic, misogynistic or otherwise identity-hostile. The party places affirmative emphasis on enforcement in these areas and advances no narrowing agenda.
* Retention and amendment of the Online Safety Act imposing platform duties against content described as falsehoods, fakes or half-truths in political debate. The party does not seek repeal; it seeks to keep and potentially widen platform-level content controls.
* Institutional rules (Equality Act and related frameworks) that treat critical speech on gender, race and immigration as harmful and subject to restriction or sanction in public and quasi-public settings.
* Mandatory unconscious-bias / EDI / sensitivity training for staff, implemented as compulsory in Green-influenced councils (notably Brighton & Hove). The training is designed to correct attitudes and beliefs (bias, privilege, correct views on identity) rather than merely to regulate discrete external behaviours; non-compliance carries institutional consequences.
* Self-identification rules that generate additional speech and association constraints: once affirmed gender is treated as the operative category, speech that asserts biological sex (misgendering) and association decisions around single-sex spaces become subject to hostile-environment or potential hate-incident framing.
* Employment and professional sanctions for holding or expressing views deemed incompatible with institutional roles, including manifesto language concerning police officers whose views are considered incompatible with service.
* Retention of the core non-violent hate-speech and communications offences that criminalise certain forms of expression.

### Expanded Analysis

Under pure final-set scoring only the restrictions listed above are counted. Anything the party proposes to remove (Public Order Act protest provisions, Prevent) is simply absent from the list and exerts no residual influence. Each retained element is a direct prohibition or compulsion on voluntary expression, association or belief-related conduct.

The decisive feature is the simultaneous operation of multiple reinforcing layers. The criminal speech provisions and platform duties create a dense regulatory surface. Institutional orthodoxy rules and professional sanctions extend the same logic into employment and public roles. Mandatory attitude-correction training adds the explicit 19–27 element: it is compulsory, targets internal attitudes rather than merely external conduct, has been implemented in Green-run institutions, and is linked to institutional consequences. Self-identification rules add a further secondary constraint on speech and association once institutional practice treats affirmed gender as authoritative. When these layers function together they produce a coherent ideological-enforcement architecture rather than a collection of isolated measures.

A stable, discretely targetable intensity differential exists. Speech critical of specific protected identity categories (race, gender identity, Islam and related topics) attracts heightened criminal, institutional and professional risk relative to other forms of political speech. This satisfies the optional-split rule. Both Endos and Xenos use identical criteria and band language. The differential is modest in absolute terms but consistent and observable in the party’s priorities and in the practice of Green-influenced institutions.

The architecture does not reach the physical-coercion bands (10–18) or the floor. Ordinary direct-harm offences remain but do not define placement; the decisive weight comes from the ideological-enforcement and attitude-correction layers.

**Key Shift:** Construction of a multi-layered system of identity-based speech criminalisation, institutional orthodoxy enforcement, mandatory attitude-correction training and self-identification speech/association constraints as the characteristic governing programme of the party.

### Why this produces Endos 26 / Xenos 21

The cumulative weight of the seven retained restrictions — including compulsory attitude-correction training whose purpose is belief change — places the core population inside the 19–27 Ideological reprogramming / thought control band at 26. Heightened application of the same architecture against identity-critical speech and persons produces Xenos 21. Framing is ignored. Only the final restriction set is scored. The placement is consistent with the progressive-party scoring guide: mandatory attitude-correction training plus dense identity-speech architecture belongs in the low-to-mid 20s, not in the 40s or 50s.
