# Core 1A: United Kingdom – Labour Party
## Personal Autonomy Analysis
**Endos Score: 52**  
**Xenos Score: 48**

### Overview

The Labour Party, as the governing party, defends and seeks to extend the post-2023 online-safety and speech-regulation architecture. It maintains the Online Safety Act, advances election-period social-media rules, and has adopted an official definition of “anti-Muslim hostility” while continuing to operate the broader hate-speech and public-order framework. The party’s platform contains no proposal to narrow or repeal the core statutory speech restrictions inherited from 1986 onward. Instead it adds concrete measures that increase the cumulative weight of direct controls on voluntary online expression and association. The result is a lower-to-mid score relative to parties that seek roll-back, with a limited but justified Endos/Xenos differential.

### Core 1A Relevant Policies / Elements

* Active defence and continued implementation of the Online Safety Act 2023, including illegal-content duties, age-assurance requirements, and platform accountability for harmful material.
* Proposed or supported measures within the Representation of the People Bill framework to impose duties on platforms regarding the amplification and sharing of political information during election periods, analogous to broadcast impartiality rules.
* Adoption of an official definition of “anti-Muslim hostility” intended to guide institutional and potentially legal responses. Critics argue this risks constraining legitimate criticism of religious doctrine or Islamist ideology.
* Maintenance of Public Order Act 1986 (including Section 5 and stirring-up provisions), Racial and Religious Hatred Act, Communications Act 2003, and Malicious Communications Act offences.
* Support for further child-protection measures such as device-level scanning or social-media access restrictions for under-16s.
* Institutional promotion of “trusted news” prominence and related discoverability duties that would shape the visibility of political and news content online.
* No proposal to repeal or substantially narrow any major pre-existing speech offence.

### Expanded Analysis

Labour’s restrictive profile is defined by retention of the dense existing speech-restriction baseline plus concrete proposals to extend platform duties into election-period political speech and to operationalise an identity-specific hostility definition. Under Direct Restriction Only these are scored as additions to the set of prohibitions and regulatory obligations that constrain voluntary expression. Negative-Only Weighting records the cumulative weight; no upward credit is available for child-protection or “accuracy” framing. The election-period amplification rules would treat social-media platforms more like broadcast media, lowering the practical autonomy of online political speech during campaigns. The anti-Muslim hostility definition introduces a focused institutional lens that raises the risk of restriction on a discrete category of religious and political criticism.

A limited Endos/Xenos split is warranted. The anti-Muslim hostility definition and related institutional focus create a modestly heightened risk of restriction on speech critical of Islam or Islamism relative to other religious or ideological criticism. This constitutes a stable, discretely targetable divergence in intensity, satisfying the optional-split rule. The differential is narrower than formal dual legal systems but is consistent and observable in the party’s stated priorities.

**Key Shift relative to current baseline:** Active defence of the Online Safety Act architecture combined with new election-period platform duties and an identity-specific hostility definition, producing a modest downward movement from the status-quo score and a limited Endos/Xenos differential.

### Why this produces Endos 52 / Xenos 48

Negative-Only Weighting applied to the retained Online Safety Act architecture, existing Public Order and communications offences, plus the proposed election-period platform duties and anti-Muslim hostility definition produces an Endos score of 52. A limited Xenos differential (48) is recorded because the anti-Muslim hostility definition and related institutional focus create a modestly heightened risk of restriction on speech critical of Islam or Islamism relative to other forms of religious or ideological criticism. No positive credit is assigned for child-protection or accuracy framing. The scores sit below the Conservative and Liberal Democrat placements and well below the repeal-oriented parties, consistent with a governing stance of retention plus targeted expansion.
