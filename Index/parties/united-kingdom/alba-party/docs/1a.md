### Overview

The Alba Party, scored strictly on the restrictions that would exist under its governing programme, produces a mid-to-lower Core 1A profile defined by continued operation within the Hate Crime and Public Order (Scotland) Act 2021 and the UK Online Safety Act, tempered by clearer resistance than the SNP to expansive gender-identity institutional speech controls. Framing and rights language are discarded under Direct Restriction Only and Negative-Only Weighting. The final set remains denser than pure baseline retention because the Scottish Hate Crime Act and Online Safety Act stay fully in force, but lighter than the SNP/Green cluster because the party actively resists some of the gender-identity orthodoxy layers that intensify speech and association constraints. No Endos/Xenos split is applied.

### Core 1A Relevant Policies / Elements

* Continued operation within the Hate Crime and Public Order (Scotland) Act 2021 framework without a high-priority repeal commitment. The Act expands protected characteristics beyond the English framework (including transgender identity and age) and, in important formulations, incorporates a lowered threshold relative to intent-based English stirring-up offences; these features remain part of the legal environment Alba would govern under.
* Retention of the UK Online Safety Act architecture as it applies in Scotland, including illegal-content duties and platform accountability, with no comprehensive repeal agenda of the Reform UK type.
* Greater scepticism than the SNP toward expansive institutional and educational measures focused on gender identity. This includes resistance to self-identification rules that convert misgendering into hostile-environment or potential hate-incident matters and that generate secondary constraints on association and access to single-sex spaces.
* Defence of women’s sex-based rights and associated expression. Under pure final-set scoring this is relevant only insofar as it means certain restrictive institutional layers present under the SNP are absent under Alba; it is not scored as a positive.
* No proposal for wholesale narrowing of public-order or communications offences as they operate in Scotland.
* Limited engagement with the more expansive forms of non-crime hate-incident recording supported by the SNP; administrative push-back is modest and does not eliminate underlying primary offences.
* Retention of core criminal prohibitions on violence, true threats and direct physical harm.

### Expanded Analysis

Under pure final-set scoring only the restrictions that remain are counted. Alba does not repeal the Hate Crime and Public Order (Scotland) Act 2021 or the Online Safety Act; both therefore stay on the list and continue to exert downward weight. The Scottish Act’s expanded characteristics and lowered threshold relative to English stirring-up offences remain operative. Platform content duties under the Online Safety Act likewise remain fully present.

The decisive difference from the SNP is the party’s resistance to gender-identity institutional expansions. Self-identification rules that treat affirmed gender as the operative category, thereby converting speech that asserts biological sex (misgendering) and association decisions around single-sex spaces into hostile-environment or potential hate-incident questions, are not part of Alba’s programme. Their absence lightens the final set relative to the SNP without any removal-credit logic: those layers simply do not appear on the list.

Defence of sex-based rights is not scored as an upward force. It is relevant only as evidence that specific restrictive institutional layers are absent. Rhetorical commitments to free expression generate no upward credit. Framing is ignored.

No Endos/Xenos split is applied. Although the party’s emphasis on sex-based rights creates a distinctive focus, it does not generate a stable, discretely targetable dual structure of expression controls of the kind required by the optional-split rule. Differential intensity that exists in the wider Scottish system is not uniquely formalised by Alba policy into an out-group divergence.

The architecture sits above the SNP/Green/Sinn Féin/Plaid cluster because the gender-identity speech and association constraints are resisted, and below pure baseline-retention parties because the Scottish Hate Crime Act and Online Safety Act remain fully in force. The cumulative weight therefore sits in the low 40s.

**Key Shift:** Continuity with the dense Scottish hate-crime and UK Online Safety Act architecture combined with active resistance to gender-identity institutional speech and association constraints as the characteristic governing programme of the party.
