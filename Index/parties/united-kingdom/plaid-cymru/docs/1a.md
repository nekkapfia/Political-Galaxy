# Core 1A: United Kingdom – Plaid Cymru
## Personal Autonomy Analysis
**Endos Score: 47**  
**Xenos Score: 43**

### Overview

Plaid Cymru operates within the same UK-wide Online Safety Act and public-order framework as other parties while supporting the general direction of identity-based hate-speech protections and platform regulation. Its platform emphasises equality, anti-discrimination and the protection of minority groups, which in practice translates into retention and affirmative defence of the existing content-based speech architecture rather than any programme of narrowing. The party does not propose repeal of the Online Safety Act, Section 5 of the Public Order Act, or the stirring-up offences. The result is a restrictive profile close to that of the Scottish National Party and the Green Party, with a modest Endos/Xenos differential arising from heightened focus on identity-critical speech.

### Core 1A Relevant Policies / Elements

* Support for the continued operation of the Online Safety Act 2023 framework, including illegal-content duties and platform accountability for harmful material.
* Retention and affirmative defence of Public Order Act 1986 provisions (including Section 5) and the Racial and Religious Hatred Act stirring-up offences.
* Support for institutional and educational measures aimed at combating hate speech and discrimination, including speech critical of protected characteristics.
* Acceptance of communications offences under the Communications Act 2003 and related statutes.
* No proposal to repeal or substantially narrow the core hate-speech, public-order or online-safety architecture.
* Emphasis on equality and anti-discrimination frameworks that can generate secondary institutional pressure on expression in public and quasi-public settings.
* Retention of core criminal prohibitions on violence, true threats and direct physical harm.

### Expanded Analysis

Plaid Cymru’s restrictive profile is driven by continuity with, and defence of, the dense post-1986 and post-2023 speech-restriction baseline. The party does not seek to dismantle the Online Safety Act or the long-standing public-order and hate-speech statutes; instead it treats these instruments as necessary protections. Under Direct Restriction Only the scored weight is the set of prohibitions and regulatory duties the party would maintain. Negative-Only Weighting records that retained architecture; equality and anti-discrimination language generates no upward credit. The absence of any repeal agenda places the party well below Reform UK and Restore Britain and in the same band as the SNP and Greens.

A limited Endos/Xenos differential is recorded. The party’s emphasis on identity-based protections and institutional measures against hate speech creates heightened scrutiny of expression critical of certain protected characteristics. This produces a stable, discretely targetable divergence in the intensity of controls, satisfying the optional-split rule at a modest level. The differential is narrower than formal dual legal systems but is consistent with the party’s stated priorities.

**Key Shift relative to current baseline:** Affirmative defence of the existing Online Safety Act, public-order and hate-speech architecture with an equality-focused institutional emphasis, producing a lower-to-mid restrictive score and a limited Endos/Xenos differential.

### Why this produces Endos 47 / Xenos 43

Negative-Only Weighting applied to the retained Online Safety Act architecture, Public Order Act speech offences, stirring-up provisions and related institutional measures yields an Endos score of 47.
