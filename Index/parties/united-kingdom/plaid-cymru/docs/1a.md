# Core 1A: United Kingdom – Plaid Cymru
## Personal Autonomy Analysis
**Endos Score: 28**  
**Xenos Score: 24**

### Overview

Plaid Cymru’s governing programme produces a restrictive Core 1A profile built from full retention of the Online Safety Act, Public Order Act Section 5, Racial and Religious Hatred Act stirring-up offences, institutional measures against hate speech and discrimination, and communications offences. Equality and anti-discrimination frameworks add secondary institutional pressure on expression. Cumulative weight sits inside the ideological-enforcement range. Heightened focus on identity-critical speech justifies a limited Endos/Xenos split.

### Core 1A Relevant Policies / Elements

* Online Safety Act 2023 framework retained in full, including illegal-content duties and platform accountability. No repeal or substantial narrowing agenda.
* Public Order Act 1986 Section 5 (harassment, alarm or distress) and Racial and Religious Hatred Act stirring-up offences retained and affirmatively defended.
* Institutional and educational measures aimed at combating hate speech and discrimination, including speech critical of protected characteristics. These extend into public-sector and educational settings and treat certain critical expression as potentially harmful or as creating a hostile environment.
* Communications offences under the Communications Act 2003 and related statutes criminalising a range of non-violent expression, accepted and retained.
* Equality and anti-discrimination frameworks generating secondary institutional pressure on expression in public and quasi-public settings, including hostile-environment framing and potential disciplinary risk for speech that conflicts with institutional orthodoxy.
* No proposal to repeal or substantially narrow the core hate-speech, public-order or online-safety architecture.
* Core criminal prohibitions on violence, true threats and direct physical harm retained.

### Expanded Analysis

Plaid Cymru treats the existing architecture of platform duties, public-order offences and hate-speech provisions as necessary protections and retains them in full. The party’s equality and anti-discrimination emphasis reinforces institutional constraints on speech that challenges protected characteristics.

Institutional and educational measures against hate speech and discrimination function as orthodoxy requirements in public and quasi-public settings; they are not mere process. Equality frameworks generate secondary but real constraints on speech that conflicts with the prevailing institutional consensus on protected characteristics.

Speech critical of certain protected characteristics attracts heightened scrutiny relative to other political speech. This is a stable, discretely targetable intensity differential — narrower than formal dual legal systems but consistent with the party’s priorities and operational practice.

Mandatory attitude-correction training is not evidenced as a central party-defining feature of the Green type. This keeps the score marginally above the Green/SNP/Sinn Féin cluster while remaining inside the same broad ideological-enforcement band. The absence of any comprehensive repeal agenda means the dense post-1986 and post-2023 speech-restriction layers, together with equality-framework institutional pressure, remain fully present.

### Why this produces Endos 28 / Xenos 24

Online Safety Act retention, Public Order Act Section 5 and Racial/Religious Hatred Act provisions, institutional orthodoxy measures against hate speech and discrimination, communications offences, and equality-framework pressure on expression place Endos at 28.
