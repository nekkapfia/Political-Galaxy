# Core 1A: United Kingdom – Plaid Cymru
## Personal Autonomy Analysis
**Endos Score: 28**  
**Xenos Score: 24**

### Overview

Plaid Cymru, scored strictly on the restrictions that would exist under its governing programme, produces a restrictive Core 1A profile defined by full retention and affirmative defence of the Online Safety Act, Public Order Act Section 5, Racial and Religious Hatred Act stirring-up offences, institutional measures against hate speech and discrimination, and communications offences. Framing, equality language and anti-discrimination justifications are discarded under Direct Restriction Only and Negative-Only Weighting. The cumulative weight of the final set places the party inside the ideological-enforcement range, with a limited Endos/Xenos differential arising from heightened focus on identity-critical speech. The architecture is denser than pure baseline retention and lighter than the Green package because mandatory attitude-correction training is not evidenced as a central, party-defining feature.

### Core 1A Relevant Policies / Elements

* Support for the continued operation of the Online Safety Act 2023 framework, including illegal-content duties and platform accountability for harmful material. There is no repeal or substantial narrowing agenda.
* Retention and affirmative defence of Public Order Act 1986 provisions (including Section 5) and the Racial and Religious Hatred Act stirring-up offences.
* Support for institutional and educational measures aimed at combating hate speech and discrimination, including speech critical of protected characteristics. These measures extend into public-sector and educational settings and treat certain forms of critical expression as potentially harmful.
* Acceptance of communications offences under the Communications Act 2003 and related statutes that criminalise a range of non-violent expression.
* Equality and anti-discrimination frameworks that generate secondary institutional pressure on expression in public and quasi-public settings, including hostile-environment and potential disciplinary risk.
* No proposal to repeal or substantially narrow the core hate-speech, public-order or online-safety architecture.
* Retention of core criminal prohibitions on violence, true threats and direct physical harm.

### Expanded Analysis

Under pure final-set scoring only the restrictions listed above are counted. Plaid Cymru treats the existing architecture of platform duties, public-order offences and hate-speech provisions as necessary protections and retains them in full. The party’s equality and anti-discrimination emphasis reinforces institutional constraints on speech that challenges protected characteristics. Framing is ignored.

Each retained element is a direct prohibition or regulatory compulsion on voluntary expression. Institutional and educational measures against hate speech and discrimination function as orthodoxy requirements in public and quasi-public settings; they are not mere process. Equality frameworks generate secondary but real constraints on speech that conflicts with the prevailing institutional consensus on protected characteristics.

A limited Endos/Xenos differential is recorded. The party’s emphasis on identity-based protections and institutional measures against hate speech creates heightened scrutiny of expression critical of certain protected characteristics. This produces a stable, discretely targetable divergence in the intensity of controls, satisfying the optional-split rule at a modest level. The differential is narrower than formal dual legal systems but is consistent with the party’s stated priorities.

Mandatory attitude-correction training is not evidenced as a central, party-defining feature of the Green type. This keeps the score marginally above the Green/SNP/Sinn Féin cluster while remaining inside the same broad ideological-enforcement band. The absence of any comprehensive repeal agenda means the dense post-1986 and post-2023 speech-restriction layers, together with equality-framework institutional pressure, remain fully present and fully scored.

**Key Shift:** Affirmative defence of the existing Online Safety Act, public-order and hate-speech architecture with an equality-focused institutional emphasis as the characteristic governing programme of the party.
