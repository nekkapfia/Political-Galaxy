# Core 1A: United Kingdom – Mid-2022 onward
## Online Safety Act and Persistent Framework
**Base Score: 58**  
**Xenos Score: N/A** (no split)

### Overview

Implementation of the Online Safety Act 2023, together with the full retention of the pre-existing public-order, communications and hate-speech architecture, produced a partial recovery from the COVID low that remains well below pre-2016 levels. The new platform duties constitute a significant additional layer of restriction on the online expression environment. At the same time the long-standing statutory speech controls (Public Order Act 1986, Communications Act 2003, stirring-up provisions) continued without material liberalisation. The net result is a clear upward movement from the COVID score of 38 that is nevertheless constrained by the denser post-2023 regulatory baseline.

### Core 1A Relevant Restrictions / Elements

* Online Safety Act 2023: statutory duties on user-to-user and search services regarding illegal content, child-protection obligations, risk assessments, and related harms, including requirements for age assurance and content-moderation systems. These are direct regulatory restrictions that shape the availability, visibility and circulation of voluntary online expression.
* Full retention of the Public Order Act 1986 architecture, including Section 5 and the racial-hatred / stirring-up offences.
* Full retention of Communications Act 2003 Section 127 and Malicious Communications Act offences.
* Continued operation of terrorism-related expression controls.
* Ongoing enforcement practice and administrative recording around non-crime hate incidents and speech offences.
* No wholesale repeal of any major pre-COVID speech restriction.
* The COVID emergency movement and gathering restrictions themselves had been lifted, removing that temporary multi-domain weight.

### Expanded Analysis

The restrictive profile of the post-COVID period is defined by the removal of the temporary emergency superstructure and the simultaneous addition of a comprehensive online regulatory layer. Negative-Only Weighting records the lifting of lockdowns, stay-at-home orders and gathering bans as a reduction in negative weight, producing the upward movement from 38. At the same time the Online Safety Act introduces new direct duties on platforms that constrain the practical environment for online speech; these duties are scored as additional restrictive weight. The long-standing Public Order Act, Communications Act and hate-speech provisions remain fully in force and continue to contribute their pre-existing downward pressure. The combination yields a partial recovery that stops well short of a return to the 2016–2019 plateau or the earlier post-war baseline.

No system-wide Endos/Xenos split is applied. Differential intensity of enforcement and platform moderation against particular forms of political or identity-critical speech continues to be observable, yet it has not crystallised into a formal dual legal structure of the kind required by the optional-split rule.

**Key Shift:** Removal of the temporary COVID multi-domain restrictions combined with the addition of the Online Safety Act platform-duty regime, while the pre-COVID statutory speech baseline remains intact, producing only partial recovery.

### Why this produces a Base Score of 58

Recovery from the COVID low of 38 is real and substantial under Negative-Only Weighting. It is limited by the new Online Safety Act weight plus the continued operation of the long-standing Public Order Act, Communications Act and related speech controls. The net Base Score of 58 sits substantially above the COVID trough, yet still below the 2016–2019 level of 60 and well below the 1986–2000 level of 68 and the post-war common-law baseline of 78. The placement reflects the cumulative restrictive architecture documented across the entire post-1945 sequence.
