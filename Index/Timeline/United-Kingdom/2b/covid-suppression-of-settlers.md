### Covid Suppression of Settlers Era (2020 – mid/late 2022) — Score: 43 (41–50 band)

Following the end of the transition period, many formal EU legal constraints were lifted. However, the practical improvement in effective border control and removal capacity was substantially supported by temporary global travel restrictions resulting from the COVID-19 pandemic, which sharply reduced irregular migration volumes. While structural constraints persisted — particularly the Northern Ireland Protocol arrangements and ongoing ECHR influence on removals — the temporary suppression of migration flows reduced the intensity of several key drags during this period. The score reflects a moderate improvement over the preceding era, while recognising the temporary and volume-dependent nature of much of the improvement.

**Temporary Volume Suppression (External Factors)**

**Scoring Band**: 41–50 band (temporary)

* “Several core functions (trade, migration, regulation) operate under substantial external agreements.”

**Mechanical Description**:  
Short-term reduction in irregular migration or border pressure caused by external events (e.g. pandemic-related travel restrictions) rather than changes in national policy or legal authority.

**Analysis**:  
This represents a temporary easing of border control pressures rather than a structural change in sovereignty. The mechanical effect can produce an uplift in scores during the period of suppression, but it does not permanently alter the baseline constraints. It is therefore treated as temporary and does not provide a lasting improvement to the sovereignty score.
