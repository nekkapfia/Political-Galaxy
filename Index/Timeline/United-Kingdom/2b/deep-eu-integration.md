### Deep EU Integration Era (1993–2020) — Score: 34 (31–40 band)

Following the Maastricht Treaty and subsequent treaties, the scope of supranational legal supremacy expanded considerably. EU law acquired direct effect and supremacy across a broader range of policy areas, including aspects of migration, justice and home affairs, and regulation. Free movement rules created large-scale, legally protected cross-border movement with limited national discretion to restrict it on general grounds. The European Court of Justice exercised growing enforceable authority over UK institutions in covered domains. These cumulative constraints significantly reduced the UK’s unilateral control over regulation, certain areas of migration policy, and decision-making across multiple core functions.

- **Brexit Transition Sub-Era (2016–2020) — Score: 31 (31–40 band)**  
  Although the United Kingdom formally left the European Union on 31 January 2020, it remained fully subject to EU law and institutions throughout the transition period while losing all voting rights and representation. This created a net deterioration in sovereignty compared to full membership: the constraints of external legal authority remained in place, but the mechanisms for influencing those rules were removed. It is treated as a sub-era of Deep EU Integration rather than a standalone period due to the continued dominance of EU legal constraints during this time.
